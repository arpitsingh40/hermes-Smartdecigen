# V166 · THE LAWS OF HUMAN NATURE — Robert Greene
Tier 2 · Human Nature & Decision Psychology · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Every person you deal with—cofounder, investor, customer, competitor, employee, yourself—is driven by deep, often invisible forces: envy, grandiosity, insecurity, role-playing, generational myopia, death denial. These forces operate below conscious reasoning and determine outcomes more than spreadsheets, term sheets, or strategy documents ever will. Reading these forces accurately is the founder's real competitive advantage, because business decisions are always human decisions.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Greene synthesizes evolutionary psychology, neuroscience, and historical biography into 18 laws that govern human behavior. The master insight: people are not rational actors making calculated decisions—they are emotional creatures rationalizing decisions they've already made. The founder who can read the law operating in the room (envy in a cofounder who didn't get the title, grandiosity in an investor performing for his partners, defensiveness in an employee who won't admit a mistake) has a radar that sees three moves ahead. The founder who doesn't is flying blind through a minefield of human irrationality, attributing every explosion to "bad luck" or "market conditions."

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The law of irrationality
├─ Humans think they're rational but are ruled by emotions; every decision is an emotional one with a rational wrapper stitched on afterward
├─ The founder's defense: widen the time horizon (what will I think of this decision in 3 months?), distance the emotions (what would I tell someone else in this situation?), and watch for arousal (high emotion = low judgment)
└─ LEAF: when the founder describes a decision with high emotional language (excited, terrified, furious, desperate), flag it—emotion is the signal that the irrationality law is active

### Branch 2 — The law of narcissism
├─ Every person has a self-image they defend; narcissists take this to pathological levels—they need constant validation, cannot receive criticism, and interpret everything through "how does this reflect on me?"
├─ The spectrum: healthy self-love (can take feedback, genuinely cares about others) ←→ deep narcissism (feedback is attack, others are audience, empathy is performance)
└─ LEAF: when the founder describes someone who "can't take feedback," "takes everything personally," or "makes everything about them," name the narcissism law—this person's primary motivation is image maintenance, not business outcomes

### Branch 3 — The law of role-playing
├─ Everyone wears a mask; people present the version of themselves they want you to see, not who they are
├─ The mask slips under three conditions: stress (crisis reveals character), success (power reveals what was always there), and low stakes (how they treat waiters, interns, junior staff)
└─ LEAF: when evaluating a key person (cofounder candidate, senior hire, investor), observe them in at least one low-stakes, unobserved-by-power context before committing

### Branch 4 — The law of compulsive behavior
├─ People have patterns they cannot break despite negative consequences—these are the diagnostic signature of character
├─ A person's character is their fate: someone who has burned every previous bridge will burn yours; someone who has delivered through difficulty will deliver again
├─ Past behavior is the single best predictor of future behavior—not intentions, promises, or charm
└─ LEAF: for every key decision about a person, check their pattern across the last 5 years, not their pitch in the last 5 days; patterns don't lie, presentations do

### Branch 5 — The law of envy
├─ Envy is the most universal and most denied human emotion—everyone feels it, no one admits it, and it operates silently to sabotage relationships and decisions
├─ Envy signals: effusive praise that feels slightly off, subtle digs disguised as jokes, the "they're so lucky" framing, celebrating your failures slightly too much
├─ Founders attract envy: their visibility, autonomy, and potential wealth trigger it in employees, peers, even friends and family
└─ LEAF: when the founder describes someone whose behavior toward them has shifted inexplicably after a success milestone, check the envy law before any other diagnosis

### Branch 6 — The law of defensiveness
├─ People defend their self-image with extraordinary energy; admitting a mistake threatens the self, so the brain constructs elaborate defenses
├─ In organizations, defensiveness creates information distortion: bad news gets softened, failures get reframed, and the founder becomes the last to know
├─ Greene's antidote: create environments where admitting error is rewarded, not punished—the person who says "I was wrong" is building trust, not losing status
└─ LEAF: when no one on the founder's team ever admits a mistake, the culture has a defensiveness epidemic—start by modeling public error admission from the top

### Branch 7 — The law of grandiosity
├─ Success inflates the ego; the grandiosity law states that people who achieve power or wealth begin to believe they are exceptional in ALL domains, not just the one they succeeded in
├─ Founder grandiosity: "I built a $10M business, therefore I know how to invest, how to parent, how to write a book, how to..."
├─ The antidote: maintain a reality check—a person, metric, or practice that tells you the truth without filtering
└─ LEAF: when the founder uses absolutist language about their own judgment ("I'm never wrong about people," "my instincts are always right"), flag the grandiosity law—this is the prelude to a catastrophic miss

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN the founder describes someone in their orbit (cofounder, investor, key hire) → APPLY law identification: which of Greene's laws is most visibly operating in this person right now? State the law and its predictable trajectory.
- WHEN the founder is about to make a key people decision → APPLY the character pattern check: what is the 5-year pattern, not the 5-day pitch? Character is fate; promises are noise.
- WHEN the founder's language is highly emotional about a decision → APPLY irrationality law: widen the time horizon, add distance, flag that high emotion = low judgment right now.
- WHEN the founder's team culture shows no error admission → APPLY defensiveness diagnosis: the culture has a defensiveness loop—model public error admission, reward truth-telling.
- WHEN the founder has achieved a success milestone → APPLY grandiosity check: is the founder extending domain-specific success to universal judgment? Who or what is their reality check?
- WHEN a relationship with a peer/team member shifts inexplicably after success → APPLY envy law: name the envy pattern before it becomes sabotage.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Emotion is high. That means judgment is low. Add 48 hours and distance before deciding."
- "Character is fate. Their 5-year pattern tells you more than their 5-minute promise."
- "Everyone wears a mask. Watch how they treat the person who can do nothing for them."
- "Envy is the emotion no one admits. If praise feels slightly off, that's what you're hearing."
- "Admitting you're wrong is the fastest way to prove you're worth trusting."
- "Success in one domain is not wisdom in all domains. Who's your reality check?"
