# V91 · GRIT: THE POWER OF PASSION AND PERSEVERANCE — Angela Duckworth
Tier 2 · Performance Psychology · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Talent counts — but grit — sustained passion and perseverance for a long-term goal through setbacks, plateaus, and boredom — predicts success more reliably than IQ, talent, or luck in every domain where the finish line is years away.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Duckworth's research across West Point cadets, National Spelling Bee finalists, and novice teachers showed: the highest achievers share not superior talent but extraordinary GRIT — the combination of CONSISTENCY OF INTEREST (staying with one top-level goal for years) and PERSEVERANCE OF EFFORT (working through difficulty without giving up). Grit grows through four psychological assets: INTEREST (passion that starts as fascination and deepens through knowledge), PRACTICE (deliberate, focused, feedback-driven improvement), PURPOSE (the conviction that your work matters beyond yourself), and HOPE (the expectation that your own efforts can improve your future). Founders are, by definition, in a grit-testing environment: the company is a multi-year goal with consistent setbacks; the question is how to sustain grit through the valley of despair without becoming rigid and ignoring quit signals. The engine's job: distinguish between grit as productive tenacity and grit as stubborn sunk-cost entrapment.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Talent × Effort = Skill; Skill × Effort = Achievement
├─ Duckworth's formula: effort counts TWICE — once to turn talent into skill, once to turn skill into achievement
├─ The "natural" who doesn't work hard is out-performed by the gritty improver
├─ LEAF: when a founder says "they're so talented" about a team member, follow up with: "but are they gritting through the hard work, or relying on talent?"
└─ LEAF: the inverse for the founder: your own talent ceiling is irrelevant if you out-effort everyone; effort is the multiplier you control

### Branch 2 — The grit scale: consistency of interest
├─ Duckworth's Grit Scale measures: do you stay with a goal for years despite new interests, or do you pivot every 6 months?
├─ For founders: the tension is between grit (stick with the vision) and agility (pivot when data says change course)
├─ LEAF: distinguish between a QUARTERLY pivot (changing tactics) and a YEARLY vision change (changing the top-level goal); the former is agility, the latter is lack of grit
└─ LEAF: a founder who changes their top-level goal every 6 months has a grit problem — the company cannot compound if the vision keeps shifting

### Branch 3 — The four assets of grit: Interest, Practice, Purpose, Hope
├─ Interest: passion starts with curiosity; deepen it through deliberate exposure to the domain
├─ Practice: deliberate practice (specific goal, full attention, immediate feedback, repetition, refinement) — not just "doing" the work
├─ Purpose: "my work matters"; reframing the daily grind as serving others keeps effort alive when motivation dies
├─ Hope: "I can improve my situation through my own efforts" — the growth-mindset belief that effort will pay off
└─ LEAF: when a founder is losing steam, check which of the four assets is depleted: interesting work? Practice structure? Sense of purpose? Belief in improvement?

### Branch 4 — The hard thing rule
├─ The Duckworth family rule: everyone must pick a HARD THING (deliberate practice required, not just fun), CANNOT QUIT mid-season, but CAN quit at a natural stopping point (year-end, season-end)
├─ This builds grit through: doing hard things by choice, finishing what you start, learning when to stop (a natural break, not in the middle of difficulty)
└─ LEAF: the hard thing rule applied to the founder: "did you pick this hard thing by choice? Are you committed to a natural stopping point (not quitting in the dip)? And have you built a practice structure?"

### Branch 5 — Grit vs quitting: the wisdom to know the difference
├─ The Dip (Godin): quitters in the dip never reach mastery; but quitting a dead-end is wisdom
├─ Duckworth: gritty people don't quit on hard days — but they do quit when the goal truly doesn't matter or when the cost exceeds the mission's value
└─ LEAF: the diagnostic: is this a DIP (temporary plateau that mastery requires you to push through) or a DEAD-END (structural impossibility)? Grit through the dip; quit the dead-end

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder is in a persistent struggle → APPLY grit assets audit: "which of the four (interest, practice, purpose, hope) is depleted? Restore that one."
- WHEN a founder pivots frequently → APPLY consistency of interest check: "is this a tactical adjustment or a top-level goal change? If the latter, you might lack grit."
- WHEN a founder feels like quitting → APPLY dip vs dead-end diagnosis: "is this a temporary plateau (push through) or a structural dead-end (quit wisely)?"
- WHEN a founder praises natural talent → APPLY effort reframe: "remember: effort counts twice. Talent without hard work is wasted."
- WHEN the team is losing motivation → APPLY purpose rebuild: "reconnect each person's daily work to the customer's life change — purpose is the grit fuel."

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Effort counts twice: once to build skill, once to turn skill into achievement."
- "Grit is passion + perseverance for a TOP-LEVEL goal — not a tactic that shifts every quarter."
- "Have you chosen a hard thing, committed to finish the season, and built a practice routine?"
- "Is this a dip (push through) or a dead-end (quit wisely)?"
- "Hope is not passive optimism — it's the belief that your effort can change the outcome."
