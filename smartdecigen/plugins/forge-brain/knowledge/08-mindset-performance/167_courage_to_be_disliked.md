# V167 · THE COURAGE TO BE DISLIKED — Ichiro Kishimi & Fumitake Koga
Tier 2 · Existential Psychology · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The founder's deepest constraint is not market, money, or talent—it is the need for approval. Adlerian psychology, as presented through a Socratic dialogue, argues that ALL problems are interpersonal relationship problems, that the past does not determine the present (teleology over etiology), and that freedom is earned through the courage to be disliked by people whose tasks are not yours to carry. For a founder, this means: every decision avoided because "what will they think," every conflict dodged because "I don't want to upset anyone," every pricing concession made to be liked, every hire kept too long because firing feels mean—these are not kindness; they are the abdication of the founder's task in exchange for the comfort of approval.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Alfred Adler, a contemporary of Freud and Jung who broke away to found Individual Psychology, argued three revolutionary ideas that Kishimi and Koga bring to life: (1) TRAUMA DOES NOT CAUSE BEHAVIOR—present goals cause behavior (teleology); the past provides explanations we choose, not compulsions we suffer. (2) ALL PROBLEMS ARE INTERPERSONAL RELATIONSHIP PROBLEMS—isolate a human being and they have no problems; every problem is a friction with someone else's expectations, judgment, or demands. (3) SEPARATION OF TASKS—whose problem is this? Whose life will bear the consequence of this decision? If the answer is "not mine," it is not my task, and I must not let others' judgment of my task control my action. The synthesis: the founder who separates tasks, refuses to live for others' approval, and accepts that being disliked is the price of freedom—that founder can make the hard calls. The founder who cannot is a prisoner of every stakeholder's expectations, and the business is merely the prison yard.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Teleology: the denial of trauma
├─ Adler's most controversial claim: the past does not determine the present—we USE the past to justify present goals
├─ A founder who says "I can't raise money because I failed once" is using the past failure to serve the present goal of avoiding the discomfort of fundraising
├─ The shift: from "I am this way because X happened to me" to "I am choosing this behavior because it serves a present purpose—what purpose?"
└─ LEAF: when the founder says "I can't because [past event]," reframe: "what present discomfort are you avoiding by holding onto that past event as a reason?"

### Branch 2 — Separation of tasks
├─ The central Adlerian practice: for every decision, ask "whose task is this?"—meaning, who will bear the ultimate consequence of this choice?
├─ The founder's decision to raise prices is the founder's task; the customer's reaction (anger, departure, complaint) is the customer's task
├─ Collapsing tasks: "I can't raise prices because customers will be upset"—the founder has taken on the customer's emotional task and made it the reason to fail at their own
└─ LEAF: when the founder is paralyzed by someone else's potential reaction, ask: "whose life bears the consequence? Yours or theirs? If yours, their opinion on YOUR task is their task to manage, not your reason to fail."

### Branch 3 — All problems are interpersonal
├─ Strip away other people and you have no problems—loneliness requires another person to miss, inferiority requires another person to compare with, failure requires another person's judgment
├─ The founder's "business problems" are almost always interpersonal: cofounder tension, team performance, investor pressure, customer demands, partner conflicts
├─ Adler's reframe: stop asking "how do I fix this business problem" and start asking "what relationship friction is actually causing this?"
└─ LEAF: when the founder describes a business problem, ask "who is the other person in this problem?"—every business problem resolves to an interpersonal one

### Branch 4 — The courage to be normal
├─ Adler's radical proposition: superiority and inferiority complexes are two sides of the same coin—both are competition frameworks where self-worth depends on ranking against others
├─ The alternative: community feeling—the sense that you belong, contribute, and are valued not because you're better but because you're HERE
├─ The founder trapped in the superiority game must constantly win; the founder grounded in community feeling can take risks, fail, and try again because their worth isn't on the line
└─ LEAF: when the founder's self-worth language is competitive ("I have to prove," "I need to show them," "I'll be nothing if this fails"), flag the hierarchy trap—recommend community-feeling reframes: contribution, not comparison

### Branch 5 — Living in the here and now
├─ Adler: life is a series of moments; the past is gone, the future is not guaranteed, the only real thing is NOW
├─ The founder who lives in the future (exit, Series B, IPO) is never present to the decisions they need to make today
├─ The founder who lives in the past (the last failure, the missed opportunity) is shackled to a ghost
└─ LEAF: when the founder defers life, relationships, or peace to a future milestone ("after we raise," "after we exit"), name it: the future doesn't exist—what decision needs to be made in the here and now?

### Branch 6 — The courage to be disliked
├─ The synthesis of all Adlerian practice: freedom is being disliked by some people—it is impossible to live on your own terms AND have everyone approve
├─ The price of approval: every time the founder compromises their judgment to avoid disapproval, they pay for temporary comfort with permanent self-betrayal
├─ The practice: make the decision YOUR task demands, communicate it clearly and kindly, and let the other person's reaction be their task
└─ LEAF: when the founder is avoiding a necessary action because "people will be upset," ask: "who, specifically? And is their temporary upset worth more than your permanent self-betrayal?"

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN the founder says "I can't because [past event]" → APPLY teleology reframe: "what present purpose is this past story serving? What discomfort are you avoiding by keeping it alive?"
- WHEN the founder is paralyzed by someone else's expected reaction → APPLY task separation: "whose life bears the consequence of this decision? If yours, their emotional reaction to your decision is their task, not yours."
- WHEN the founder is people-pleasing, seeking validation, or avoiding conflict → APPLY the courage-to-be-disliked question: "who specifically will be upset, and is their temporary upset worth the permanent cost of not acting?"
- WHEN the founder's identity is wrapped up in winning/comparing/proving → APPLY community-feeling reframe: "what would this decision look like if your goal were contribution, not comparison?"
- WHEN the founder describes a "business problem" → APPLY interpersonal reframe: "who is the other person in this problem? What relationship friction is the real issue?"
- WHEN the founder is deferring life to a future milestone → APPLY here-and-now check: "what decision needs to be made today, not after the raise, not after the exit—today?"

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Whose life bears the consequence of this decision? That's whose task it is."
- "You're not stuck because of what happened. You're using what happened to stay stuck—what are you avoiding?"
- "Freedom is being disliked. You cannot live on your own terms AND have everyone's approval."
- "Every business problem has a person inside it. Who?"
- "The future doesn't exist. What needs to be decided right now, in this moment?"
- "Your worth is not your rank. Contribute, don't compare."
- "Their emotional reaction to your decision is their task. Your task is to make the right decision and communicate it clearly."
