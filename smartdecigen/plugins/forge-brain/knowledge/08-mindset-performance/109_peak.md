# V109 · PEAK — Anders Ericsson
Tier 3 · Mindset · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Ericsson's research (the basis for the "10,000-hour rule" that Gladwell popularized — and oversimplified) shows that expert performance is not about talent or raw hours. It's about deliberate practice: focused, structured, feedback-driven effort at the edge of your current ability. The key distinction: experience ≠ expertise. 20 years of driving produces a slightly-better-than-average driver. 20 years of deliberate practice produces a Formula 1 champion.

## TRUNK
Deliberate practice: (1) Specific goal — target a well-defined, specific performance aspect. (2) Intense focus — full concentration; mindless repetition is worthless. (3) Immediate feedback — know instantly whether you succeeded or failed. (4) At the edge of ability — not comfortable, not impossible. The sweet spot where you fail 20-40% of the time. (5) Mental representations — the expert's ability to see patterns, anticipate, and self-correct. Mental representations are both the result of practice and the engine that drives further improvement. Key finding: the primary constraint on improvement is not time spent but the quality of mental representations and the availability of good feedback. Most people plateau because they stop practicing deliberately and start performing on autopilot.

## FRUIT
- WHEN skill development stalls → APPLY deliberate practice: are you practicing at the edge with immediate feedback, or performing on autopilot?
- WHEN designing training → APPLY mental representations: don't just explain the skill, show the expert's mental model.
- WHEN evaluating progress → APPLY the feedback test: do you know within seconds whether your last action was correct?

## SEEDS
- "Experience is not expertise. 20 years of driving doesn't make you a Formula 1 driver."
- "Deliberate practice: specific goal + intense focus + immediate feedback + at the edge."
- "Mental representations are the engine of expertise. Build them."
- "The plateau: you stopped practicing and started performing on autopilot."

## GRAFTS
- → Ultralearning: the project-based framework built on deliberate practice.
- → Grit: the perseverance that sustains deliberate practice over years.
- → Flow: the state of optimal experience that deliberate practice can produce.
