# V108 · ULTRALEARNING — Scott H. Young
Tier 3 · Mindset · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Aggressive, self-directed learning can compress years of education into months. Young's ultralearning framework emerged from his experiments (learning MIT's 4-year CS curriculum in 12 months, learning 4 languages in 1 year). The 9 principles: metalearning (learning how to learn the subject first), focus, directness (learn by doing the thing), drill (attack your weakest points), retrieval (test yourself, don't re-read), feedback, retention, intuition, and experimentation.

## TRUNK
Key principles: (1) Metalearning — before learning anything, spend 10% of total project time mapping what to learn, how to learn it, and what resources to use. (2) Directness — learn in the context you'll use it. If you want to code, build projects. If you want to speak, talk to natives. Transfer is fragile. (3) Drill — identify your weakest sub-skill and isolate it for intensive practice. (4) Retrieval — testing yourself is far more effective than re-reading. The harder the retrieval, the stronger the memory. (5) Feedback — seek immediate, specific, and sometimes harsh feedback. Avoid the comfort of positive-only feedback.

## FRUIT
- WHEN learning any new skill → APPLY metalearning: spend 10% of time mapping the learning path first.
- WHEN stuck on a sub-skill → APPLY the drill principle: isolate the weak point and practice it until it's no longer the bottleneck.
- WHEN studying → APPLY retrieval: close the book and try to recall, don't re-read.

## SEEDS
- "Spend 10% time planning what to learn before starting."
- "Learn by doing. Transfer from classroom to real world is fragile."
- "Testing yourself is 3x more effective than re-reading."
- "Isolate and drill your weakest sub-skill."
