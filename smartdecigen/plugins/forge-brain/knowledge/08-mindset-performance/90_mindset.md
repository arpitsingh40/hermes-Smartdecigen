# V90 · MINDSET: THE NEW PSYCHOLOGY OF SUCCESS — Carol Dweck
Tier 1 · Founder Psychology · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
There are two fundamental mindsets that shape everything — fixed (talent is static, effort is for those who lack it, failure is a verdict) and growth (ability develops through effort, challenges are growth opportunities, failure is information) — and the mindset you choose determines whether you stagnate or compound.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Dweck's decades of research show: people with a fixed mindset believe intelligence/talent is innate — so they avoid challenges (risk of exposure), give up easily when things get hard, ignore useful negative feedback, and feel threatened by others' success. People with a growth mindset believe abilities can be developed — so they embrace challenges, persist through setbacks, learn from criticism, and find lessons in others' success. The mindsets are not permanent personality traits; they can be shifted by how we praise, how we frame failure, and what we value. For founders, the implications are existential: fixed-mindset founders avoid the hard feedback that would save them, hire people who flatter their ego rather than challenge their thinking, take failure as identity-ending instead of data. Growth-mindset founders seek disconfirming information, hire people smarter than themselves, treat pivot not as surrender but as learning. The engine's job: recognize fixed-mindset patterns in a founder's language and decisions, and re-frame them in growth terms — not as therapy, but as strategy.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The two mindsets in action
├─ Fixed: "I'm not a numbers person" → avoids financial analysis → misses cash flow problems → dies
├─ Growth: "I can learn anything I need" → hires a finance tutor → learns the P&L → survives
├─ LEAF: when a founder says "I'm just not good at X" (sales, operations, strategy), flag the fixed-mindset move — the growth reframe is "what's the fastest way to learn X?"
└─ LEAF: the most expensive fixed-mindset sentence: "I already know what works"

### Branch 2 — The danger of praise
├─ Praising EFFORT and STRATEGY ("you worked hard on that pitch and it showed") → growth mindset, resilience
├─ Praising TALENT ("you're a natural") → fixed mindset, risk-aversion, fear of being "found out"
├─ LEAF: audit how the founder praises their team — if the praise is "you're so smart/talented," shift it to "your strategy and persistence made this happen"
└─ LEAF: founders with fixed mindset often hire people who are LESS capable (to maintain their "smartest person" status) — the growth move is to hire people better than you at their function

### Branch 3 — Failure as identity vs failure as data
├─ Fixed: "I failed" = "I AM a failure" — identity collapse, defensive retreat, blame external
├─ Growth: "I failed" = "that approach didn't work" — data point, diagnostic value, iteration source
├─ LEAF: when a founder talks about a failure, listen for identity language ("I'm not cut out for this") vs data language ("that channel didn't convert"); reframe identity language as data
└─ LEAF: the growth perspective on pivot: the company changed direction because the founder learned something, not because the founder made a mistake

### Branch 4 — CEOs and the fixed-mindset trap
├─ Dweck studied CEOs: fixed-mindset leaders ran hierarchical, judgment-heavy cultures where people feared being wrong; growth-mindset leaders ran collaborative, development-heavy cultures where people grew
├─ Fixed CEOs surround themselves with yes-people; growth CEOs surround themselves with truth-tellers
└─ LEAF: diagnostic question for founder culture: "in your last team meeting, was the last person to speak disagreeing with you or agreeing with you?"

### Branch 5 — Shifting mindset (neuroplasticity)
├─ The brain grows new connections with effort; "talent" is mostly developed, not discovered
├─ The process shift: from "I need to prove I'm good at this" to "I need to get better at this"
├─ LEAF: translate every founder self-assessment from static to dynamic: instead of "are we good at this?" ask "how can we get better at this?"

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder says "I'm not good at X" → APPLY growth reframe: "what's the fastest learning path to become good enough at X to survive this phase?"
- WHEN a founder praises a team member for innate talent → APPLY praise shift: redirect to effort, strategy, persistence — the things the person controlled.
- WHEN a founder describes failure as identity → APPLY data reframe: "that's not who you are — that's a data point about what didn't work."
- WHEN a founder avoids a necessary but uncomfortable decision → APPLY challenge test: "is this avoidance protecting your self-image or serving the company?"
- WHEN the founder's team agrees with everything → APPLY yes-person audit: "who on your team has disagreed with you in the last month? If nobody, your culture might be fixed."

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Not good at X is not an identity. It's a learning gap."
- "Praise the effort, not the talent — talent praise creates fragility."
- "Failure is not identity. It's information."
- "Are you hiring people who challenge you or people who flatter you?"
- "Your team's last disagreement with you — can you name it? If not, fix that."
