# V65 · THE 4 DISCIPLINES OF EXECUTION — McChesney
Tier 8 · Mindset & Performance · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Organizations fail to execute strategy not because people are lazy but because the urgent whirlwind of daily operations drowns the strategic priorities — and the ONLY way to execute in the face of the whirlwind is a repeatable discipline that protects the few "wildly important goals" with focused energy, a compelling scoreboard, and a cadence of accountability that no fire drill can override.

## TRUNK — core thesis
The 4 Disciplines bridge the gap between strategy formulation and execution: Discipline 1 — FOCUS on the Wildly Important Goal (WIG): one or two goals, not 20, because focus is a scarce resource. Discipline 2 — ACT on the Lead Measures: the inputs that drive the lag measure (outcome) — you can control lead measures directly, lag measures only indirectly. Discipline 3 — KEEP a COMPELLING SCOREBOARD: people play differently when they're keeping score and the scoreboard is simple enough that they know at any moment whether they're winning or losing. Discipline 4 — CREATE a CADENCE OF ACCOUNTABILITY: a weekly meeting (20-30 minutes) where each team member reports their commitment from last week, reviews the scoreboard, and makes a new commitment for the week. The meta-lesson: execution is not about knowing what to do — it's about doing what you know, consistently, through a system that makes it impossible to ignore the WIG.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Discipline 1: Focus on the wildly important goal
├─ The WIG must be specific, measurable, and time-bound: "From X to Y by When" — no vague directions
├─ One or two WIGs maximum; any more and focus is diluted across all of them
├─ The WIG is the ONE thing that, if achieved, makes everything else possible or irrelevant
└─ LEAF: when a founder lists 5+ quarterly goals, force the reduction to exactly ONE WIG — the rest are operations, not execution priorities

### Branch 2 — Discipline 2: Act on lead measures
├─ Lag measures (revenue, profit, market share) tell you if you won — too late to change the game
├─ Lead measures (calls made, prototypes tested, demos delivered) are the inputs you can control daily
├─ Good lead measures are predictive (moving them drives the lag) and influenceable (the team owns them)
└─ LEAF: for every WIG, define exactly ONE lead measure that the team controls and that predicts the lag; track it relentlessly

### Branch 3 — Discipline 3: Keep a compelling scoreboard
├─ A scoreboard is compelling when the players know immediately whether they're winning or losing
├─ The scoreboard must be simple (one glance), visible (no buried spreadsheet), and focused on the lead and lag measures
├─ People play differently when they're keeping score — especially when the score is theirs to influence
└─ LEAF: the founder's scoreboard should fit on one page; if it takes a presentation to explain, it's not compelling

### Branch 4 — Discipline 4: Create a cadence of accountability
├─ The weekly WIG meeting: same day, same time, 20-30 minutes, standing
├─ Each person reports: (1) what I committed last week and whether I kept it, (2) the scoreboard update, (3) my ONE commitment for this week
├─ The meeting is about the WIG, not operations — operational updates happen elsewhere
└─ LEAF: every leadership team needs a recurring WIG meeting within the first 30 days; no excuses — if there's no cadence, there's no execution

### Branch 5 — The whirlwind vs. the WIG
├─ The whirlwind (emails, meetings, customer fires, payroll) will always demand attention; it cannot be eliminated
├─ The WIG is the strategic priority that the whirlwind will push out unless explicitly protected
├─ The four disciplines are not about doing more — they're about doing the most important thing despite the noise
└─ LEAF: at the end of each week, ask the founder: "what percentage of your energy went to the WIG vs. the whirlwind?" — the gap IS the execution problem

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder's team is busy but not progressing → APPLY WIG identification: reduce to exactly one wildly important goal with a clear "from X to Y by when."
- WHEN a founder tracks only outcomes → APPLY lead measure definition: what one input drives the outcome and the team can control today?
- WHEN team meetings are operational status updates → APPLY the WIG meeting format: commitments → scoreboard → next commitment, 30 minutes max.
- WHEN the founder feels the whirlwind winning → APPLY the weekly WIG audit: energy % on WIG vs. whirlwind; the ratio is the metric that matters.
