# V58 · THE PERSONAL MBA — Josh Kaufman
Tier 2 · Business Fundamentals · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
A business exists to CREATE and DELIVER value in exchange for money — and every business, everywhere, is governed by five interdependent parts: VALUE CREATION, MARKETING, SALES, VALUE DELIVERY, and FINANCE; master these five and you understand business as well as any MBA.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Kaufman wrote the anti-MBA: everything you need to know about business distilled into mental models, without the $200K degree. The five-part business system: Value Creation, Marketing, Sales, Value Delivery, Finance. Core mental models include: the Iron Law of the Market, the 12 Forms of Value (product, service, shared resource, subscription, resale, lease, agency, audience, loan, option, insurance, capital), Mental Accounting, and the 80/20 Principle. The key insight: FORMAL EDUCATION is not required — clear thinking about the business system is.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The five-part business system
├─ Value Creation: identifying a market need and creating something to fill it — the CORE of the business
├─ Marketing: getting people to know about and desire what you offer
├─ Sales: converting that desire into an actual transaction
├─ Value Delivery: fulfilling the promise — producing and delivering the product/service
├─ Finance: managing cash flow, profit, and sustainability — the scoreboard
└─ LEAF: when evaluating any business, check ALL five parts — a weakness in any one breaks the system; most startups fail because they're strong at 3/5 and ignore the missing 2

### Branch 2 — The 12 forms of value
├─ Product, Service, Shared Resource, Subscription, Resale, Lease, Agency, Audience, Loan, Option, Insurance, Capital — every business monetizes through one or a combination of these
├─ The combination determines the business's economics: margins, scalability, cash flow pattern
└─ LEAF: map the business's value form(s); the mix defines how and how much you can charge

### Branch 3 — The Iron Law of the Market
├─ "Build something people want" is the entire law in four words
├─ The best product in the world fails if nobody needs it enough to pay; a mediocre product succeeds if the market demand is strong enough
├─ Market matters more than product: it's easier to find a market that wants something and build the product, than to build a great product and find a market
└─ LEAF: before evaluating ANY business detail, ask the Iron Law question: "does a REAL market exist that WANTS this enough to pay?" — if the answer is unclear, test that BEFORE building anything else

### Branch 4 — Core mental models for founders
├─ 80/20 Principle: 20% of customers produce 80% of revenue; 20% of features produce 80% of usage; focus on the vital few, not the trivial many
├─ Opportunity Cost: doing A means not doing B; every "yes" is a "no" to something else
├─ Mental Accounting: people treat money differently depending on its source (gift vs salary vs bonus) — use this in pricing and messaging
├─ Confirmation Bias: people seek evidence that confirms what they already believe — design tests to DISPROVE your hypothesis, not confirm it
├─ The Sunk Cost Fallacy: past costs are irrelevant to forward-looking decisions — only future costs and benefits matter
└─ LEAF: when a founder is overwhelmed by data/options, apply the 80/20: what's the 20% of customers, features, or activities that produce 80% of the results? Focus there, ignore the rest

### Branch 5 — Subconscious triggers in sales and marketing
├─ The Contrast Principle: present a more expensive option first (the anchor), then the target option looks reasonable
├─ The Reciprocity Trigger: give something of value first (content, sample, help) — people feel compelled to give back
├─ Scarcity: limit availability (time, quantity, access) — people want what's rare
├─ The Commitment Trigger: get a small yes first, then ask for the bigger yes — consistency principle
└─ LEAF: when a founder designs a sales page or pricing page, check: did they use contrast (anchor price), scarcity (limited time/qty), and reciprocity (value before ask)? If not, the conversion rate will be suboptimal

### Branch 6 — Interpreting financial statements
├─ Profit is NOT cash flow: you can be profitable and go bankrupt (receivables vs payables timing)
├─ The Income Statement captures a period; the Balance Sheet captures a moment; the Cash Flow Statement connects them
├─ Key metrics: Gross Margin (revenue - COGS / revenue), Net Margin (profit / revenue), Cash Conversion Cycle (days to turn inventory into cash), Runway (cash / monthly burn)
└─ LEAF: for a founder who doesn't read financials, teach the three statements and the four metrics — the engine's job is to translate business health into these numbers, not confuse with complexity

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder has a business idea → APPLY Iron Law test: is the market need real and strong enough? Test demand before building product.
- WHEN a business struggles → APPLY five-part diagnosis: which of the five parts (creation, marketing, sales, delivery, finance) is weakest? Fix that first.
- WHEN a founder is overwhelmed by choices → APPLY 80/20: what 20% of efforts produce 80% of results? Concentrate there.
- WHEN pricing a product → APPLY value forms analysis: what form is the value delivered (product, subscription, service, etc.)? Price accordingly.
- WHEN a founder doesn't understand their numbers → APPLY three-statement literacy: income statement, balance sheet, cash flow — explain health through these.
