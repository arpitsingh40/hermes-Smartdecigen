# V113 · VENTURE DEALS — Brad Feld & Jason Mendelson
Tier 3 · Finance · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Raising venture capital is a one-time transaction for the founder but a repeat game for VCs. The asymmetry of experience means founders consistently leave money, control, and ownership on the table. Understanding the mechanics — term sheets, liquidation preferences, anti-dilution, board rights — is the only defense. You don't need to become a lawyer; you need to know what to fight for and what to let go.

## TRUNK — core thesis
The term sheet is the single most important document a founder will sign. Every clause is a lever that affects economics (who gets paid how much when) and control (who decides what). Feld & Mendelson walk through every term: price, liquidation preference (1x non-participating is standard; anything beyond shifts returns to investors), vesting (4 years with 1-year cliff — for founders too), anti-dilution (weighted average is standard; full ratchet is aggressive), board composition, protective provisions, drag-along rights, and information rights. The best defense is a competitive process — multiple term sheets create the only leverage founders have.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The term sheet: economic terms
├─ Valuation: don't optimize the number; optimize the percentage sold (20-25% per institutional round is healthy)
├─ Liquidation preference: 1x non-participating = standard; participating = investor gets money back THEN shares in the remainder (double-dip)
├─ Option pool: negotiate it BEFORE the valuation — a 20% unallocated pool in the pre-money effectively lowers the founder's price
├─ Anti-dilution: weighted average broad-based = fair; full ratchet = punitive (if you ever do a down round, full ratchet wipes out founders)
└─ LEAF: the liquidation preference and anti-dilution terms matter more than valuation in a downside scenario; negotiate them as if a downside scenario is 50% likely

### Branch 2 — The term sheet: control terms
├─ Board composition: standard = 2 founders, 2 investors, 1 independent; never give investors a board majority at Series A
├─ Protective provisions: veto rights on sale, new financing, option pool changes — standard is fine; anything beyond is investor overreach
├─ Drag-along rights: forcing minority to go along with a sale — standard, but threshold (majority or supermajority) matters
├─ Founder vesting: 4 years, 1-year cliff, double-trigger acceleration on change of control — standard; investors may ask single-trigger
└─ LEAF: once control is given, it cannot be taken back; control terms are permanent, economic terms can be renegotiated

### Branch 3 — The raise process
├─ Target: raise enough to hit 18-24 months of milestones that unlock the next round at a higher valuation
├─ Raise too little: you spend half your runway fundraising again — lethal distraction
├─ Raise too much: you set a valuation you can't grow into — down round triggers anti-dilution
├─ Syndicate: 1-2 lead investors + 2-3 followers; too many VCs = no one cares enough; too few = concentration risk
└─ LEAF: the best time to raise is when you don't need to; the worst is when you have 3 months of runway left

### Branch 4 — Dilution math
├─ Founders selling 25% per round will own ~42% after A, ~32% after B, ~24% after C
├─ Liquidation preference stack: if you raised $30M with participating preferred and exit at $50M, founders might get $0
├─ Carve-outs and management incentive plans matter at exit — negotiate them early
└─ LEAF: model dilution through 3 rounds before you take any money; the spreadsheet is the cheapest negotiation tool

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder considers fundraising → APPLY dilution modeling: project ownership through 2-3 rounds and show the founder what they'll own at exit.
- WHEN a founder has a term sheet → APPLY term-by-term analysis: liquidation preference, anti-dilution, board composition, protective provisions — flag non-standard terms.
- WHEN a founder is choosing between investors → APPLY partner assessment: difference between a partner who adds value and one who adds meetings.
- WHEN the raise amount is unclear → APPLY milestone math: what must be true in 18 months for the next round, how much cash reaches that point.
- WHEN a down round looms → APPLY anti-dilution stress test: who gets crushed under full ratchet vs weighted average.
