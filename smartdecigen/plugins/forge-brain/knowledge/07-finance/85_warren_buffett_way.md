# V85 · THE WARREN BUFFETT WAY — Robert G. Hagstrom
Tier 2 · Value Investing · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
A business owner who buys as if they were buying the whole company — focusing on durable competitive advantage, predictable earnings, honest management, and a price that leaves a margin of safety — will, over decades, compound capital at rates that speculators cannot approach.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Hagstrom distills Buffett's method into twelve principles organized around four filters: BUSINESS (simple and understandable, consistent operating history, favorable long-term prospects), MANAGEMENT (candid, rational, independent of the institutional imperative), FINANCIAL (high return on equity, strong owner earnings, high profit margins, retained earnings that create at least a dollar of market value for every dollar retained), and MARKET (margin of safety through intrinsic value — buy at a discount). The core of Buffett's approach is treating a stock as partial ownership of a real business — the opposite of trading pieces of paper. For founders, the framework is even more directly applicable: you ARE the owner of the whole business; the questions are whether it has a moat (durable competitive advantage), whether your management decisions are rational and candid, whether your financial metrics indicate genuine earnings power, and whether you are allocating retained capital wisely.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Durable competitive advantage (the moat)
├─ Buffett buys businesses with a "wide, sustainable moat" — brand power (Coca-Cola), low-cost producer (GEICO), switching costs (See's Candies), network effects (American Express)
├─ The moat must be understandable and durable; if he can't understand the business in 5 minutes, he passes
├─ LEAF: what is the founder's moat? Is it a REAL 7 Power or just operational excellence? If the moat statement takes longer than 10 words to explain, it's not a moat
└─ LEAF: Buffett avoids businesses that require constant innovation (tech, pharma) — for founders in those sectors, the moat must be in the business MODEL, not the product

### Branch 2 — Owner earnings vs accounting earnings
├─ Owner earnings = net income + depreciation/amortization - maintenance capex; this is the cash the owner can actually take out
├─ Accounting earnings can be manipulated by depreciation schedules, one-time charges, stock compensation
└─ LEAF: compute the founder's OWNER earnings — does the business generate real cash after the capex required to stay competitive? If not, the "profit" is an illusion

### Branch 3 — The institutional imperative
├─ Buffett's warning: managers unconsciously mimic peers, resist change, expand headcount and budget regardless of need, and pursue pet projects without economic justification
├─ The cure: rational, candid, independent management that asks "would this decision improve intrinsic value per share?" not "what are others doing?"
└─ LEAF: apply the institutional imperative test to every founder decision: "are we doing this because it's right, or because it's what successful companies in our space do?"

### Branch 4 — Retained earnings: the $1 test
├─ Every dollar of retained earnings should create at least $1 of market value; if retained earnings grow assets but not intrinsic value, the money should be returned to shareholders
├─ For founders: every rupee reinvested in the business (new hire, new product, new channel) should produce >1 rupee of incremental value over time
└─ LEAF: audit the past year's retained earnings: for every rupee you kept in the business instead of taking as profit, what value did you create? If the answer is unclear, you're spending retained earnings, not investing them

### Branch 5 — Circle of competence and temperament
├─ Buffett only invests in businesses within his circle of competence; he passes on brilliant opportunities he doesn't understand
├─ Temperament: the ability to be greedy when others are fearful, to sit on cash when there's nothing compelling, to hold through volatility without panic
└─ LEAF: map the founder's circle of competence honestly — which parts of the business are you genuinely expert in? For areas outside it (legal, finance, tech), get expert input or partner

### Branch 6 — Margin of safety (Graham → Buffett)
├─ Buffett modified Graham's deep-discount approach: pay a FAIR price for a WONDERFUL business, not a wonderful price for a FAIR business
├─ The margin of safety changes: it's not the gap between price and liquidation value, but the gap between price and the present value of predictable future earnings
└─ LEAF: the founder's margin of safety is a cost structure that survives at 60% of projected revenue, combined with a business model that compounds regardless of short-term economic noise

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN the founder describes their "advantage" → APPLY moat test: is this a durable competitive advantage (a 7 Power) or just a feature? Name the specific barrier.
- WHEN the founder reinvests all profits into growth → APPLY $1 test: will this reinvestment create >$1 of value per rupee spent? Show the math.
- WHEN facing a competitor's move → APPLY institutional imperative check: "are we reacting because it's strategically correct, or because we feel the pressure to match?"
- WHEN the founder's business model needs constant innovation to survive → APPLY moat-in-model not moat-in-product: is there a switching cost, network effect, or scale advantage in the model itself?
- WHEN the founder chases a new opportunity outside their expertise → APPLY circle of competence: "do you genuinely understand this business better than the average operator in it?"

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "A moat you can explain in 10 words is a moat. A longer story is a product description."
- "Does your retained rupee create more than a rupee of value? If not, give it back."
- "Are you doing this because it's right, or because it's what everyone else does?"
- "Fair price for a wonderful business beats wonderful price for a fair business."
- "What's your circle of competence? Stay inside it; partner for the rest."
