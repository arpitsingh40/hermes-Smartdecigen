# V83 · RICH DAD POOR DAD — Robert Kiyosaki
Tier 3 · Financial Mindset · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The rich don't work for money — they make money work for them, buying assets that generate passive income while the middle class works for a paycheck and buys liabilities they call assets; the single shift from "how do I earn more?" to "how do I OWN more income-producing assets?" changes everything.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Kiyosaki tells the story of two fathers: Poor Dad (his educated, hardworking, financially struggling biological father) and Rich Dad (his friend's entrepreneur father who built wealth through assets and businesses). The book's six lessons: the rich don't work for money (they acquire assets), financial literacy is learning to distinguish assets from liabilities, mind your own business (build your asset column, not your job), the history of taxes and the power of corporations (corporations earn→spend→tax; individuals earn→tax→spend), the rich invent money (financial creativity), and work to learn (skills, not money). The polarizing critique: the book is light on specific mechanics and heavy on mindset — but the mindset shift is genuinely powerful. For founders, the core insight is: build a business that is an ASSET (can run without you) not a JOB (collapses without you). The asset/liability distinction is the single most useful frame for founder financial decisions.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Assets vs liabilities (Kiyosaki's definition)
├─ Asset: puts money IN your pocket (rental property, dividend stock, a business with a manager, royalties, IP)
├─ Liability: takes money OUT of your pocket (mortgage on your home, car payment, vacation property that costs more than it yields)
├─ LEAF: classify every founder expense as asset or liability on Kiyosaki's definition — not the accountant's — and watch most "investments" flip to liabilities
└─ LEAF: a founder's business is an asset ONLY if it generates positive cash flow without the founder's daily labor; if it needs you every day, it's an expensive job

### Branch 2 — The rat race
├─ Most people: work → earn → spend (on liabilities they think are assets) → need more → work more → pay more tax
├─ The exit: acquire enough income-producing assets that their passive income covers expenses
└─ LEAF: ask the founder "what is your monthly passive income vs monthly burn?" — the gap is how far they are from exiting the rat race; a business that burns cash without asset value is the rat race in entrepreneur form

### Branch 3 — Mind your own business
├─ Your real business is your asset column, not your job description; founders confuse "building a business" with "working in a business"
├─ Build systems, not customers: the goal is a machine that produces value without you
└─ LEAF: audit the founder's calendar — how much time is spent WORKING IN the business vs ON building the asset (systems, team, IP, channels that run without the founder)

### Branch 4 — Work to learn, not to earn
├─ Kiyosaki advises taking jobs that teach you skills (sales, persuasion, leadership, cash flow management) rather than those that pay the most
├─ Each career phase should build a skill bucket, not a bank balance; the bank balance follows the skill stack
└─ LEAF: when a founder is stuck in a low-growth role, ask "what skill is this building? If the answer is 'none', it's time to move"

### Branch 5 — The power of corporations
├─ Corporations: earn → spend on business expenses (pre-tax) → pay tax on what's left
├─ Employees: earn → pay tax → spend what's left
├─ LEAF: founders in India should understand the corporate vs personal tax structure they operate in — the structure itself is a financial lever
└─ LEAF: founders often confuse personal and business finances; a clean separation is the first step toward treating the business as an asset

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder describes "investments" that are actually lifestyle expenses → APPLY Kiyosaki's asset test: "does this put money in your pocket or take it out? If the latter, it's a liability."
- WHEN a founder is trapped in daily operations → APPLY "mind your own business": what percentage of your time is spent building something that can run without you?
- WHEN a founder is choosing between a high-paying job and a low-paying learning role → APPLY work-to-learn: "which role builds a skill that appreciates, vs one that just pays rent?"
- WHEN a founder's personal and business finances are entangled → APPLY corporate separation: structure the entity so the business is an independent financial machine.
- WHEN a founder works harder but cash flow doesn't improve → APPLY rat race diagnosis: are you buying assets or buying liabilities you call assets?
