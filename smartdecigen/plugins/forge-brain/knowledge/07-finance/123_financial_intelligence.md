# V123 · FINANCIAL INTELLIGENCE — Karen Berman & Joe Knight
Tier 3 · Finance · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Most founders and managers lack financial literacy — they can't read an income statement, can't distinguish profit from cash, and make decisions based on bank balances rather than financial reality. Financial Intelligence provides the framework for understanding the numbers that drive business performance: income statement (profitability), balance sheet (financial position), cash flow statement (liquidity), and the ratios that connect them.

## TRUNK
The three core financial statements: (1) Income Statement: Revenue - COGS = Gross Margin - Operating Expenses = Operating Profit. (2) Balance Sheet: Assets = Liabilities + Equity — a snapshot of what you own and owe. (3) Cash Flow Statement: cash in - cash out = change in cash — PROFIT DOES NOT EQUAL CASH. You can be profitable and bankrupt (if you sell on credit and can't collect). Key ratios: Gross Margin %, Net Margin %, Current Ratio (assets/liabilities), Debt-to-Equity, ROA, ROE. The book demystifies accrual accounting, depreciation, capitalizing vs expensing, and how assumptions shape the numbers.

## BRANCHES
- Profit vs Cash: the single most important distinction. Profit is an accounting opinion; cash is a fact. Growth consumes cash (inventory, receivables, hiring ahead of revenue).
- The Income Statement: revenue recognition rules matter. Annual contracts booked upfront aren't revenue — they're deferred revenue. Revenue ≠ cash received.
- The Balance Sheet: working capital = current assets - current liabilities. Negative working capital means you're funding growth with supplier credit — risky but efficient.
- Ratios: Return on Assets (ROA) = net income / total assets. Return on Equity (ROE) = net income / shareholder equity. Gross margin = gross profit / revenue — the most important ratio for any product business, because gross margin funds everything else.
- Budgeting: budgets should be built on assumptions (pipeline velocity, retention rate, hiring plan), not last year + 10%. Zero-based budgeting forces re-justification.

## FRUIT
- WHEN a founder says "we're profitable!" → APPLY profit vs cash check: are you profitable on accrual basis but negative on cash flow? That's dangerous growth.
- WHEN reviewing financials → APPLY ratio analysis: gross margin (is the business model sound?), current ratio (can you pay bills?), burn rate (how many months of runway?).
- WHEN building a budget → APPLY zero-based discipline: what would you spend if starting from zero today? Kill inertia costs.
- WHEN a decision involves spending → APPLY T/OE/I framework: does this increase throughput, reduce operating expense, or reduce inventory? If none, don't spend.

## SEEDS
