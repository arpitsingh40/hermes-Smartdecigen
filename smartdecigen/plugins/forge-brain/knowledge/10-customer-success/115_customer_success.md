# V115 · CUSTOMER SUCCESS — Nick Mehta, Dan Steinman, Lincoln Murphy
Tier 3 · Customer Success · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
In subscription/SaaS businesses, the sale is not the finish line — it's the starting gun. Customer Success is the function that ensures customers achieve their desired outcome through your product, creating retention and expansion revenue. Companies that treat post-sale as an afterthought leak value: high churn, low NRR, and acquisition treadmill. CS is not support (reactive problem solving); CS is proactive — driving adoption, value realization, and outcomes.

## TRUNK — core thesis
The 10 Laws of Customer Success: (1) Sell to the right customer, (2) CS and sales must be aligned, (3) You need a CS strategy and team, (4) Customer onboarding is everything, (5) Track customer health rigorously, (6) Proactive engagement beats reactive, (7) Build customer communities, (8) Expand through advocacy, (9) Operationalize CS (not heroic effort), (10) It's a company-wide commitment, not a department. Net Revenue Retention (NRR) is the single most important SaaS metric — above 100% means existing customers grow faster than they churn. Below 100%, you're on a treadmill.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Onboarding: the first mile
├─ Time-to-first-value (TTFV): the clock from sign-up to the moment the customer experiences the core value prop
├─ Most churn happens in the first 90 days, and most first-90-day churn happens because onboarding failed
├─ Structured onboarding: pre-defined milestones, assigned CSM, proactive communication (not "call us if you need help")
├─ Implementation: data migration, integrations, user setup — these are make-or-break; a bad implementation kills trust permanently
├─ "Aha moment": define the specific action/state that predicts long-term retention — time users to reach it fast
└─ LEAF: every customer who hasn't hit their "aha moment" by day 30 is at high risk; have you identified yours?

### Branch 2 — Health Scoring
├─ Health score = weighted composite of usage (frequency, breadth, depth), relationship (exec engagement, survey responses), and outcomes (ROI, goal achievement)
├─ Red accounts (health < 50): immediate exec escalation, dedicated save plan
├─ Yellow accounts (health 50-70): proactive outreach, additional training, executive sponsor assignment
├─ Green accounts (health > 70): expansion conversations, advocacy asks, reference requests
├─ Leading indicators: login frequency dropping, support tickets rising, key user leaving — these precede churn by 30-60 days
└─ LEAF: if you can't look at a dashboard and instantly name every red account, your health scoring isn't working

### Branch 3 — Retention and Expansion
├─ Gross Revenue Retention (GRR): % of revenue retained from existing customers, excluding expansion — should be 80%+ for SaaS
├─ Net Revenue Retention (NRR): GRR + expansion revenue — should be 100%+; best-in-class is 120%+
├─ Expansion levers: seat/user growth, feature upgrades, cross-sell to other products, price increases as value grows
├─ Renewal is a process, not an event: start demonstrating value 6 months before renewal, not 30 days
└─ LEAF: if your NRR is below 100%, fix churn before investing in acquisition — you're filling a leaking bucket

### Branch 4 — At-risk recovery
├─ Early warning signals: support ticket spike, usage drop >20% month-over-month, key contact departure, NPS detractor
├─ Save playbook: diagnose root cause (product, support, expectations, internal champion loss), assign exec sponsor, create recovery plan with milestones, check weekly
├─ Executive engagement: a 15-minute call from the CEO saves accounts that CSMs can't
├─ When to stop trying: if the customer has already made their decision, negotiate a clean exit and learn from it
└─ LEAF: the most expensive thing in customer success is saving accounts that were never a fit — qualify better at sale

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN churn is rising → APPLY health score diagnosis: which accounts are red, what's the common pattern, is it onboarding or ongoing?
- WHEN a founder wants to grow faster → APPLY NRR check: is NRR above 100%? If not, fix retention before scaling acquisition.
- WHEN onboarding feels chaotic → APPLY time-to-first-value analysis: what's the aha moment, how long does it take to get there, what's the bottleneck?
- WHEN a key account is at risk → APPLY save playbook: root cause, exec sponsor, recovery milestones, weekly check.
- WHEN considering a new feature → APPLY the expansion question: would existing customers pay more if we built this?
