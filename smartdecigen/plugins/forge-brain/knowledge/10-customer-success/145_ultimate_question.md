# V145 · THE ULTIMATE QUESTION 2.0 — Fred Reichheld
Tier 3 · Customer Success · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The single best predictor of customer behavior — will they stay, spend more, or refer others? — is the answer to one question: "How likely are you to recommend us to a friend or colleague?" (0-10). The Net Promoter Score (NPS) = % Promoters (9-10) minus % Detractors (0-6). NPS > 50 is world-class. But Reichheld's deeper point: NPS is useless if you don't act on it. The metric is a prompt for action, not a score to celebrate. The real value is in the follow-up: "why did you give that score?" — and then fixing what the detractors told you.

## TRUNK
The NPS system: (1) Ask the question regularly at key touch points (post-purchase, post-support, post-onboarding). (2) Segment into Promoters (9-10 — loyal enthusiasts who fuel growth), Passives (7-8 — satisfied but vulnerable), Detractors (0-6 — unhappy customers who damage your brand). (3) Follow-up: call every Detractor within 24 hours. Understand the problem. Fix it. (4) Close the loop: tell the customer what you changed because of their feedback. (5) Track trends, not snapshots — NPS rising or falling matters more than the absolute number. The "Golden Rule" of NPS: earn the right to grow by treating customers how you'd want to be treated.

## FRUIT
- WHEN measuring customer satisfaction → APPLY the one-question test: are you collecting NPS? If not, start. If yes, are you following up on Detractors within 24 hours?
- WHEN retention is dropping → APPLY NPS trend analysis: is NPS declining? What are the top 3 reasons Detractors cite?
- WHEN spending on growth → APPLY the earn-the-right rule: is NPS high enough that word-of-mouth will amplify your spend?
