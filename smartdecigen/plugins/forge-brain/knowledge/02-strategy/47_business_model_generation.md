# V47 · BUSINESS MODEL GENERATION — Alexander Osterwalder & Yves Pigneur
Tier 3 · Innovation · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
A business model is not a plan, a pitch, or a product idea — it is the NINE BUILDING BLOCKS of how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value, and the first job of a founder is to design a model that is coherent, testable, and aligned around a compelling value proposition before building anything at scale.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas replaced the 50-page business plan with a single-page framework: 9 boxes covering Customer Segments, Value Propositions, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams, Key Resources, Key Activities, Key Partnerships, and Cost Structure. The key insight: a business model is a HYPOTHESIS to be tested, not a plan to be followed. The Canvas forces the entrepreneur to see the WHOLE system — each block depends on every other. Changing one block changes the model. The book provides patterns: Unbundling (separate product innovation, customer relationship management, and infrastructure), Long Tail (sell less of more), Multi-Sided Platforms (connect two or more distinct groups), and Free as a Business Model (subsidize one side to monetize the other). It also introduces the Value Proposition Canvas as the deep-dive tool for the VP-Customer Segment pair.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The nine building blocks
├─ Customer Segments: whom are you creating value for? (mass market, niche, segmented, diversified, multi-sided)
├─ Value Propositions: what problems do you solve, what needs do you satisfy? (newness, performance, customization, design, brand/status, price, cost reduction, risk reduction, accessibility, convenience)
├─ Channels: how do you reach customers? (awareness → evaluation → purchase → delivery → after-sales)
├─ Customer Relationships: what relationship does each segment expect? (personal assistance, self-service, automated, communities, co-creation)
├─ Revenue Streams: how is value monetized? (asset sale, usage fee, subscription, leasing, licensing, brokerage, advertising)
├─ Key Resources: what assets are essential? (physical, intellectual, human, financial)
├─ Key Activities: what must you do well? (production, problem-solving, platform/network)
├─ Key Partnerships: who do you need outside the firm? (optimization, reduction of risk, acquisition of resources)
├─ Cost Structure: what are the major cost drivers? (cost-driven vs value-driven, fixed vs variable, economies of scale/scope)
└─ LEAF: the canvas is coherent only when every box connects causally to the value proposition; if a cost makes no sense given the revenue model, or a channel doesn't reach the chosen segment, the model has a design flaw

### Branch 2 — The Value Proposition Canvas (deep-dive)
├─ Customer Profile: jobs (functional, social, emotional), pains (negative outcomes, risks, obstacles), gains (benefits, aspirations)
├─ Value Map: products/services, pain relievers, gain creators
├─ FIT: when the value map addresses the pains and gains that matter most to the customer profile — that's product-market fit at the model level
└─ LEAF: before the full canvas, start with the VP-Customer pair — the value proposition canvas; if there's no fit here, no other block matters

### Branch 3 — Business model patterns
├─ Unbundling: separate three businesses (infrastructure management, customer relationship, product innovation) that often conflict under one roof
├─ Long Tail: sell less of more (niche products × digital distribution × low inventory costs)
├─ Multi-Sided Platforms: connect two+ distinct groups, subsidize one side, monetize the other (marketplaces, ad-supported, payment networks)
├─ Free: give core value free (or subsidized), monetize through premium or the other side
└─ LEAF: most startups are in the "multi-sided platform" space without realizing it — map BOTH sides, know which side is subsidized and which pays, or the economics fail

### Branch 4 — Business model environment
├─ A model doesn't exist in vacuum: it's shaped by market forces (segment needs, demand, competition), industry forces (suppliers, stakeholders, incumbents), key trends (tech, regulatory, social), and macroeconomic forces (capital, commodities, infrastructure)
├─ Design successful models by understanding the environment first; ignore the environment and the best-designed model fails
└─ LEAF: when a canvas looks perfect but the business struggles, the flaw is usually outside the canvas — in the environment assumptions that were never stated

### Branch 5 — Prototyping and iteration
├─ A business model is a hypothesis; multiple prototypes (different canvases) should be developed and compared before committing
├─ Scenarios: build "what if" models for different futures (rapid growth, recession, competitor entry)
├─ Test assumptions: each block has implicit assumptions; identify the RISKIEST one (the single assumption whose falsification kills the model) and test it cheapest-first
└─ LEAF: when a founder has committed to ONE model without alternatives, force the creation of 2-3 alternative canvases — the exercise reveals hidden assumptions and blind spots

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder presents a business idea → APPLY canvas construction: map all 9 blocks; test internal coherence (do the revenue streams fund the activities? do channels reach the segments?).
- WHEN the value proposition is unclear → APPLY Value Proposition Canvas: define the customer jobs/pains/gains and the product's pain relievers/gain creators — measure fit.
- WHEN the founder is building a marketplace or platform → APPLY multi-sided pattern: identify both sides, subsidy vs revenue, chicken-and-egg strategy.
- WHEN the business struggles despite good execution → APPLY environmental scan: check market forces, industry forces, trends, macro forces — the flaw is likely outside the canvas.
- WHEN a founder is committed to one model → APPLY prototyping: create 2-3 alternative canvases; identify the riskiest assumption in the chosen model; test it cheapest-first.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners

- "The canvas is only as good as the coherence between its nine boxes."
- "What customer job are you really solving? Not the product — the job."
- "Multi-sided? Which side pays and which side is subsidized?"
- "The failure is usually outside the canvas, not inside it."
