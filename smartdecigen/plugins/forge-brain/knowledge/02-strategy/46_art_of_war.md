# V46 · THE ART OF WAR — Sun Tzu
Tier 2 · Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The supreme art of war is to subdue the enemy WITHOUT fighting — every battle is a failure of strategy; the greatest victory is taking a position so strong, so informed, and so aligned with reality that the opponent yields or is made irrelevant without a costly confrontation.

## TRUNK — core thesis
The oldest and most concise strategy manual in existence, distilled into 13 chapters of short aphorisms. Core: strategy is KNOWLEDGE applied to conflict. Know yourself, know your enemy, and know the ground — and you cannot lose. The five constant factors (moral influence, weather, terrain, command, doctrine) govern every engagement. The essence: AVOID strength, attack weakness. Make the opponent come to you through exhaustion. Deceive constantly — when near seem far, when able seem unable. Build momentum (shih) so that the force is overwhelming at the point of contact. Speed over protracted war — a founder's resources are always limited. And the most dangerous enemy is not the opponent but the leader's own emotions — anger, pride, overconfidence.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Know yourself, know your enemy
├─ "If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles."
├─ Self-knowledge: be brutally honest about your own resources, constraints, capabilities, and weaknesses before planning any move
├─ Enemy knowledge: study competitors' behavior, incentives, leadership temperament, and past patterns — not just their product
├─ Ground knowledge: understand the market terrain, regulatory environment, channel dynamics — these are the battlefield
└─ LEAF: before any major move (pivot, launch, pricing change), the founder should write down what they know about their own position, their competitors' current situation, and the market dynamics — if any column is thin, that's where intelligence is needed first

### Branch 2 — Strategy without confrontation: the highest form
├─ "Supreme excellence consists in breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting."
├─ For founders: the ideal competitive outcome is not destroying a rival in a costly price war — it's making the market your space by being so clearly differentiated, so entrenched, that competitors rationally choose a different segment
├─ Deception is not lying — it's making your moves opaque; let competitors think you're going one direction while building another
└─ LEAF: when a founder prepares for a direct competitive battle (price war, feature parity race), Sun Tzu asks: can you achieve your objective WITHOUT that confrontation by changing the battlefield?

### Branch 3 — Attack strategy when they are weakest
├─ "Appear at points which the enemy must hasten to defend; march swiftly to places where you are not expected."
├─ The best competitive move is one the opponent cannot respond to in time — because they're committed elsewhere, because it targets a segment they ignore, because it exploits a structural weakness they can't fix quickly
├─ Hit where the defense is absent, not where it's strong — that's playing to win, not to fight
└─ LEAF: when analyzing competition, map where the rival has OVER-invested (and thus must defend) and where they have UNDER-invested (and thus are vulnerable) — attack the latter

### Branch 4 — Momentum (shih) and speed
├─ Shih: the strategic advantage that makes victory seem effortless — positioning your forces so their natural movement produces the desired outcome
├─ "The momentum of one skilled in war is overwhelming, and his attack precisely timed."
├─ Speed: protracted campaigns drain resources and morale; the startup that executes faster than the incumbent moves through the OODA loop more times and wins
└─ LEAF: evaluate every strategic move by whether it builds momentum (makes the next move easier) or consumes it (makes the next move harder) — the shih-generating move is almost always better than the apparently profitable but momentum-consuming one

### Branch 5 — The five fatal flaws of leadership
├─ Recklessness: charging without intelligence → death
├─ Cowardice: failing to act when the moment is right → capture
├─ Hasty temper: responding emotionally to provocation → destruction
├─ Delicacy of honor: making decisions based on ego/appearance → shame
├─ Over-solicitude for people: failing to make hard decisions that harm individuals but save the whole → anxiety
└─ LEAF: when a founder's decision seems irrational, check which of these five flaws is driving it — and name it; Sun Tzu's list is the original founder-temperament diagnostic

### Branch 6 — Terrain and the nine situations
├─ Ground types: accessible (both can move), entangling (hard to leave), deadly (fight or die), etc. — each demands different tactics
├─ The nine situations: from "dispersive" (home ground, troops want to go home) to "deadly" (fight without escape) — leadership style must adapt per situation
├─ Founder translation: the competitive situation determines the approach — a pre-revenue startup is in "deadly ground" (fight desperately or die); a post-PMF company is on "accessible ground" (choose engagements carefully)
└─ LEAF: diagnose the founder's "terrain" before prescribing tactics — a founder in deadly ground needs very different advice than one on accessible ground

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder faces a competitive threat → APPLY self/enemy/ground audit: what do you know about each? Fill the intelligence gap before deciding.
- WHEN a founder plans a direct competitive attack → APPLY non-confrontation reframe: is there a way to win WITHOUT fighting on their terms?
- WHEN a founder is spread too thin → APPLY momentum audit: which of your activities build shih and which consume it? Feed the builders, starve the consumers.
- WHEN a founder makes an emotionally driven decision → APPLY five-flaws diagnosis: anger? pride? fear? Name the flaw, strip the emotion, re-decide.
- WHEN founders of different stages need different advice → APPLY terrain classification: what ground are you on? Deadly ground needs desperate focus; accessible ground allows patient strategy.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Know yourself, know your enemy — a hundred battles without danger."
- "What victory would you achieve without fighting at all?"
- "Attack where they are weak — not where they are strong."
- "Are you building momentum or consuming it?"
- "Which of the five flaws is running this decision?"
