# V45 · MEASURE WHAT MATTERS — John Doerr
Tier 4 · Execution · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Ideas are easy, execution is everything, and the operating system for execution is OKRs — OBJECTIVES (the memorable, qualitative direction) paired with KEY RESULTS (the quantitative, verifiable outcomes) — because what gets measured gets done, but what gets measured AND connected to purpose gets done brilliantly.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Doerr brought OKRs from Grove's Intel to Google and beyond: OKRs are not a performance measurement tool but a COMMUNICATION AND ALIGNMENT system. The magic is in the coupling: Objectives are inspirational and qualitative ("dominate the mid-range smartphone market"); Key Results are specific and time-bound ("ship 10M units in India by Q4", "achieve 4.5★ average rating"). CFRs (Conversations, Feedback, Recognition) are the people-side companion. OKRs should be STRETCH goals (70% completion is success — 100% means you didn't aim high enough), PUBLIC (transparency creates alignment), and not directly tied to compensation (or people sandbag). The book's core insight: most startups fail not from bad ideas but from poor alignment — everyone rowing in different directions.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The OKR structure: Objective + 3-5 KRs
├─ Objective: WHAT you want to accomplish — qualitative, inspirational, time-bound
├─ Key Results: HOW you measure progress — quantitative, verifiable (yes/no by end of period)
├─ Common error: KRs that are tasks ("launch the website") not outcomes ("1000 visitors in first week")
├─ KRs should be stretch: achievable but uncomfortable; 0.7 is a good score
└─ LEAF: test KR quality: can you say "yes" or "no" at the end of the quarter? If the answer is a number or judgment, it's not a real KR

### Branch 2 — Alignment and transparency
├─ OKRs cascade: company OKRs → team OKRs → individual OKRs (loose connection, not mechanical)
├─ ALL OKRs are public — everyone sees everyone else's objectives; this substitutes hierarchy with peer awareness of priorities
├─ Horizontal alignment: teams see which other teams' KRs they depend on; dependencies surface before they block
└─ LEAF: for a founder with 10+ people, transparency is the alignment mechanism; if OKRs are kept in a founder's head or a private doc, they are not OKRs — they are wishes

### Branch 3 — Stretch goals and the 70% rule
├─ A met-all-KRs quarter means the bar was too low; the stretch is where growth comes from
├─ Failure is data, not punishment — sandbagging (setting safe targets) defeats the purpose
├─ The 70% completion sweet spot: enough progress to feel momentum, enough gap to feel the stretch
└─ LEAF: when a founder sets easy targets and meets them all, the engine should ask "what would you aim for if failure were safe?" — that's the real objective

### Branch 4 — CFRs: the people side
├─ Conversations: periodic, light-touch check-ins on progress against OKRs (not annual reviews)
├─ Feedback: peer-to-peer, upward, continuous — specific and actionable, not evaluative
├─ Recognition: public acknowledgment of contributions that advance OKRs
├─ Separation from compensation: OKRs measure stretch/learning; comp measures performance/impact — different things
└─ LEAF: when a founder complains about execution problems, first check if OKRs are in place; if yes, check if CFRs are in place — the human loop is where alignment breaks

### Branch 5 — The superpower: focus + commitment
├─ The #1 OKR superpower: choosing 1-2 company objectives per quarter, not 10
├─ Saying no to good ideas is harder than saying no to bad ones — but the good idea that doesn't serve the ONE objective is a distraction
├─ Every additional objective dilutes attention geometrically
└─ LEAF: a founder with >3 objectives in a quarter has no priorities — they have a list. Force the priority rank until one emerges as the hill to die on.

### Branch 6 — OKRs for early-stage startups
├─ Pre-PMF: the objective should be finding PMF, not hitting revenue targets; KRs measure learning velocity (customer interviews, trial-to-paid, retention data)
├─ Post-PMF: OKRs align growth around the chosen power-building metrics
├─ Solo founder: OKRs still work — external commitment (share with a trusted advisor/board) replaces team visibility
└─ LEAF: adapt the system to stage — a pre-revenue startup's OKRs look nothing like Google's; the principle (focus + alignment + measurement) stays, the content changes

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder has vague direction → APPLY OKR construction: articulate ONE objective and 2-3 verifiable key results for the quarter; test if the KRs pass the yes/no test.
- WHEN a team misaligns → APPLY transparency protocol: publish everyone's OKRs; map who depends on whom; make the mismatches visible.
- WHEN a founder sets easy targets → APPLY stretch recalibration: ask what the stretch target would be if failure were safe; move the bar up.
- WHEN a founder has too many priorities → APPLY focus forcing: rank all objectives; the #2 priority gets shelved, not pursued alongside #1.
- WHEN execution is good but impact is missing → APPLY KR quality audit: are the KRs outcomes (real result) or outputs (tasks done)? Convert outputs to outcomes.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
