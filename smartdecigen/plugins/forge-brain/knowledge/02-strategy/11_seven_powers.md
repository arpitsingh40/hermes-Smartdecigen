# V11 · 7 POWERS — Hamilton Helmer
Tier 3 · Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
A business only compounds value if it holds POWER — a market condition creating persistent differential returns — and every power must pass a dual test: a BENEFIT (value created) plus a BARRIER (why competitors can't arbitrage it away); everything else is operational excellence that competition will eventually equalize.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Helmer reduces durable advantage to exactly seven powers: Scale Economies, Network Economies, Counter-Positioning, Switching Costs, Branding, Cornered Resource, and Process Power. Strategy's fundamental equation: value = market size × power. Crucially, powers have a TIME CLOCK — each is typically established in a specific stage (origination, takeoff, stability), so a founder must know which powers are available to them NOW and design the route to power before the window closes. Operational excellence is necessary but is not strategy: whatever competitors can copy, they will.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The seven powers (benefit + barrier each)
├─ Scale Economies: per-unit cost falls with volume; barrier = challenger must lose money to match price
├─ Network Economies: value rises with each user; barrier = challenger can't offer comparable value at any price below critical mass
├─ Counter-Positioning: newcomer adopts a superior model the incumbent CAN'T copy without damaging its own economics; barrier = incumbent's rational self-harm avoidance
├─ Switching Costs: users face real cost to leave; barrier = challenger must compensate the switch
├─ Branding: habitual trust/affect commands premium; barrier = decades and consistency to replicate
├─ Cornered Resource: exclusive access to a coveted asset (talent, recipe, license, location); barrier = the resource is literally unavailable
├─ Process Power: embedded organizational capability evolved over years; barrier = time-compression diseconomies (can't buy it fast)
└─ LEAF: for any claimed advantage, demand BOTH halves — a benefit without a barrier is a feature awaiting copying

### Branch 2 — The power progression (timing windows)
├─ Origination stage (pre-PMF): Counter-Positioning and Cornered Resource are available NOW — the two powers a tiny founder can actually hold
├─ Takeoff stage (explosive growth): Network Economies, Scale Economies, Switching Costs get built HERE or never
├─ Stability stage: Branding and Process Power mature over long consistency
└─ LEAF: diagnose the founder's stage first; recommending brand-building to a pre-PMF founder or counter-positioning hunts to a mature one wastes the window

### Branch 3 — Operational excellence is not power
├─ Better ads, better ops, harder work — all copyable, all eventually competed away to zero excess return
├─ They matter (you must be operationally viable) but they are the price of entry, not the moat
└─ LEAF: when a founder's "advantage" is working harder or executing better, name it honestly: that's fuel, not a moat — then hunt for the real power route

### Branch 4 — Counter-positioning (the founder's weapon)
├─ The incumbent sees the new model, understands it, and still rationally declines to copy (cannibalization, channel conflict, margin dilution)
├─ Example logic: a D2C brand with direct customer data vs distributors' shelf model; discount brokers vs full-service
└─ LEAF: ask "what could I do that the big player would refuse to match because matching hurts them more than losing me?"

### Branch 5 — Cornered resource in small business form
├─ Grandmother's recipes, a locked supplier relationship, a rare license, a location, a person
├─ Test: is it truly exclusive, truly coveted, and does it transfer to the P&L?
└─ LEAF: inventory the founder's genuinely exclusive assets; most founders undervalue what only they have and overvalue what everyone can buy

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder describes their advantage → APPLY the dual test: benefit AND barrier; label copyable advantages as operational excellence and say what that implies.
- WHEN planning long-term direction → APPLY power progression: identify stage, name the 1-2 powers whose window is open NOW, design the route.
- WHEN a founder competes against incumbents → APPLY counter-positioning search: find the move that punishes the incumbent for copying.
- WHEN evaluating deals that trade away assets (exclusivity, data, brand control) → APPLY power audit: does this contract SELL a future power (cornered resource, direct customer relationships) for short-term volume?
- WHEN a founder over-invests in polish pre-PMF → APPLY stage discipline: the only strategy questions that matter now are power-route questions.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "That's a benefit. Where's the barrier?"
- "Anything a competitor can copy, they eventually will."
- "Which power's window is open at YOUR stage?"
- "What would the big player refuse to copy because it hurts them?"
- "Are you selling your future moat for this quarter's volume?"
