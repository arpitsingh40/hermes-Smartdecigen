# V10 · GOOD STRATEGY / BAD STRATEGY — Richard Rumelt
Tier 3 · Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Strategy is not goals, vision, or ambition — it is a DIAGNOSIS of the critical challenge, a GUIDING POLICY for dealing with it, and a set of COHERENT ACTIONS that concentrate force at the decisive point; anything without these three is decoration.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Most "strategies" are bad strategy: fluff (buzzwords), failure to face the actual problem, mistaking goals for strategy ("reach 15L/month" is a desire, not a plan), and incoherent objectives (a dog's dinner of wishes). Good strategy is rare because it requires choosing — saying no — and honestly naming the obstacle. Its power comes from LEVERAGE: anticipating rivals' and customers' behavior, finding the pivot point where focused effort is amplified, and CONCENTRATING resources there instead of spreading them. Strategy is a HYPOTHESIS to be tested and revised, not a commitment to be defended.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The kernel: diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent action
├─ Diagnosis: name the critical challenge, simplifying the mess to its decisive aspect ("our real problem is not growth, it's cash conversion speed")
├─ Guiding policy: an overall approach that rules things OUT ("fast-payback channels only until break-even")
├─ Coherent actions: resource-backed moves that reinforce each other, not a wish list
└─ LEAF: test any plan by asking for its kernel; if the diagnosis is missing, everything downstream is guessing

### Branch 2 — The four hallmarks of bad strategy
├─ Fluff: jargon masquerading as insight ("customer-centric omnichannel synergy")
├─ Failure to face the challenge: strategy that never names the obstacle cannot overcome it
├─ Mistaking goals for strategy: revenue targets and "push harder" are outcomes, not paths
├─ Bad objectives: incoherent (conflicting) or impracticable (blue-sky) goal lists
└─ LEAF: when a founder presents targets as their plan, the engine's first job is to say "that's the goal — what's the obstacle?"

### Branch 3 — Leverage: anticipation, pivot points, concentration
├─ Anticipation: predictable behaviors of others (competitors' habits, customers' inertia, platforms' incentives) are free strategic material
├─ Pivot point: where a small, feasible push produces outsized effect (the ONE channel, constraint, or segment that unlocks the rest)
├─ Concentration: victory comes from focusing on the fewest, most decisive objectives — spreading kills
└─ LEAF: if the plan advances everything a little, it advances nothing; force-rank until one pivot point remains

### Branch 4 — Proximate objectives
├─ Leaders should set targets CLOSE enough to be hit: ambiguity collapses when the next objective is concrete and feasible
├─ A proximate objective for a fragile startup: "get 10 pilot stores reordering within 45 days", not "win Kerala"
└─ LEAF: translate every grand goal into the nearest objective whose achievement changes what's possible

### Branch 5 — Chain-link systems
├─ When performance is limited by the weakest link (product × distribution × cash × founder-time), strengthening strong links wastes resources
├─ Excellence in chain-linked systems requires fixing the limiting link FIRST, sequentially
└─ LEAF: identify the founder's current weakest link; refuse to optimize other links until it's fixed

### Branch 6 — Strategy as hypothesis; inertia and entropy as weapons
├─ Good strategy is an educated guess about what will work, deliberately designed to produce feedback fast
├─ Rivals' inertia (their commitments, routines, margins they won't cannibalize) is an exploitable asset
└─ LEAF: ask "what can't the incumbent copy without hurting itself?" — that's where a small player wins

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder states a goal as a plan → APPLY kernel construction: extract the diagnosis (the ONE critical obstacle), propose a guiding policy that excludes options, derive 2-4 mutually reinforcing actions.
- WHEN the founder's efforts are spread across many fronts → APPLY concentration: identify the pivot point, explicitly deprioritize the rest, name what saying-no protects.
- WHEN facing bigger competitors → APPLY inertia analysis: find the move incumbents cannot mirror without self-harm.
- WHEN a goal overwhelms ("15L/month") → APPLY proximate objective: define the nearest concrete win that re-opens the game.
- WHEN reviewing progress → APPLY hypothesis discipline: what did the strategy predict, what happened, what does the revision look like?

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "That's the goal. What's the obstacle?"
- "A plan that advances everything a little advances nothing."
- "What's the nearest objective that changes what's possible?"
- "Which link is weakest? Fix that. Only that."
- "What can your competitor not copy without bleeding?"
