# V12 · PLAYING TO WIN — A.G. Lafley & Roger Martin
Tier 3 · Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Strategy is an integrated cascade of five explicit choices — winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities, and management systems — and the essence of the cascade is EXCLUSION: a strategy that doesn't clearly say who you will NOT serve and how you will NOT compete is a budget, not a strategy.

## TRUNK — core thesis
From P&G's transformation: strategy = choice, specifically five nested choices where each constrains the next. "Where to play" (segments, geographies, channels, price tiers) and "how to win" (cost leadership or differentiation IN that playing field) form the heart. Playing everywhere = playing to participate = slow death. The book's greatest tool for decision-making under disagreement: WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE (WWHTT) — instead of debating opinions, reverse-engineer the conditions under which each option would be the right choice, then test the least certain condition. This converts boardroom fights into checkable facts.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The strategy choice cascade
├─ 1. Winning aspiration: define winning (not "be present", WIN with specific customers)
├─ 2. Where to play: choose the field narrowly enough to dominate — segment, geography, channel, tier
├─ 3. How to win: the value proposition + competitive advantage ON that field; must be chosen, not assumed
├─ 4. Core capabilities: what you must be reliably great at to deliver #3
├─ 5. Management systems: the measures and routines that keep capabilities real
└─ LEAF: the cascade must read coherently downward; a how-to-win that doesn't fit the where-to-play is fantasy

### Branch 2 — Where to play: the choice founders dodge
├─ Playing to play ("we sell to everyone in India online and offline") guarantees mediocrity for a resource-constrained founder
├─ The narrower the field, the more a small player's force concentrates (30 stores in Kochi CAN be dominated; "South India" cannot, yet)
└─ LEAF: when channels multiply (D2C + retail + quick commerce + distributor), force the where-to-play choice: which field can you WIN this year?

### Branch 3 — How to win: two doors only
├─ Low cost (structural, not discounting) or differentiation (a reason to pay more) — stuck-in-the-middle loses to both
├─ Must name WHY customers choose you over each real alternative, per playing field
└─ LEAF: "better quality" is not a how-to-win unless customers measurably pay for the difference

### Branch 4 — What Would Have To Be True (the crown jewel)
├─ For each strategic option, list the conditions (industry, customer value, capabilities, competitor reaction) under which it's a GREAT choice
├─ Identify the condition you're LEAST confident about; design the cheapest test of that condition
├─ Kills advocacy battles: nobody defends an opinion, everyone examines conditions
└─ LEAF: convert every "should I do X?" into "what would have to be true for X to be right?" — then check the shakiest condition first

### Branch 5 — Strategy as ongoing choice under uncertainty
├─ Strategy is not perfect planning; it's making the clearest choices possible, then revising as conditions reveal themselves
├─ Reverse-engineer competitors: what choices are THEY making, what must they believe?
└─ LEAF: infer competitor strategy from their behavior; their cascade exposes where they will and won't fight you

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder faces competing options → APPLY WWHTT: enumerate the conditions for each option to win, isolate the least certain, propose its cheapest test.
- WHEN the founder is spread across channels/segments → APPLY where-to-play forcing: pick the winnable field for the next 6-12 months; write down who is deliberately NOT served.
- WHEN a value proposition is vague → APPLY how-to-win doors: structurally cheaper, or worth paying more — which one, proven how?
- WHEN building the direction package → APPLY cascade coherence: aspiration → field → win-mechanism → needed capability → tracking system, each constraining the next.
- WHEN competitors move → APPLY reverse cascade: what must they believe for that move to make sense, and where does that leave openings?

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "What would have to be true for this to be the right choice?"
- "Which field can you actually WIN this year — not enter, win?"
- "Who are you deliberately NOT serving? No answer = no strategy."
- "Cheaper or worth-more — pick a door."
- "Test the condition you're least sure about, before money moves."
