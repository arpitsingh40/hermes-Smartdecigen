# V13 · THE ART OF ACTION — Stephen Bungay
Tier 3 · Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Between a plan and its results lie three unavoidable gaps — knowledge (we know less than we'd like), alignment (people understand differently than we'd like), and effects (actions produce outcomes different than we'd like) — and the cure is not more planning or control but DIRECTED OPPORTUNISM: state the intent and its why, give people freedom in the how, and re-plan as reality answers.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Drawing on 19th-century Prussian military doctrine (Auftragstaktik/mission command) refined through von Moltke: "no plan survives contact with the enemy" is not a complaint, it's a design constraint. Organizations fail by responding to uncertainty with MORE detail: thicker plans (knowledge gap), more instructions (alignment gap), more controls (effects gap) — each makes things worse. Instead: plan only as far as circumstances allow, communicate INTENT (the what and the why, never the detailed how), let executors adapt methods, and use back-briefing to verify shared understanding. Strategy becomes a framework for decision-making, not a script.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The three gaps
├─ Knowledge gap: plan-vs-reality — you cannot know enough; act on the knowable, discover the rest by acting
├─ Alignment gap: plan-vs-people — instructions are interpreted; shared intent survives interpretation, detailed orders don't
├─ Effects gap: action-vs-outcome — environments respond; feedback loops beat forecasts
└─ LEAF: when a founder's plan failed "despite the plan being right", locate WHICH gap ate it — the fix differs per gap

### Branch 2 — The reflexive failure (doing more of the wrong thing)
├─ More detail in plans → slower, more brittle (knowledge gap widens)
├─ More instructions → less thinking at the edge (alignment gap widens)
├─ More metrics/controls → gaming and report-theater (effects gap widens)
└─ LEAF: when a founder responds to chaos by writing a longer plan or micromanaging harder, name the reflex and reverse it

### Branch 3 — Commander's intent
├─ Every directive: MY INTENT (outcome + why it matters), constraints (what must not happen), freedoms (what the executor owns)
├─ Two levels rule: everyone should know the intent of their own task AND the intent one level above it — so when circumstances break the plan, they can improvise toward the higher purpose
└─ LEAF: test delegation quality by asking the delegate: "what are you trying to achieve, and why?" — if the answer is a task list, alignment has already failed

### Branch 4 — Back-briefing
├─ After giving intent, the receiver explains BACK their understanding and their plan — misalignment surfaces before execution, not after failure
├─ Cheap, fast, catastrophically underused
└─ LEAF: prescribe back-briefs for every important delegation, vendor agreement, or co-founder split of work

### Branch 5 — Plan only as far as you can see
├─ Von Moltke: plan up to the first contact with the enemy; beyond that, prepare options, not scripts
├─ For founders: detailed planning to the next feedback event (pilot result, campaign data), sketch beyond
└─ LEAF: replace the 12-month detailed roadmap with: this month in detail → next quarter in intent → the year in direction

### Branch 6 — Strategy as decision framework
├─ A good strategy lets people at the edge make THE SAME decision the founder would, without asking
└─ LEAF: write guiding policies as decision rules ("we never trade margin below X for volume"), not as aspirations

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a plan failed → APPLY gap diagnosis: knowledge, alignment, or effects? Prescribe per-gap (act-to-learn / intent+back-brief / faster feedback), never "plan harder".
- WHEN a founder delegates or hires → APPLY intent formulation: outcome + why + constraints + freedoms, then demand a back-brief.
- WHEN a founder builds a long detailed roadmap → APPLY planning horizon discipline: detail to the next feedback event only; options beyond.
- WHEN the founder micromanages under stress → APPLY the reflex reversal: fewer instructions, clearer intent, faster feedback loops.
- WHEN the engine issues next actions → APPLY the two-level rule: every action states its own purpose AND the higher intent it serves — so the founder can adapt the action when reality shifts without losing the point.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Which gap ate the plan: knowledge, alignment, or effects?"
- "Tell people what and why. Let them own the how."
- "Ask them to explain it back. Misalignment surfaces in minutes, not months."
- "Plan in detail only to the next moment reality gets a vote."
- "A strategy works when your people make your decision without you in the room."
