# V48 · YOUR STRATEGY NEEDS A STRATEGY — Martin Reeves, Knut Haanæs, Janmejaya Sinha
Tier 3 · Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
There is no one best way to do strategy — the right approach depends on the PREDICTABILITY of your environment (can you forecast?) and the MALLEABILITY (can you shape it?); most companies fail not because their strategy was wrong but because they applied the wrong style of strategy for the environment they were actually in.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The five strategy archetypes
├─ CLASSICAL (predictable, hard to shape): Analyze, plan, execute — the Porter/Rumelt approach; works in mature industries with stable structures
├─ ADAPTIVE (unpredictable, hard to shape): Experiment, iterate, scale what works — the Lean Startup approach; works in high-velocity markets
├─ SHAPING (predictable, malleable): Engage an ecosystem to create a new market; the platform/disruption approach — works when you can set the rules
├─ VISIONARY (unpredictable, malleable): Envision a new possibility and realize it against the odds — the "bet the farm" startup approach
├─ RENEWAL (environment hostile to existing model): Preserve cash, defend core, pivot — the survival approach, used when current model is dying
└─ LEAF: diagnose the founder's environment FIRST — what is the predictability and malleability? Then choose the strategy style; applying Classical strategy in an Adaptive environment (or vice versa) is the root of most strategic failure

### Branch 2 — The Classical style: plan your way to success
├─ Assumptions: industry is stable and predictable, competitive positions are durable, you can forecast
├─ Approach: analyze industry structure, pick a position, build a plan, execute
├─ Tools: Porter's five forces, experience curve, growth-share matrix
├─ Signals the environment is classical: slow growth, stable regulation, mature industry, established competitors
└─ LEAF: most startup founders use Classical strategy by default (write a plan, execute it) — but most startups live in adaptive or shaping environments; the misfit is the primary cause of "strategy didn't work"

### Branch 3 — The Adaptive style: experiment your way to success
├─ Assumptions: unpredictable, can't forecast more than a few months; advantage is temporary, not durable
├─ Approach: rapid experimentation, iterate, scale winners, kill losers fast
├─ Tools: A/B testing, minimum viable product, real options, portfolio of experiments
├─ The trap: treating an adaptive environment as classical (building a 3-year plan when the market shifts every 3 months)
└─ LEAF: when a founder works from a rigid annual plan in a fast-moving market, name the mismatch and suggest Adaptive strategy: shorter feedback loops, more experiments, less commitment

### Branch 4 — The Shaping style: orchestrate an ecosystem
├─ Assumptions: industry is malleable, you can set standards that others follow; growth comes from platform effects
├─ Approach: build a platform, engage third parties, create network effects, set the rules
├─ Tools: ecosystem mapping, platform design, open innovation
├─ For founders: shaping usually requires a platform play (marketplace, API ecosystem, protocol) — it's the highest-risk and highest-return style
└─ LEAF: shaping strategy suits a founder who has a belief about how the market COULD be structured differently; the key question is: do you have the credibility and resources to set the new rules?

### Branch 5 — The Visionary style: bet your way to success
├─ Assumptions: the environment can be transformed by a single bold bet; you can't predict the path but you can imagine the destination
├─ Approach: commit to a bold vision, persist despite setbacks, learn as you go but don't change the destination
├─ Signals: unique insight (a secret, per Thiel), high uncertainty, the environment is malleable but only through determined effort
└─ LEAF: Visionary strategy is appropriate when the founder has a genuine, contrarian belief about what the future CAN be; it's inappropriate when the founder is just stubborn

### Branch 6 — The Renewal style: survive and pivot
├─ Assumptions: the current business model is no longer viable; incremental fixes won't work
├─ Approach: cut costs drastically, protect cash, refocus the core, prepare to re-enter with a new model
├─ Signals: structural decline, technological disruption, regulatory shock, sustained negative cash flow
└─ LEAF: when a founder's existing strategy is clearly failing, the question is not "how do we execute better" but "do we need Renewal?" — if yes, the first action is cash preservation, not strategy refinement

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder presents a strategy → APPLY environment diagnosis: rate the market's predictability (high/medium/low) and malleability (shapable/not); identify the implied strategy style; check for mismatch.
- WHEN a Classical strategy fails → APPLY environment reassessment: did the founder assume stability that isn't there? Suggest Adaptive or Shaping instead.
- WHEN a founder churns through experiments without progress → APPLY Visionary test: does anyone have a clear belief about the destination? If not, there's no vision to guide the experiments.
- WHEN a platform idea is proposed → APPLY Shaping readiness: does the founder have user base, credibility, or resources to get the first side on board?
- WHEN the current model is dying → APPLY Renewal warning: the time for pruning is NOW, not when cash runs out.
