# V128 · THE COLD START PROBLEM — Andrew Chen
Tier 3 · Growth · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Networked products (marketplaces, social networks, SaaS with collaboration) face a unique challenge: they're useless until enough people use them. This is the cold start problem. Chen (former Uber growth lead, a16z partner) decomposes how successful network-effect companies solved this: starting with a tiny, atomic network where the product delivers value at small scale, then systematically expanding. The Tipping Point = when the network becomes self-sustaining and growth compounds.

## TRUNK
The atomic network: the smallest unit where the product works. For Uber, it was 5 drivers and 20 riders in a neighborhood radius. For Slack, a single team of 3-5 people. For Airbnb, 50 listings in a single neighborhood. Find your atomic network, manually recruit it, prove value, then replicate. The stages: (1) Cold Start — define the atomic network. (2) Tipping Point — the network becomes self-sustaining. (3) Escape Velocity — growth compounds without proportional investment. (4) The Ceiling — growth plateaus. (5) The Moat — network effects defend against competitors. Key metric: network density = active edges / possible edges. Low density = fragile network.

## BRANCHES
- Hard sides vs easy sides: in a marketplace, one side is harder to get (drivers in Uber, hosts in Airbnb). Subsidize the hard side. Charge the easy side.
- Growth loops vs funnels: funnels are linear (money in → customers out). Loops compound (existing users bring new users → more value for all → more users). Design loops, not just funnels.
- Invite mechanics: the most powerful growth loops are invite flows where inviting someone makes the product more valuable for the inviter (collaboration SaaS, social networks, referral programs).
- The magic moment: define the specific action that predicts long-term retention (first ride, first booking, first collaboration). Time users toward it ruthlessly.

## FRUIT
- WHEN a marketplace isn't growing → APPLY atomic network check: have you defined the smallest viable network? Are you recruiting it manually?
- WHEN retention is low → APPLY the magic moment: what's the action that predicts retention, and how long does it take to get there?
- WHEN growth is linear → APPLY loop design: does your product have a built-in mechanism for users to bring users?
- WHEN paid acquisition is the only growth lever → APPLY the cold start diagnosis: you're spending before the network works at small scale.

## SEEDS
- "Find the smallest network where your product creates value. Recruit it manually. Then replicate."
- "Design growth loops, not just funnels. Loops compound; funnels don't."
- "One side of your marketplace is harder to get. Subsidize that side."
- "The magic moment is the action that predicts retention. Time users toward it."
- "Network density matters more than total users. A dense tiny network beats a sparse big one."
