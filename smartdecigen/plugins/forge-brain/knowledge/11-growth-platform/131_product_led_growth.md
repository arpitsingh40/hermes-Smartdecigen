# V131 · PRODUCT-LED GROWTH — Wes Bush
Tier 3 · Growth · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The traditional SaaS growth model — sales-led (SDR → AE → CSM) — is being displaced by product-led growth (PLG), where the product itself is the primary driver of acquisition, activation, retention, and expansion. Bush's framework: the product must deliver value BEFORE the purchase (free tier, free trial, freemium), and the user's experience of that value must naturally lead to upgrade. The result: lower CAC (product does the selling), faster adoption (no sales gatekeeper), and higher NRR (users who discover value independently stay longer).

## TRUNK
The PLG flywheel: (1) Signup — frictionless, instant, no credit card. (2) Activation — user hits the "aha moment" in minutes, not days. (3) Adoption — user integrates the product into their workflow, frequency increases. (4) Expansion — user naturally hits a paywall or usage limit that's tied to value received, not arbitrary pricing tiers. (5) Advocacy — user becomes a champion, inviting colleagues and generating word-of-mouth. Key metrics: time to value (TTV), activation rate (% of signups who hit aha moment), PQL (product qualified lead — a user whose behavior indicates buying intent), expansion revenue. PLG doesn't replace sales — it creates a pipeline of PQLs that sales can close efficiently.

## BRANCHES
- Free tier design: the free tier must be genuinely useful — enough to solve a real problem, but limited enough that growing usage naturally requires paid. Bowling alley framework: one lane free, upgrade for more lanes.
- PQL definition: a PQL is NOT a marketing lead who signed up. A PQL is a user whose behavioral data (usage frequency, team size, features used, time to value) indicates high purchase intent. Sales only touches PQLs.
- Reverse trial: give the paid experience first (all features for 14 days), then downgrade to free. Users who've experienced the premium version upgrade at 2-3x the rate of freemium users.
- Sales + PLG: sales doesn't disappear — it shifts from cold outreach to consultative expansion. Sales receives PQLs that have already experienced value and need help scaling.

## FRUIT
- WHEN CAC is too high → APPLY PLG diagnosis: can the product deliver value before purchase? Is there a free tier or trial?
- WHEN activation rate is low → APPLY time-to-value analysis: how long from signup to aha moment? Can it be reduced to under 5 minutes?
- WHEN free users don't convert → APPLY paywall design: is the paywall tied to value received or arbitrary feature gates?
- WHEN considering PLG → APPLY reverse trial test: would giving the premium experience first, then downgrading, increase conversion?

## SEEDS
- "Let the product do the selling. The best sales call is one the user never needs to make."
- "If a user hasn't experienced the aha moment in 5 minutes, they won't."
- "A PQL is not a lead. It's a user whose behavior is screaming 'I'm ready to buy.'"
- "The free tier must be useful enough to solve a real problem. Too little, and nobody stays."
- "Reverse trials convert at 2-3x freemium. Show them what they're missing first."

## GRAFTS
- → The Lean Startup: the build-measure-learn loop applied to the free-to-paid conversion funnel.
- → Hooked: the habit loops that drive daily active usage in PLG products.
- → Predictable Revenue: the sales process that kicks in after PLG generates PQLs.
