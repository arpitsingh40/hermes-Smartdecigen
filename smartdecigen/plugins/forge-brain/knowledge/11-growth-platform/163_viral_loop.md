# V163 · VIRAL LOOP — Adam Penenberg
Tier 3 · Growth · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The most powerful growth engine is a viral loop — a mechanism where existing users bring new users, who bring more users, in a self-reinforcing cycle. Penenberg traces the history of viral businesses from Tupperware parties to Facebook, showing that viral growth is not luck — it's designed. The viral loop has three stages: (1) a user uses the product, (2) the product inherently exposes the product to new users, (3) those new users sign up and repeat. The viral coefficient (K) = number of new users each existing user brings × the conversion rate of those invites.

## TRUNK
Viral mechanics: (1) Viral coefficient > 1 = exponential growth. Between 0.5 and 1 = linear growth. Below 0.5 = no viral benefit. (2) Cycle time: how long it takes for one user to invite → invitee signs up. Shorter cycle time = faster growth. A K of 0.8 with a 1-day cycle grows faster than a K of 1.2 with a 30-day cycle. (3) Types of virality: word-of-mouth (inherent — product is remarkable), incentivized (user gets benefit for inviting — Dropbox free storage), embedded (product exposes itself inherently — Hotmail's "get your free email" signature), social (network effects — the product is better with friends). (4) Friction points: each step in the viral loop (see product → try product → sign up → use → invite) has a conversion rate. Improving any step improves K more than adding top-of-funnel spend.

## FRUIT
- WHEN designing growth → APPLY viral loop design: does the product inherently expose itself to new users?
- WHEN growth is bottlenecked → APPLY loop friction analysis: which step in the viral loop has the lowest conversion rate?
