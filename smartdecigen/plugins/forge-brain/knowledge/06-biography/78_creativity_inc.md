# V78 · CREATIVITY, INC. — Ed Catmull
Tier 6 · Founder Stories · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Creativity cannot be managed into existence — it can only be protected from the forces that kill it: fear, hierarchy, hidden problems, and the illusion that a successful past predicts a successful future; the leader's job is to build a culture where candor is safe, failure is learning, and the best ideas win regardless of rank.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Catmull, co-founder of Pixar and president of Disney Animation, describes the operating system behind 20+ consecutive animated hits (Toy Story, Finding Nemo, The Incredibles, etc.). The core: the SUCCESS of a previous hit is the #1 danger — it creates arrogance, risk-aversion, and the belief that the formula is known. The antidote is a set of institutional practices: (1) CANDOR — the Braintrust (a peer-review group that gives brutally honest feedback without authority to dictate changes — the no-power rule makes the feedback purely about the work). (2) FAILURE AS PART OF CREATIVITY — every Pixar film starts as an "ugly baby"; the goal is not to avoid problems but to surface them early when they're fixable. (3) BALANCE BETWEEN STRUCTURE AND AUTONOMY — enough process to coordinate, not enough to stifle; the University system gives artists continuing education. (4) POSTMORTEMS WITHOUT BLAME — every project has a postmortem to learn, not to punish. The insight: creativity is not a spark — it's a process of problem-solving embedded in a culture that makes it safe to be wrong.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The Braintrust
├─ A group of peers who review each other's work in progress — no holds barred, no authority, no consequences
├─ The rule: the Braintrust can recommend, not dictate; the director retains final authority — this is essential because it separates the ego from the feedback
├─ The effectiveness depends on the CANDOR of the group — which depends on safety — which depends on the leader modeling vulnerability first
└─ LEAF: establish a founder's Braintrust: a weekly or biweekly meeting where the founder presents the most uncertain current decision to a small trusted group — no authority, pure feedback

### Branch 2 — The ugly baby
├─ Every creative work starts as an "ugly baby" — ugly, flawed, not yet working — and the only way to make it beautiful is to see it honestly and fix it relentlessly
├─ The cost of hiding the ugly baby: problems compound and surface too late to fix (the "saber-toothed tiger" that emerges at the last minute)
├─ The discipline: show unfinished work early and often — the vulnerability is the speed
└─ LEAF: when a founder delays showing a work-in-progress, ask: "what are you protecting?" — the answer reveals the fear that blocks candor

### Branch 3 — Postmortems without blame
├─ After each film, Pixar runs a detailed postmortem: what worked? What didn't? What would we change?
├─ The rule: no names — the focus is on the PROCESS, not the people; blame-free inquiry produces learning; blame-driven inquiry produces defensiveness
├─ The most dangerous thing a postmortem can produce: CONFIRMATION that success was deserved and failure was bad luck
└─ LEAF: after every milestone (failed or successful), run a 30-minute postmortem with the rule: no names — only process and patterns

### Branch 4 — The hidden cost of success
├─ Success creates the illusion that you know what you're doing; the next project starts from a place of arrogance rather than curiosity
├─ The danger: "we've done it before, we can do it again" — a belief that kills the beginner's mind and the willingness to experiment
├─ The antidote: institutional paranoia — "every success hides problems we haven't seen yet"
└─ LEAF: after a win, the founder should ask: "what are we getting complacent about right now?" — the answer identifies the next hidden problem

### Branch 5 — Structure that enables, not constrains
├─ Pixar's building was designed to force unexpected collisions: bathrooms in the atrium, a central staircase, the mailroom next to the cafe
├─ The principle: the physical and organizational structure should increase the odds of DIFFUSION — ideas crossing boundaries accidentally
├─ But structure is a servant, not a master: too little and there's chaos, too much and there's bureaucracy — the founder's job is to keep the tension productive
└─ LEAF: audit the team's communication patterns: where do ideas meet by accident? If there's no space for serendipity, design one — a shared Slack channel, a weekly show-and-tell, a cross-functional lunch

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder avoids giving honest feedback → APPLY the Braintrust model: separate feedback from authority — make it safe to be wrong.
- WHEN a project is hidden until "ready" → APPLY the ugly-baby rule: show the earliest, worst version now — when it's cheap to fix.
- WHEN a postmortem blames people → APPLY the no-names rule: focus on process, not person — the learning goes up, the defensiveness goes down.
- WHEN the team rests on past success → APPLY the success-paranoia question: "what are we blind to because we won?"
- WHEN communication feels siloed → APPLY the collision audit: where should ideas meet that they currently don't?
