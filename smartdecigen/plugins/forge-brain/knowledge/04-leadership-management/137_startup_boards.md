# V137 · STARTUP BOARDS — Brad Feld & Mahendra Ramsinghani
Tier 3 · Leadership · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Most founders build their first board by accident — whoever led the last round gets a seat. This is a catastrophic mistake. The board is one of the most powerful forces in a company's trajectory; a bad board can destroy a good company. Feld's framework: build the board deliberately, manage it proactively, and understand that board dynamics are not governance theater — they are the actual decision-making body for the most important strategic choices.

## TRUNK
Board composition: ideal = 5 members at Series A (2 founders, 2 investors, 1 independent). Independent directors bring objectivity, experience, and no conflict of interest. Avoid: investor-heavy boards (they have fiduciary duty to their LPs, not to your company), celebrity boards (famous names who never show up), and rubber-stamp boards (yes-men who add no value). Board meetings: send materials 3-5 days ahead, spend 80% of the meeting on strategic discussion (not status updates), ensure every director participates, and close with an executive session (CEO + independent directors only, no investors). The CEO-board relationship: the CEO manages the board — not the other way around. The CEO sets the agenda, drives the conversation, and owns the relationship.

## BRANCHES
- Selecting directors: screen for strategic contribution, network reach, and industry expertise. The best directors teach the CEO something every meeting. Interview them like you'd interview a key hire.
- Board failures: (1) Investor-heavy (boards fire founders faster), (2) Absentee directors (miss meetings, miss context, make bad decisions), (3) Board as status-report-theater (no real discussion, no value).
- Firing a director: it's legal (board vote) and sometimes necessary. A director who adds no value is occupying a seat that could be transformative.
- The independent director: the secret weapon. They have no economic conflict, they've often been CEOs themselves, and they provide the honest feedback investors and employees won't.
- Legal duties: duty of care (be informed), duty of loyalty (put company first), duty of confidentiality. Board members have legal obligations, not just advisory roles.

## FRUIT
- WHEN building the first board → APPLY the 5-member target: 2 founders, 2 investors, 1 independent. Never give investors majority.
- WHEN board meetings are status updates → APPLY the 80/20 rule: 80% strategy discussion, 20% updates. Send updates in advance.
- WHEN a director adds no value → APPLY the hiring test: would you hire this person today? If no, have the conversation.
- WHEN considering an independent → APPLY the CEO-test: has this person been a CEO? Will they teach you something every meeting?
