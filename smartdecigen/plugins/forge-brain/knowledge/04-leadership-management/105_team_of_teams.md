# V105 · TEAM OF TEAMS — General Stanley McChrystal
Tier 3 · Leadership · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The hierarchical command-and-control model that worked for centuries is failing in a world of complexity and speed. McChrystal transformed JSOC from a slow, siloed bureaucracy into a networked "team of teams" that could move faster than Al Qaeda. The core insight: in complex environments, adaptability beats efficiency. A team of teams shares consciousness (everyone sees the same information) and empowered execution (decisions pushed to the lowest possible level).

## TRUNK
Key principles: (1) Shared consciousness — radical information sharing. JSOC's daily O&I (Operations & Intelligence) briefing connected 7,000 people across 70 locations. Everyone knew what everyone else was doing. (2) Empowered execution — push decision authority down. The person closest to the problem makes the call within a clear commander's intent. (3) Liaison officers — embed people from one team into another. Builds trust and breaks silos. (4) "Eyes on, hands off" — leader sets the vision, then trusts the team to execute.

## FRUIT
- WHEN teams are siloed → APPLY shared consciousness: daily cross-team standup, open dashboards, O&I briefings.
- WHEN decisions bottleneck at the top → APPLY empowered execution: push authority down within commander's intent.
- WHEN building cross-functional teams → APPLY liaison embedding: rotate people into other teams temporarily.

## SEEDS
- "In complexity, adaptability beats efficiency."
- "Shared consciousness: everyone sees the same picture."
- "Eyes on, hands off. Set intent, trust execution."
- "The leader's job is to create the environment, not make every decision."

- → The Culture Code: the psychological safety that enables empowered execution.
- → Turn the Ship Around: the leader-leader model applied to naval command.
