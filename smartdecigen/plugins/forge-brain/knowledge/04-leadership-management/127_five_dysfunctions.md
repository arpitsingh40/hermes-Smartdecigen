# V127 · THE FIVE DYSFUNCTIONS OF A TEAM — Patrick Lencioni
Tier 3 · Leadership · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The single biggest driver of organizational performance is the health of the leadership team — and dysfunction follows a predictable, layered pattern. Lencioni's pyramid has 5 levels, each building on the one below: (1) Absence of Trust → (2) Fear of Conflict → (3) Lack of Commitment → (4) Avoidance of Accountability → (5) Inattention to Results. Fix the bottom layer first; you cannot fix accountability without trust.

## TRUNK
The 5 dysfunctions: (1) Trust: unwillingness to be vulnerable. Team members hide weaknesses, mistakes, and needs for help. (2) Conflict: fear of productive ideological debate. Meetings are boring and decisions avoid the real issues. (3) Commitment: without airing disagreements, people don't buy in — they feign agreement and later undermine decisions. (4) Accountability: without commitment, people avoid calling out peers on behaviors that hurt the team. (5) Results: when trust, conflict, commitment, and accountability are absent, people focus on their own ego, career, and department — not collective results. The first team (the leadership team) sets the pattern for the entire organization.

## BRANCHES
- Building trust: personal histories exercise (share where you grew up, your family, your biggest challenge), 360 feedback, and the leader going first with vulnerability.
- Mastering conflict: real-time permission — when conflict gets uncomfortable, the leader names it: "this is good, we need this debate." Mining for conflict — actively surfacing disagreements rather than burying them.
- Achieving commitment: at the end of every meeting, explicitly state the decision, the deadline, and what each person committed to. Cascading communication — within 24 hours, every team member communicates the same message to their teams.
- Embracing accountability: peer accountability (team members calling out team members) is more powerful than leader accountability. The leader must model receiving hard feedback without retribution.
- Focusing on results: define a clear, shared, measurable scoreboard. Team-based rewards, not individual recognition. The leader must be the first to sacrifice their ego/department for the team's results.

## FRUIT
- WHEN the exec team feels siloed → APPLY dysfunction diagnosis: start at level 1 (trust). Personal histories exercise this week.
- WHEN meetings have no real debate → APPLY conflict mining: "what are we NOT saying that we should be?"
- WHEN decisions are revisited next meeting → APPLY commitment check: did we explicitly state the decision and get public commitment?
- WHEN teammates aren't holding each other accountable → APPLY peer accountability: will the leader model receiving hard feedback first?
- WHEN individual departments are winning but the company is losing → APPLY results focus: what's the one shared scoreboard?

## SEEDS
- "Trust is the foundation. Without vulnerability, there is no trust."
- "If your meetings are boring, you're avoiding conflict."
- "Agreement at the meeting followed by disagreement in the hallway is a commitment failure."
- "Peer accountability is more powerful than leader accountability."
- "The first team sets the pattern. Fix the leadership team first."

## GRAFTS
- → The Culture Code: the psychological safety and belonging cues that enable trust.
- → Principles: radical truth and transparency — the practices Lencioni describes.
- → Radical Candor: the care personally + challenge directly framework for accountability.
