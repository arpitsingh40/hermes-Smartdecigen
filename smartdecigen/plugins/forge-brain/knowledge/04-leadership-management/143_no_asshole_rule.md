# V143 · THE NO ASSHOLE RULE — Robert Sutton
Tier 3 · Leadership · Tree Memory

## ROOT
One toxic person — a "certified asshole" — can destroy team performance, drive out top performers, and create costs that far exceed their individual contribution. Sutton's research (from Stanford) shows that toxic employees create 2-3x their salary in costs: turnover of good people, lost productivity from people avoiding them, management time spent managing around them. The rule is simple: no matter how brilliant, never hire or tolerate assholes. The cost is always higher than the benefit.

## TRUNK
The total cost of assholes (TCA): (1) direct costs — their salary, benefits. (2) Indirect costs — turnover of people who leave because of them, distraction of people who have to manage around them, lost productivity of people they demoralize. (3) Reputation costs — customers and partners who won't deal with you. Even "brilliant jerks" cost more than they produce — and the evidence that they're irreplaceable is almost always false. Enforcement: define what "asshole behavior" IS in behavioral terms (public humiliation, personal attacks, credit-stealing), enforce the rule consistently (yes, even the top salesperson), and model it from the top.

## FRUIT
- WHEN a brilliant performer is toxic → APPLY TCA calculation: add up the cost of people who've left, the lost productivity, the management time. Compare to their contribution.
- WHEN setting culture policy → APPLY behavioral definition: list the specific behaviors that won't be tolerated. "Asshole" is too vague.

## SEEDS
- "One toxic person costs 2-3x their salary in turnover, lost productivity, and management time."
- "No matter how brilliant, the cost of an asshole is always higher than the benefit."
- "Define 'asshole' in behavioral terms. Then enforce the rule — even for the top performer."

## GRAFTS
- → The Culture Code: the psychological safety that toxic people destroy.
- → Radical Candor: how to give the hard feedback before it reaches the firing point.
