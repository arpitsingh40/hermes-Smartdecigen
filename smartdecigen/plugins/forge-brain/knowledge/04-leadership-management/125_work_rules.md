# V125 · WORK RULES! — Laszlo Bock
Tier 3 · Leadership · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Google's former SVP of People Operations shares the data-driven, scientific approach that made Google one of the most desired workplaces. The core insight: most HR practices are based on tradition and intuition, not evidence — and the evidence often points in the opposite direction. From hiring (structured interviews beat unstructured by massive margins) to compensation (pay unfairly — reward your best people disproportionately), Work Rules! applies the scientific method to people management.

## TRUNK
Key principles: (1) Give your work meaning — people are motivated by mission, not perks. (2) Trust your people — default to open (transparent salaries, open calendars, shared OKRs). (3) Hire only people better than you — never compromise on quality; B-players hire C-players. (4) Pay unfairly — your best people are worth 10x and should be paid accordingly; don't let fairness hold back rewards for excellence. (5) Nudge — small environmental changes shape behavior more than policies. (6) Manage the struggling and the stellar differently — treat everyone with respect but don't manage everyone the same way.

## BRANCHES
- Hiring: structured interviews with pre-defined questions and scoring rubrics predict performance 2x better than unstructured conversations. Work sample tests are the single best predictor. Brainteasers are useless.
- Performance management: annual reviews are too infrequent. Continuous feedback + quarterly check-ins + peer input = a richer picture. Separate development conversations from rating conversations.
- Compensation: spread (10x between top and average) is not a moral failing — it's an economic reality. Pay your best people what they're worth or they'll find it elsewhere.
- Development: the best managers are coaches, not supervisors. The 8 key behaviors of great managers (from Google's Project Oxygen): be a good coach, empower the team, express interest in well-being, be productive and results-oriented, be a good communicator, help with career development, have a clear vision, have technical skills.
- Culture: default to open. Transparency (salaries, decisions, metrics) builds trust faster than any team-building exercise.

## FRUIT
- WHEN hiring → APPLY structured interviews + work sample: scorecard-based, not gut-based. Skip brainteasers entirely.
- WHEN a top performer might leave → APPLY "pay unfairly": what would it cost to lose them vs. what would it cost to keep them?
- WHEN performance reviews are overdue → APPLY continuous feedback: separate development from evaluation, make feedback weekly not annual.
- WHEN building culture → APPLY "default to open": what information is hidden that, if shared, would increase trust?

## SEEDS
- "Structured interviews with scoring rubrics predict performance 2x better than unstructured chats."
- "Pay your best people what they're worth. 'Fairness' across all performers is a retention killer."
- "Annual reviews are too infrequent. Weekly coaching beats annual evaluation."

- "The best managers are coaches, not supervisors. The 8 behaviors can be learned."
