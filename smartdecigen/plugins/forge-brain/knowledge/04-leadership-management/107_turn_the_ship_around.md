# V107 · TURN THE SHIP AROUND — L. David Marquet
Tier 3 · Leadership · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Marquet took command of the USS Santa Fe, the worst-performing submarine in the fleet. His key insight: the traditional leader-follower model ("I order, you execute") creates passive followers who wait for orders. When he gave an impossible order by accident and his crew tried to execute it anyway without speaking up, he realized the model was broken. He shifted to a leader-leader model: every person is a leader in their domain. The crew went from worst to best, with the highest retention and promotion rates in the Navy.

## TRUNK
The leader-leader model: (1) Control → competence: give control away. Instead of giving orders, give people the technical knowledge to make good decisions. (2) "I intend to..." — the most powerful phrase. Crew members state their intention and the captain approves, rather than the captain giving orders and the crew complying. (3) Push authority to information — the person with the information makes the decision. (4) Don't brief, certify — instead of the leader briefing the team, the team briefs the leader. (5) Think out loud — leaders model uncertainty and problem-solving publicly. The result: a crew of 135 leaders, not 1 leader and 134 followers.

## FRUIT
- WHEN the team waits for orders → APPLY "I intend to": shift from "tell me what to do" to "here's what I intend to do."
- WHEN the leader is the bottleneck → APPLY control → competence: stop giving orders, start teaching. Give away control as competence grows.
- WHEN meetings are leader monologues → APPLY team briefs: the team presents to the leader.

## SEEDS
- "Move from leader-follower to leader-leader. Everyone is a leader in their domain."
- "Don't give orders. Give competence. Then give control."
- "I intend to — the most powerful two words in leadership."
- "Push authority to where the information is."

## GRAFTS
- → Team of Teams: the networked organization that enables leader-leader at scale.
- → High Output Management: the task-relevant maturity that determines how much control to give.
