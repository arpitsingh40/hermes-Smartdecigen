# V112 · WHO — Geoff Smart & Randy Street
Tier 3 · Leadership · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The single biggest business decision is WHO you hire. A-players deliver 3-10x the output of B-players, yet most hiring processes are unstructured conversations that predict nothing. The solution is the A Method: a 4-step process that dramatically improves hiring accuracy by replacing gut feel with structured scorecards, sourcing, selecting, and selling.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Traditional hiring is broken: job descriptions are wishlists not outcomes, interviews are unstructured chats, references are skipped, and hiring decisions are made on likability. Smart & Street's research (400+ CEO terminations studied) found that 50% of hires fail within 18 months. The A Method flips this: define outcomes (not activities), source systematically (not reactively), interview using the chronological deep-dive, and sell the opportunity to the right candidate — in that order, every time.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Scorecard: outcomes, not activities
├─ A scorecard defines 3-5 outcomes the person must achieve (not responsibilities or requirements)
├─ Mission: 1-2 sentence overview of why the role exists
├─ Outcomes: specific, measurable results (not "manage the sales team" but "grow new ARR from $2M to $5M in 12 months with team of 4")
├─ Competencies: 3-5 behaviors that distinguish A-players (e.g., efficiency, honesty, organization)
├─ Culture fit: specific values alignment, not "they seem nice"
└─ LEAF: if you can't write a scorecard, you don't understand the role — don't hire until you can

### Branch 2 — Source: systematic, not reactive
├─ Generate referrals before you have an opening — ask "who are the 3 best people you've worked with?"
├─ Recruit the person who already has the job, not the one looking for it
├─ Deputize: give each team member 5 names to call — social networks beat job boards
└─ LEAF: the best people aren't looking; you must find them

### Branch 3 — Select: 4 structured interviews
├─ Screening interview (phone, 30 min): 4 questions — what are your career goals, what are you great at, what are you NOT great at, who were your last 5 bosses and what would they rate you 1-10
├─ Topgrading interview (in-person, 3 hours): walk chronologically through every job, asking for each — what were you hired to do, what did you accomplish, what mistakes did you make, who did you work with, why did you leave
├─ Focused interviews (2-3 people): each interviewer probes one specific competency deeply
├─ Reference interviews (3-5 calls): ask peers, bosses, subordinates — not the candidate's picks — "in what context would this person NOT succeed?"
└─ LEAF: the chronological deep-dive is the most predictive tool in hiring; skip everything else before you skip this

### Branch 4 — Sell: close the right person
├─ Fit: show how their scorecard outcomes align with their personal goals
├─ Family: involve their family — a move that family opposes will fail within 18 months
├─ Freedom: define their autonomy and decision rights explicitly
├─ Fortune: tie compensation to scorecard outcomes, not time served
├─ Fun: describe the culture honestly — don't oversell
└─ LEAF: the best candidates have options; the A Method works because it shows you're the most professional, serious, and attractive option

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder is about to hire → APPLY scorecard discipline: force definition of 3-5 measurable outcomes before allowing the conversation to proceed to sourcing.
- WHEN a candidate feels "off" but the founder can't articulate why → APPLY the topgrading interview pattern: walk through every prior role, find the pattern of success and failure.
- WHEN the founder is the bottleneck because they haven't hired → APPLY sourcing discipline: name who already has this job and build a referral chain.
- WHEN a hire failed within months → APPLY autopsy: was the scorecard missing? Were references skipped? Which step of the A Method was skipped?
- WHEN a hire is dragging → APPLY the 3-month test: have they achieved ANY of their scorecard outcomes? If not by month 3, the trajectory rarely reverses.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "What are the 3-5 outcomes this person must achieve in their first year — not their responsibilities, their results."
- "Who already has this job elsewhere that you wish you had?"
- "Walk me through every job they've had. What did they accomplish? What mistakes did they make?"
- "Reference-check the reference — who else worked with them?"
- "If this person hasn't hit their scorecard outcomes in 3 months, what's the plan?"
