# V69 · FIRST THINGS FIRST — Stephen Covey
Tier 4 · Founder Judgment · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The urgent screams louder than the important, and most people spend their lives responding to noise while the few things that actually matter remain undone; the only solution is a weekly discipline of organizing life around roles and priorities before the urgent has a chance to hijack your calendar.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Covey's time management framework moves beyond "put big rocks in first" to a role-based weekly system. The core insight: urgency ≠ importance. The classic 2×2 matrix (urgent/important × not urgent/not important) reveals that Quadrant II — Important but Not Urgent — is where all leverage lives: relationship-building, planning, prevention, learning. The system: identify your key roles (CEO, parent, partner, mentor), define one or two weekly goals per role from Quadrant II, schedule them before any urgent items touch the calendar, and review weekly. The meta-lesson: you can't manage time — you manage yourself within a framework that protects the important from the tyrannical urgent.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The urgency addiction
├─ Urgency creates a dopamine loop: responding to fire feels productive even when it isn't
├─ The urgent-but-not-important quadrant (III) is where most people spend most of their day
├─ The cost: Quadrant II (important but not urgent) is perpetually starved, and those activities are the ONLY ones that compound
└─ LEAF: flag any activity that feels urgent but would not matter if assessed calmly 24 hours later — that's Quadrant III noise

### Branch 2 — Roles as the organizing principle
├─ You are not one person — you operate in multiple roles; each role has distinct responsibilities and needs its own goals
├─ A balanced week is one where at least one goal from each role makes it onto the calendar
├─ Neglecting a role for too long creates a crisis that eventually gets loud enough to force attention — at which point it's a Quadrant I disaster
└─ LEAF: list the founder's current roles — if any role has not produced a scheduled goal in 2 weeks, that neglect is a ticking Quadrant I bomb

### Branch 3 — Weekly Quadrant II scheduling
├─ Plan the week, not the day: daily planning is reactive, weekly planning is proactive
├─ Schedule Quadrant II time blocks FIRST, then let the urgent fill around them
├─ The goal is not more productivity — it's more contribution to what YOU value, not what others demand
└─ LEAF: the founder's calendar should show at least one protected Quadrant II block per role per week before any meeting requests go in

### Branch 4 — Conscience over clock
├─ The inner compass (what matters) must override the clock (what's pressing)
├─ The question is not "how can I do this faster?" but "is this worth doing at all?"
├─ Saying yes to everything = saying no to the few things that matter — by default, not by choice
└─ LEAF: for every new commitment, ask "what Quadrant II activity does this displace?" — if the displacement is in a valued role, decline

### Branch 5 — Integrity in the moment of choice
├─ Between stimulus and response is a space — in that space is your power to choose
├─ The weekly plan is a commitment; honoring it in the moment of temptation is integrity
├─ The test of the system is not how well you plan but how often you execute the plan when it's hard
└─ LEAF: when the urgent bites during a Quadrant II block, pause one breath and ask "does this urgent thing serve a more important role than the one I'm protecting?"

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN the founder's calendar is reactive → APPLY the role-based weekly plan: list roles, assign one Quadrant II goal per role, schedule them first.
- WHEN priorities conflict → APPLY the urgency filter: is this urgent but not important? If yes, defer or delete.
- WHEN a new commitment arrives → APPLY the displacement question: what Quadrant II activity does this replace? Then decide.
- WHEN the founder feels stretched thin → APPLY the role audit: which role has starved for two weeks? Fix it before it becomes a crisis.
- WHEN a fire drill dominates → APPLY the pause test: one breath, one question: "will this matter tomorrow?"
