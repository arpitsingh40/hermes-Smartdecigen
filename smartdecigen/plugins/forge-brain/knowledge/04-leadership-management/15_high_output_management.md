# V15 · HIGH OUTPUT MANAGEMENT — Andrew S. Grove
Tier 4 · Founder Judgment · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
A manager's output is not what they do — it is the output of their organization plus the organizations they influence; therefore the only question that matters about any founder activity is LEVERAGE: how many units of team output does one hour of this produce?

## TRUNK — core thesis
Grove ran Intel like a production system and wrote the operating manual: everything is a PROCESS with a limiting step; find it, fix it, monitor with leading indicators. Managerial leverage comes from a small set of high-multiplier activities — training, decision-making at the right moment, information gathering, and nudges delivered early. Management style must match TASK-RELEVANT MATURITY (how experienced is this person at THIS task — not in general). Meetings are the MEDIUM of management, not a distraction from it. And measurement needs PAIRED indicators — quantity with quality — or the metric gets gamed.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Production thinking: the limiting step
├─ Every value stream (breakfast factory → D2C fulfilment → sales pipeline) has ONE constraining stage that sets total throughput
├─ Build around the limiting step; optimizing non-constraints is invisible work
├─ Fix problems at the LOWEST-VALUE stage: reject bad eggs before boiling, disqualify bad leads before demos
└─ LEAF: map the founder's engine as stages; find the constraint; refuse to optimize anything else until it moves

### Branch 2 — Managerial leverage
├─ High leverage: training (one hour × all future work of the trainee), clear decisions at the decision point, catching problems early, saying no early
├─ Negative leverage: micromanaging (subtracts subordinate output), waffling (blocks everyone downstream), the late no
├─ Delegation without a monitoring system is abdication; check-ins sample QUALITY at low frequency, not control at high frequency
└─ LEAF: audit the founder's week: classify hours by leverage; a solo founder doing low-leverage work IS the company doing low-leverage work

### Branch 3 — Task-relevant maturity (TRM)
├─ Style follows the person's maturity AT THIS TASK: low TRM → structured, precise instruction; medium → mutual dialogue; high → objectives and monitoring only
├─ The best style is not a personality trait; it changes per person per task, and shifts as they grow
└─ LEAF: delegation failures are usually TRM mismatches — freedom given at low TRM, or micromanagement at high TRM; diagnose before blaming the person

### Branch 4 — Meetings and decisions as production tools
├─ One-on-ones: the subordinate's meeting, their agenda; the manager learns problems while they're small (huge leverage)
├─ Decision process: free discussion → clear decision → FULL commitment (disagree and commit); ambiguity after the decision is poison
├─ Peer-plus-one: decisions made by peers stall without a senior tie-breaker in the room
└─ LEAF: for any stalled decision, ask: was discussion truly free? was a decision clearly announced? did dissenters explicitly commit?

### Branch 5 — Indicators: paired, leading, honest
├─ Pair every quantity metric with its quality shadow: orders shipped + error rate; content published + engagement; sales calls + conversion
├─ Leading indicators (pipeline, reorder intent, complaint rate trend) buy reaction time; lagging ones only assign blame
└─ LEAF: when a founder proudly reports one number, ask for its paired shadow before celebrating

### Branch 6 — OKRs (Grove's invention)
├─ Objective = direction with meaning; Key Results = verifiable milestones ("yes/no at quarter end")
├─ Few (focus is the point), cascading loosely (each level's KRs inform the next level's Os), NOT tied mechanically to compensation
└─ LEAF: convert vague founder goals into 1 objective + 2-3 verifiable KRs per quarter, and grade them honestly at the end

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a solo founder is drowning → APPLY leverage audit: classify their activities; identify the training/delegation/no that frees the most output.
- WHEN throughput disappoints → APPLY limiting-step diagnosis: map stages, find the constraint, concentrate there exclusively.
- WHEN a delegation failed → APPLY TRM lens: was the style matched to the person's maturity at this exact task?
- WHEN goals are vague → APPLY OKR conversion: one meaningful objective, 2-3 verifiable key results, review date fixed.
- WHEN a metric improves → APPLY pairing: check the quality shadow before declaring victory.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners

- "Every number needs its shadow: quantity with quality."
- "Free discussion, clear decision, full commitment — in that order, no skipping."
