# V154 · SLICING PIE — Mike Moyer
Tier 3 · Leadership · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The single biggest early-stage mistake: splitting equity at the founding with fixed percentages before anyone knows who will contribute what. Moyer's solution: dynamic equity (the "Grunt Fund") — equity is allocated based on each person's actual contributions (time, money, ideas, relationships, equipment) relative to the total contributions, adjusted continuously over time. Fixed equity splits are a gamble; dynamic splits are fair. If someone leaves early, their equity reflects what they actually contributed, not a guess made at day one.

## TRUNK
The Grunt Fund model: (1) Assign theoretical values to each type of contribution. Time at fair market rate × hours. Cash at 2-4x multiplier (cash is riskier than time). Ideas at a low fixed value. Relationships and equipment at fair market value. (2) As contributions accrue, each person's equity percentage adjusts continuously. (3) The pie is sliced when the company: raises a priced round, achieves sustainable profitability, or hits a predefined milestone. At the slice, contributions stop counting and percentages lock. The result: the person who contributed 70% of the value gets 70%. The person who left after 3 months gets what they actually earned, not the 33% they signed for on day one.

## FRUIT
- WHEN splitting equity with co-founders → APPLY dynamic equity: don't fix percentages now. Track contributions and slice the pie at the funding event.
- WHEN a co-founder leaves early → APPLY the Grunt Fund: what did they actually contribute? That's their fair share, not the fixed percentage.

## SEEDS
- "Fixed equity splits are a gamble based on guesses. Dynamic splits are fair based on facts."
- "The person who contributes 70% of the value should get 70%. Not the 50% they guessed at day one."
- "Slice the pie when you raise money, not when you start."
