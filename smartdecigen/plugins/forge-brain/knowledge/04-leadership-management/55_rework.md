# V55 · REWORK — Jason Fried & David Heinemeier Hansson
Tier 4 · Founder Mindset · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Most conventional business wisdom is backwards: planning is guessing, growth is optional, meetings are toxic, workaholism is a liability, outside funding is a trap, and the best way to build a business is to STAY SMALL, stay profitable, and ignore almost everything you read about "how to build a business."

## TRUNK — core thesis
DHH and Fried (Basecamp/37signals) wrote the anti-business-book: short chapters attacking sacred cows. Planning is guessing — the future is unknowable, so build for now. Growth is not the only path — small is sustainable and beautiful. Meetings are toxic — they're usually monologues with snacks. Learn from the competition but stop WORRYING about them — their success or failure has nothing to do with you. Hiring is the last thing you should do, not the first — use constraints to be creative. Fundamentals: build a half-product (do one thing well), say no by default (every yes is a no to something else), make tiny decisions (big ones are too risky before you have data), and launch NOW (if you're not embarrassed by v1, you launched too late). Remote-first principles: work doesn't have to happen in an office; trust your team; write everything down.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Planning is guessing
├─ Long-term business plans are acts of imagination, not strategy — five-year plans are fiction
├─ Instead: work on what matters NOW, adapt as reality unfolds; "plans are just educated guesses"
├─ Decisions should be made as late as possible — when you have the most information — not locked in early
└─ LEAF: when a founder produces a detailed 12-month plan, ask: what's the ONE thing we KNOW for certain about month 6? If the answer is "nothing," the plan is a security blanket, not a tool — replace it with a rolling 4-6 week horizon

### Branch 2 — Growth is optional
├─ The pressure to grow (hire more, raise more, expand more) is often external: investors, advisors, ego
├─ Small is not just a stage — it's a valid end state: sustainable, profitable, low-stress, resilient
├─ Small means you can change direction quickly, serve customers personally, and not be a hostage to payroll
└─ LEAF: when a founder feels pressure to grow, ask: does this growth serve the business's health or the founder's ego? A profitable 10-person company is not a "failure to scale" — it may be the optimal state

### Branch 3 — Build a half-product
├─ "Go build a half-product that does one thing really well, then launch it and see what happens"
├─ Adding features before anyone uses them is waste — launch with the CORE only, then add based on real demand
├─ Constraints breed creativity: limited resources force focus on what ACTUALLY matters
└─ LEAF: when a founder builds a feature-rich v1, ask: "if you COULD ONLY ship 50% of what's planned, which 50%?" — that's the real product; ship it now, skip the rest until real customers ask for it

### Branch 4 — Say no by default
├─ Every yes is a no to something else — the best companies say no to almost everything
├─ Ideas are cheap: the ability to SAY NO to good ideas (not just bad ones) is what creates focus
├─ If you're not saying no to most things, you don't have priorities — you have a list
└─ LEAF: when a founder has a backlog of ideas/features/projects, apply the ONE-thing test: "which one of these, if done, makes the rest less important?" Focus there; deprioritize everything else

### Branch 5 — Meetings are toxic
├─ Meetings are usually about TALKING about work instead of DOING work
├─ The cost of a meeting is not 1 hour — it's the sum of everyone's hourly cost × number of participants × opportunity cost of the interrupted work
├─ Protocols: no-meeting days, stand-ups with a time limit, meetings only when a decision needs to be made or information needs to be shared — not as the default
└─ LEAF: when a founder's calendar is more meetings than focused work, recommend a "meeting bankruptcy": cancel all recurring meetings for 2 weeks; re-instate only the ones people DEMAND back; the rest are dead

### Branch 6 — Funding is a trap
├─ Outside funding creates an obligation to other people's return expectations, which creates pressure to grow fast, which creates bad decisions
├─ Revenue-funded growth is the only honest signal: if customers won't pay enough to sustain the business, the business doesn't deserve to exist
├─ Constraints (limited money, limited time) force discipline; abundance breeds waste
└─ LEAF: when a founder considers fundraising, ask: "if you couldn't raise money and had to grow on revenue, what would you do?" — that's the real strategy; fundraising should enable that strategy, not replace it

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder builds a long-term plan → APPLY planning-is-guessing: shorten the horizon to 4-6 weeks of certain action, with direction beyond but not detail.
- WHEN a founder feels pressured to grow → APPLY optional-growth reframe: what does optimal look like? A profitable, sustainable size that serves the founder's life goals.
- WHEN a founder is building a feature-rich v1 → APPLY half-product: ship the core 50% that proves the hypothesis; add features only when customers demand them.
- WHEN a founder struggles to say no → APPLY default-no: for every new idea, the answer is no unless there's a compelling reason for yes.
- WHEN a founder considers fundraising → APPLY funding-trap diagnosis: what would the strategy look like if funded entirely by revenue? Is the need for capital real or a fantasy about scale?
