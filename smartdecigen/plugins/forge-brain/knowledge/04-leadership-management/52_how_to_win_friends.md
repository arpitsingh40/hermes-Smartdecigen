# V52 · HOW TO WIN FRIENDS AND INFLUENCE PEOPLE — Dale Carnegie
Tier 5 · Communication · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The deepest human need is to feel IMPORTANT and APPRECIATED — and the single most effective skill in business (and life) is making others feel genuinely valued, not through flattery but through authentic attention, appreciation, and understanding.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Carnegie's 1936 classic sells 30M+ copies because it works: business is fundamentally ABOUT PEOPLE, and getting people to like, trust, and follow you is a LEARNABLE SKILL based on principles, not personality. Don't criticize, condemn, or complain (it triggers defensive reactions). Give honest, sincere appreciation. Become genuinely interested in other people. Smile. Remember names. Be a good listener — encourage others to talk about themselves. Talk in terms of the OTHER person's interests. Make the other person feel important — and do it sincerely. The book's second half covers how to change minds without offense: avoid arguments, respect others' opinions, admit your own mistakes quickly, start on a friendly note, get the other person saying "yes, yes" early, let them do most of the talking, let them feel the idea is theirs, see things from their perspective, be sympathetic, appeal to nobler motives, dramatize your ideas, and throw down a challenge.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Fundamental techniques in handling people
├─ Don't criticize, condemn, or complain: criticism puts people on the defensive and makes them rationalize their behavior; even when you're right, you lose
├─ Give honest, sincere appreciation: everyone craves appreciation; recognition of genuine merit is the only currency that never inflates
├─ Arouse in the other person an eager want: the only way to get someone to do something is to make THEM want to do it — talk in terms of their interests
└─ LEAF: when a founder's team is underperforming, check the criticism-to-appreciation ratio; criticism is the lowest-leverage management tool — replace it with specific appreciation + a shared objective

### Branch 2 — Six ways to make people like you
├─ Become genuinely interested in other people: companies that care about customers get customers back; founders who care about their team get loyalty
├─ Smile: the simplest signal of warmth and approachability
├─ Remember names: a person's name is the sweetest sound in any language — forgetting it signals disinterest
├─ Be a good listener: encourage others to talk about themselves; the person doing the talking is the person learning
├─ Talk in terms of the other person's interests: go to their world before asking them to come to yours
├─ Make the other person feel important — and do it sincerely: the desire to be important is humanity's deepest craving
└─ LEAF: when a founder struggles with investor relationships, customer retention, or team morale, run the six-point checklist: which of these are you neglecting? The answer is usually "listening" or "talking in their terms"

### Branch 3 — How to win people to your way of thinking
├─ The only way to get the best of an argument is to AVOID it: you cannot win an argument — even if you win intellectually, you lose relationship
├─ Show respect for the other person's opinions: never say "you're wrong" — say "I think differently, but let me check my thinking"
├─ If you are wrong, admit it quickly and emphatically: before the other person can criticize you, criticize yourself — disarms opposition completely
├─ Begin in a friendly way: honey catches more flies than vinegar — even when delivering bad news, start from warmth
├─ Get the other person saying "yes" immediately: find common ground first — each "yes" creates momentum toward agreement
└─ LEAF: when a founder faces pushback from investors, co-founders, or key hires, coach them through the persuasion sequence: friendly start → common ground → admit own vulnerability → suggest rather than assert

### Branch 4 — Be a leader: how to change people without offense
├─ Praise every improvement: "be hearty in your approbation and lavish in your praise" — specific, genuine, before-critical
├─ Call attention to mistakes INDIRECTLY: "this is great work — one thing that could make it even better is..."
├─ Talk about your OWN mistakes before criticizing: "I've made this same error before — here's what I learned"
├─ Ask questions instead of giving direct orders: "how do you think we could improve this?" vs "do it this way"
├─ Make the fault easy to correct: build confidence by framing the fix as minor, not character-revealing
├─ Make the other person happy about doing what you suggest: connect the task to their values, their growth, their importance
└─ LEAF: when a founder needs to correct an employee's work, script the conversation using Carnegie's indirect model — praise → own mistake → question → suggestion → connect to their values

### Branch 5 — The Carnegie principles for founders
├─ Persuading investors: talk in terms of THEIR return, not YOUR vision — "here's how this solves a problem you already care about"
├─ Motivating a team: appreciation + challenge — people want recognition AND to be part of something important
├─ Handling difficult customers: listen fully, acknowledge their frustration, find the "yes" ground before solving
├─ Negotiating with partners: see their perspective, admit your own constraints, let them propose the idea
└─ LEAF: Carnegie's principles scale from one-on-one to organizational culture — a founder who practices Carnegie builds a company where customers feel valued and employees feel important

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder is in conflict with a co-founder or team member → APPLY criticism audit: replace criticism with appreciative inquiry and shared goal discovery.
- WHEN a founder struggles with investor relationships → APPLY talk-in-their-terms: reframe the pitch around the investor's interests (return profile, risk, timeline), not the founder's story.
- WHEN a founder loses customers → APPLY listening diagnosis: did you genuinely listen to the customer's needs, or did you talk about your product?
- WHEN a founder needs to change someone's behavior → APPLY indirect correction: praise → own mistake → question → connect to their values.
- WHEN a founder is in an argument → APPLY avoid-win reframe: choose relationship over being right — find the yes-ground, admit your piece first.
