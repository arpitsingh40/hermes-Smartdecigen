# V73 · INDISTRACTABLE — Nir Eyal
Tier 5 · Product Innovation · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Distraction is not a technology problem — it is a pain-management problem: we reach for distraction whenever internal discomfort (boredom, anxiety, uncertainty) arises, and the only lasting solution is to replace the external-avoidance loop with an internal-values-based system that makes it harder to escape discomfort than to do the important thing.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Eyal follows Hooked with the master-of-the-hook framework: how to control attention when every product is competing for it. The model has four steps: (1) MASTER INTERNAL TRIGGERS — distraction always starts with an uncomfortable internal state; identify it, sit with it, and design around it rather than reacting to it. (2) MAKE TIME FOR TRACTION — schedule your values: put the important activities (traction) on the calendar before distractions can claim the time. (3) HACK BACK EXTERNAL TRIGGERS — most notifications, emails, and interruptions are not urgent; eliminate, batch, or delay them systematically. (4) PREVENT DISTRACTION WITH PACTS — use effort (remove the app), price (pay for focus), and identity (declare the behavior publicly) to make distraction harder. The core insight: you can't manage distraction by fighting technology — you manage it by understanding what you're avoiding and designing your environment to make avoidance a choice you have to consciously make, not a default reflex.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Master internal triggers
├─ Distraction is always an escape from an uncomfortable internal state (boredom, stress, uncertainty, loneliness)
├─ The first step: identify what you're feeling when you reach for distraction — the trigger is never the ping, it's the emotion before the ping
├─ The skill: sit with the urge without acting — 10 minutes of discomfort is usually enough to dissolve the impulse
└─ LEAF: when a founder reports "distraction," ask what they're avoiding — the answer is the real problem; the phone is just the tool

### Branch 2 — Make time for traction
├─ Traction = any action that moves you toward your values. Distraction = any action that moves you away.
├─ The solution: schedule your values before the day fills with other people's priorities — time-box your MOST important activities first
├─ If it's not on the calendar, it's not real — the intention to do something valuable without a time block is a wish
└─ LEAF: audit the founder's calendar for last week: how many hours were spent on traction (toward values) vs. distraction (away from values)? The ratio is the diagnosis

### Branch 3 — Hack back external triggers
├─ External triggers are designed by experts to hijack attention; treat them as interruptions until proven otherwise
├─ The fix: batch notifications (not real-time), uninstall apps that don't serve your values, use "do not disturb" by default
├─ The key question for each app: "is this notification serving me or its maker?" — if the answer is the latter, turn it off
└─ LEAF: have the founder turn OFF all non-human notifications for 24 hours — the ones they don't miss are permanently disabled

### Branch 4 — Prevent distraction with pacts
├─ Effort pact: increase friction for distraction (delete the app, log out, use a website blocker)
├─ Price pact: put money on the line (commit to a goal and pay if you miss it)
├─ Identity pact: declare your intention publicly — "I will not check email until noon" — the social cost of breaking it is higher than the pull of the distraction
└─ LEAF: for the ONE most harmful distraction, apply all three pacts simultaneously — the triple constraint is harder to break than any one

### Branch 5 — The distraction audit
├─ Most people don't know how much time they actually waste — the perceived time is always lower than the real time
├─ Use a tracking tool for one week: record EVERY instance of distraction (checking phone, opening social media, browsing)
├─ The data is the intervention: the act of tracking itself reduces the behavior
└─ LEAF: prescribe one week of distraction tracking before any intervention — the founder's own data is more convincing than any theory

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder says "I'm too distracted" → APPLY the internal trigger diagnosis: "what are you avoiding right now?" — address the emotion, not the device.
- WHEN a founder's calendar is reactive → APPLY time-boxing: schedule traction (toward values) in the first 2 hours daily before any meeting requests.
- WHEN notifications interrupt deep work → APPLY the hack-back: batch all notifications to 3x/day; disable everything non-human.
- WHEN willpower fails → APPLY the pact stack: effort + price + identity pacts for the #1 distraction.
- WHEN the founder doesn't believe the distraction data → APPLY the one-week tracker: the numbers will speak.
