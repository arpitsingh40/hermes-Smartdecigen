# V39 · THE LEAN STARTUP + INSPIRED + CONTINUOUS DISCOVERY — Ries, Cagan & Torres
Tier 3 · Product Methodology · Engine: Build-Measure-Learn · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
A startup is a TEMPORARY ORGANIZATION DESIGNED TO SEARCH FOR A REPEATABLE AND SCALABLE BUSINESS MODEL — not a small version of a big company — and the search fails most often because founders BUILD (product) before they KNOW (what customers actually need) and MEASURE (vanity metrics) instead of LEARNING (validated insights about real behavior).

## TRUNK — core thesis
Three canon works fused: THE LEAN STARTUP (Ries) gives the core loop: BUILD-MEASURE-LEARN, Minimum Viable Product (MVP) to test riskiest hypotheses, and innovation accounting to separate progress from noise. INSPIRED (Cagan) gives the product-team operating model: empowered product teams with problem spaces and solution spaces, continuous discovery (weekly customer contact), and the distinction between output (features shipped) and outcome (behavior changes). CONTINUOUS DISCOVERY HABITS (Torres) gives the weekly cadence: 4 questions per week (What is the most important thing to learn? Who do we talk to? What do we show them? What did we learn?), structured interviews (Opportunity Solution Trees), and the habit of making discovery a daily team practice. Together: Ries provides the WHY and THE BIG LOOP (management accountability); Cagan provides the TEAM STRUCTURE (product manager as discovery lead, not backlog administrator); Torres provides the WEEKLY RITUAL (what to do every Tuesday).

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Build-Measure-Learn and the MVP
├─ The MVP is the SMALLEST experiment that tests the riskiest assumption — it is NOT a half-assed product; it's a fast test of the hypothesis that must be true for the business to work
├─ Vanity metrics (total users, page views) hide the truth; actionable metrics (cohort retention, conversion-to-value) reveal it
├─ Innovation accounting: baseline (current metric) → tuned (experiment) → pivot or persevere decision
└─ LEAF: before building any feature, ask: 'what is the riskiest assumption this feature tests? could we test it cheaper?' — if yes, the feature is gold-plating

### Branch 2 — Validated learning over shipped features
├─ The purpose of a startup is not to ship features — it's to LEARN what creates value
├─ A feature that shipped but didn't move the metric is WASTE (not 'investment'); learning why it didn't work is the only way to salvage the time
├─ Pivot (change the hypothesis) or Persevere (continue testing) — the pivot is not failure; it's a strategic course correction with data
└─ LEAF: institute the 'pivot-or-persevere' meeting at a regular cadence; if the board meeting is the first time you ask, you're 3 months late

### Branch 3 — Empowered product teams (Cagan)
├─ Empowered teams: given a problem space (outcome) and trusted to find the solution; NOT feature factories that take requirements from above
├─ Product manager is responsible for discovery (what's valuable, usable, feasible); designer for usability; engineers for feasibility
├─ Product teams must talk to customers weekly — no exceptions. 'Customer contact' is not delegated to researchers
└─ LEAF: if the founder treats product as 'write specs for engineers', the team is a feature factory; flip to 'here's the outcome we need' and let the team discover the solution

### Branch 4 — Continuous discovery habits (Torres)
├─ Weekly cadence: 4 questions → what to learn, who to talk to, what to show, what we learned
├─ Opportunity Solution Tree: desired outcome → opportunities (problems/desires to address) → solutions (ideas to test) → experiments (tests of assumptions)
├─ The tree prevents solution-jumping: test the opportunity before the solution; most teams optimize solutions for problems they haven't validated
└─ LEAF: create an Opportunity Solution Tree for the current quarter; every new feature request is a solution — put it on the tree, identify the opportunity it serves, and test the opportunity first

### Branch 5 — Output vs outcome
├─ OUTPUT: features shipped, stories completed, velocity maintained — easy to measure, often irrelevant
├─ OUTCOME: customer behavior change — harder to measure, always the point
├─ A feature that doesn't change behavior is noise; the product manager's job is to say no to noise
└─ LEAF: every quarterly planning session starts with 'what behavior change are we targeting this quarter?' — if the answer is 'ship X feature', the team hasn't defined the outcome

### Branch 6 — The pivot spectrum
├─ Zoom-in pivot: one feature becomes the whole product
├─ Zoom-out pivot: what was a feature becomes insufficient — the whole product is needed
├─ Customer segment pivot: the product solves a real problem for a different segment
├─ Platform pivot: application → platform, or vice versa
├─ Business architecture pivot: high-margin low-volume → low-margin high-volume
├─ Value capture pivot: how you make money changes (freemium → subscription → marketplace commission)
└─ LEAF: label the pivot type explicitly — 'zoom-in pivot to enterprise' is clearer and faster than 'we're also going after large accounts'

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder is building features without measuring their impact → APPLY innovation accounting: define the ONE metric that proves or disproves the hypothesis; build only what moves it
- WHEN a founder 'knows' what customers want → APPLY continuous discovery: enforce 4 customer conversations/week (Torres); let the evidence speak
- WHEN the product team is a feature factory → APPLY empowered teams: redefine the team's charter as 'outcome X, budget Y, constraints Z' — no feature specs from above
- WHEN a founder says 'we need to ship faster' → APPLY output/outcome audit: is shipping faster actually changing customer behavior? If not, speed is waste
- WHEN a pivot is being discussed emotionally → APPLY pivot spectrum: name the pivot type; the label depersonalizes the decision
