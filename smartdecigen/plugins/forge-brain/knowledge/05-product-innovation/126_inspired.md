# V126 · INSPIRED — Marty Cagan
Tier 3 · Product · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Most product teams are not empowered to solve problems — they're feature factories implementing stakeholder requests. Cagan (former SVP of Product at eBay, Netscape, AOL) defines what GREAT product teams do differently: they are given problems to solve, not features to build. They have the skills and authority to discover solutions. They are measured on outcomes, not output. The distinction between "feature team" (you get a roadmap of features to build) and "empowered product team" (you get a business problem and are accountable for the outcome) is the difference between mediocrity and excellence.

## TRUNK
Four types of risk for every product initiative: Value (will people buy/use it?), Usability (can they figure out how?), Feasibility (can we build it with the time/skills/tech we have?), and Viability (does it work for the business? legal, ethical, sales channel, finance). Product discovery runs in parallel with delivery — discovery determines WHAT to build; delivery builds it. The product manager is responsible for value and viability. The designer for usability. The tech lead for feasibility. The team collaborates intensively on discovery. The prototype is the primary discovery tool — test ideas with real users before writing a line of code. Product strategy emerges from product vision and the insights generated by product discovery.

## BRANCHES
- Good product teams vs bad: good teams are measured on business outcomes (retention, revenue, NPS); bad teams on output (features shipped, story points). Good teams prototype and test; bad teams hold meetings to decide what to build.
- Product discovery techniques: story maps, customer interviews, usability testing, A/B testing, analytics, and prototypes. Discovery is continuous, not a phase.
- Roadmaps: output-based roadmaps ("release feature X in Q2") are commitments you can't keep. OKR-based roadmaps ("improve retention by 15%") give teams problems to solve.
- Product manager: not the CEO of the product. The product manager is responsible for value and viability — deep knowledge of the customer, the data, the business, and the market.
- Product designer: responsible for the holistic experience, not just the UI. Usability, but also the conceptual design — how users think about the product.
- Tech lead: responsible for feasibility, but also a co-creator of the solution. Engineers who only code from a spec are underutilized.

## FRUIT
- WHEN building a roadmap → APPLY outcome-vs-output: does the roadmap describe business problems or feature lists?
- WHEN a feature idea comes from the CEO → APPLY discovery discipline: "great idea — let's prototype it and test with 5 users before we build."
- WHEN the team feels like a feature factory → APPLY empowerment check: are they given problems or solutions? Are they measured on outcomes or output?
- WHEN prioritizing → APPLY the 4 risks: which risk is highest for this idea — value, usability, feasibility, or viability? Prioritize de-risking the biggest one.

## SEEDS
- "Empowered product teams get problems to solve, not features to build."
- "Your roadmap should describe outcomes, not features."
- "Prototype before you build. Test before you commit."
- "Feature teams ship features. Product teams solve problems."
- "The biggest risk in most products is value: will anyone use it?"
