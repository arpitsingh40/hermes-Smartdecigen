# V35 · CROSSING THE CHASM — Geoffrey Moore
Tier 3 · Technology Adoption · Engine: Go-to-Market · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The Technology Adoption Life Cycle has a CHASM between the EARLY ADOPTERS (visionaries who buy the potential) and the EARLY MAJORITY (pragmatists who buy the complete solution) — and most startups die in that chasm because they keep selling to visionaries on the left of the gap, who cannot provide the reference-ability and volume the pragmatists on the right demand.

## TRUNK — core thesis
To cross the chasm, a startup must pick a SINGLE beachhead market segment, deliver a WHOLE PRODUCT (not just the core technology but all the ancillary pieces — support, integration, training, ecosystem), create a COMPELLING REASON TO BUY that targets one core use-case, and build a POSITION within that niche that becomes the natural reference. Once the niche is dominated, the startup can BOWLING PIN into adjacent segments (the tiniest segment that reduces risk for the most adjacent segment). Abandon the visionaries who love the 'potential' — they distract from the product features pragmatists care about. Think: 'what is the smallest market I can dominate completely within 12 months?'

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The adoption life cycle and the chasm
├─ Innovators (tech enthusiasts) → Early Adopters (visionaries) → CHASM → Early Majority (pragmatists) → Late Majority (conservatives) → Laggards (skeptics)
├─ Visionaries: buy the concept, the potential, the new paradigm — they don't need references, they want to be first
├─ Pragmatists: buy the proven solution, the complete product, the market leader — they need references from people like them
└─ LEAF: if the founder's customers all say 'I love the vision', the founder is trapped before the chasm — the goal is to make the next customer say 'I love that it works'

### Branch 2 — The beachhead segment
├─ Pick ONE narrow market where you can dominate before anyone notices: not 'SMEs in India' but 'B2B accounting firms in Mumbai with 10-50 employees'
├─ Criteria: segment has a compelling unsolved problem, budget, willing reference, and low integration complexity
├─ Domination in a niche → reference stories → credibility for the next segment
└─ LEAF: the beachhead is the smallest segment that, if won, reduces risk for the most adjacent segments; pick the bowling pin that knocks down the next

### Branch 3 — Whole product: the invisible 90%
├─ The core product is what you ship; the whole product includes documentation, onboarding, support, integrations, training, service-level agreements
├─ Pragmatists buy the whole product — they don't care about 'great API'; they care about 'can my non-technical team use it on Monday?'
├─ Most startup failure in the chasm is not product failure — it's whole-product failure
└─ LEAF: ask the customer 'what else do you need to go live with this?' — the answer defines the whole product gap; fill that gap before scaling

### Branch 4 — Positioning: the competitor as reference
├─ In the chasm, you are compared to the DO-NOTHING option (the status quo) or the DO-IT-YOURSELF option (spreadsheets, internal tools)
├─ Positioning is not about your product's features; it's about creating a space in the buyer's mind that only you occupy
├─ The elevator pitch: FOR [target segment] WHO [compelling reason to buy], OUR PRODUCT IS [category] THAT [key benefit]. UNLIKE [competitor/alternative], WE [key differentiator].
└─ LEAF: write one positioning statement per beachhead; if it fits any other segment, it's too generic

### Branch 5 — Bowling pin strategy: after the chasm
├─ After dominating beachhead #1, the next market is not the biggest adjacent — it's the one that can be won using the references from #1
├─ Each pin (segment) opens the next one; the trajectory is narrow → wider, not small → big
├─ Bowling pin sequence: pin 1 = initial beachhead; pin 2 = most related segment; pin 3 = broader application
└─ LEAF: draw the bowling pin sequence before crossing the chasm — know which pin 2 is before pin 1 is won

### Branch 6 — The D-day strategy: single target, overwhelming force
├─ Crossing the chasm requires ALL resources focused on ONE segment — no secondary initiatives, no spin-off products, no international expansion
├─ The 'Main Street' phase (after chasm crossing) is where you expand; the chasm-crossing phase is about CONCENTRATION
└─ LEAF: if the founder has more than one segment in the current plan, the plan is wrong — pick one

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a startup has early revenue but can't scale → APPLY chasm diagnosis: are the customers visionaries or pragmatists? If visionaries, the chasm is ahead, not behind
- WHEN go-to-market is scattered across segments → APPLY beachhead selection: enforce the single-segment rule; everything else is noise
- WHEN customers evaluate but don't buy → APPLY whole product audit: what is missing in the customer's 'go-live' chain? Support? Integration? Training? Fill that gap
- WHEN positioning is unclear → APPLY positioning template: FOR [X] WHO [Y], OUR PRODUCT IS [Z]. Write per segment; if two segments share a statement, the beachhead is wrong
- WHEN growth slows after initial traction → APPLY bowling pin review: which adjacent segment buys with the references from the current one? That's the next pin
