# V74 · SPRINT — Jake Knapp
Tier 5 · Product Innovation · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The fastest way to solve big problems is not months of analysis followed by months of building — it's a compressed five-day process that forces a team to prototype an idea and test it with real customers in a single week, turning guesswork into evidence before anyone writes a line of production code.

## TRUNK — core thesis
The Google Ventures Sprint process compresses months of product development into five days: MONDAY — MAP: define the problem, choose the target area, and map the journey the user takes. TUESDAY — SKETCH: diverge into individual solution sketches — no group brainstorming, everyone works alone on paper. WEDNESDAY — DECIDE: converge on the best solution through structured critique and voting; build a storyboard of the winning prototype. THURSDAY — PROTOTYPE: build a realistic facade (not a real product) in one day — the goal is to look real enough to elicit genuine customer reactions. FRIDAY — TEST: show the prototype to five real target customers in one-on-one interviews; observe what works, what confuses, and whether they'd actually use it. The core insight: five customers are enough — the first five will reveal most of what matters; more than five has diminishing returns. The sprint is not about building the product — it's about testing the most important question in the fastest possible way.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Monday: map the problem
├─ Start with the end goal: what question do we need to answer by Friday?
├─ Map the customer journey from start to finish — identify the critical moment (the "target") that the sprint will focus on
├─ The key constraint: choose ONE target — a sprint that tries to solve everything solves nothing
└─ LEAF: before the sprint starts, write the ONE question it must answer; if the sprint doesn't answer it, the sprint failed

### Branch 2 — Tuesday: sketch solutions (alone)
├─ No group brainstorming — groupthink produces average ideas; individual preparation produces better ones
├─ Each person creates a four-step sketch: notes, ideas, crazy 8s (8 variations in 8 minutes), and a solution sketch (one detailed concept)
├─ The constraint: drawings on paper, not slides or documents — the medium forces clarity
└─ LEAF: when the founder needs a breakthrough, use the Tuesday structure: individual silent work, NOT a whiteboard free-for-all

### Branch 3 — Wednesday: decide and storyboard
├─ Structured critique: each sketch is displayed, discussed, and voted on (dot voting + "supervoter" = the decider)
├─ The outcome: ONE winning solution (or a mashup of the best parts) with a detailed step-by-step storyboard
├─ The decision is final: Thursday is for building, not second-guessing the Wednesday choice
└─ LEAF: the founder appoints ONE supervoter who makes the final call — design by committee kills the sprint's speed advantage

### Branch 4 — Thursday: prototype (fake it)
├─ Build a facade: the prototype should LOOK real but be shallow — no real backend, no production code, just enough surface to test
├─ Tools: Keynote, InVision, Figma, or even paper — the goal is speed, not craftsmanship
├─ The "prototype mindset": this is a costume, not a building — it will be discarded on Friday
└─ LEAF: set a hard deadline of 3pm Thursday for prototype completion — the test needs a dry run and time to debrief

### Branch 5 — Friday: test with five customers
├─ Five one-on-one interviews, 30-60 minutes each, with real target customers recruited beforehand
├─ The pattern: watch them interact with the prototype without guiding them — their behavior reveals what the team's assumptions missed
├─ The output is not a yes/no answer — it's observation patterns: what confused them? What excited them? What did they not notice?
└─ LEAF: recruit the testers BEFORE the sprint starts — if there's nobody to test with on Friday, the sprint loses its purpose

### Branch 6 — The five-customer rule
├─ The first five customers reveal ~85% of the major usability issues; customer 6-15 reveal progressively less new information
├─ The goal is not statistical significance — it's learning the biggest blind spots fast
├─ If the first five customer interviews all show the same pattern, the team knows enough to decide
└─ LEAF: never test with more than five per round — test, learn, iterate, then test again with five more; speed is the point

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a product idea is uncertain → APPLY the Sprint framework: compress validation to one week before committing to build.
- WHEN a team debates direction → APPLY the Tuesday-Wednesday pattern: individual sketches, structured critique, one decider.
- WHEN a founder wants faster customer feedback → APPLY the five-customer rule: recruit five, test this week, learn the key patterns.
- WHEN a product team builds too much before testing → APPLY the Thursday prototype constraint: build the minimum facade that looks real enough to test.
- WHEN a decision process is slow → APPLY the Sprint's one-question discipline: what is the single question we need answered by Friday?

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "A week is long enough to answer a question that would take months to guess about."
- "Five customers are enough. Five customers don't lie."
- "A prototype is a costume, not a building. Throw it away on Friday."
- "Group brainstorming is a trap. Sketch alone, then critique together."
- "The sprint answers ONE question. If you're trying to answer more, you need more sprints."
