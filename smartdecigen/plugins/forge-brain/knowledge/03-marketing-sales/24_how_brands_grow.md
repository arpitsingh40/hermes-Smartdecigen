# V24 · HOW BRANDS GROW — Byron Sharp
Tier 1 · Brand Strategy · Engine: Growth Validation · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Brands grow overwhelmingly by PENETRATION — recruiting more buyers, mostly light and non-buyers — not by deepening loyalty; and buyers choose whatever is mentally and physically AVAILABLE in the buying moment, which makes distinctiveness (being instantly recognizable) more valuable than differentiation (being meaningfully different).

## TRUNK — core thesis
Sharp's laws come from decades of panel data across categories and countries, and they demolish romantic marketing: Double Jeopardy (small brands suffer twice — fewer buyers AND slightly lower loyalty; loyalty is a FUNCTION of size, not a lever); the Pareto law is really ~50/20 not 80/20 (light buyers matter enormously); user bases of rival brands look nearly identical (no magic segments); loyalty programs mostly enroll existing heavy buyers; advertising works not by persuasion but by building and refreshing MEMORY STRUCTURES that surface at buying moments. Growth strategy therefore = maximize mental availability (distinctive assets linked to many category entry points) + physical availability (easy to find, easy to buy, everywhere the category is bought).

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Double jeopardy & the penetration law
├─ Across virtually all categories: market share differences are driven by penetration differences; loyalty metrics move in a narrow band tied to share
├─ Implication: 'grow by making existing customers more loyal' is largely a mathematical dead end for small brands
└─ LEAF: when a founder plans growth via retention/loyalty programs alone, show the law: reach new buyers or stay small

### Branch 2 — Light buyers are the growth engine
├─ The heaviest 20% deliver ~50% of sales (not 80); the long tail of light/occasional buyers is where growth hides
├─ Light buyers barely think about you — which is WHY mass reach and mental availability matter more than deep engagement
└─ LEAF: marketing that only speaks to fans (community, advanced content) preaches to the converted; check reach math first

### Branch 3 — Mental availability & category entry points (CEPs)
├─ Buyers don't carry brand rankings; they retrieve whatever comes to mind in a buying SITUATION (kids' tiffin, office snack, gift, diet, travel)
├─ Mental availability = the breadth and freshness of links between the brand and many CEPs
├─ Building it: consistent distinctive assets + messaging tied to concrete situations, refreshed continuously
└─ LEAF: inventory the founder's category entry points; the brand should own links to MORE situations, not deeper links to one

### Branch 4 — Distinctiveness beats differentiation
├─ Meaningful differentiation is rare, quickly copied, and buyers barely perceive it; what endures is being INSTANTLY RECOGNIZABLE
├─ Distinctive brand assets: colors, logo, pack shape, character, tagline, sound — built through ruthless consistency over years
├─ Changing look/voice each campaign resets the memory bank to zero
└─ LEAF: protect the founder's distinctive assets from their own boredom — the audience notices consistency long after the founder is sick of it

### Branch 5 — Physical availability
├─ Be easy to find and buy wherever the category is bought: channels, shelf presence, delivery apps, payment options
├─ Availability gaps silently cap growth regardless of brand love
└─ LEAF: before spending on ads, audit: can every triggered buyer actually FIND and BUY within their habit path?

### Branch 6 — How advertising actually works
├─ Mostly not persuasion: it refreshes memory structures so the brand surfaces at buying moments; emotional, branded, reach-first
├─ Continuous presence beats bursts (memory decays); share of voice ≥ share of market to grow
└─ LEAF: small budgets should buy CONTINUITY and branding clarity, not sporadic brilliance nobody attributes

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN growth strategy leans on loyalty/retention of a small base → APPLY penetration law: redirect toward reaching new and light buyers; loyalty follows size.
- WHEN a founder targets a hyper-narrow 'ideal segment' for growth → APPLY user-base overlap: rival buyers look alike; buy reach across the category, refine message later.
- WHEN brand assets keep changing → APPLY distinctiveness audit: freeze colors/logo/tagline; consistency is the compounding asset.
- WHEN sales lag despite awareness → APPLY availability check: mental (which buying situations trigger recall?) and physical (can they buy where they already shop?).
- WHEN evaluating marketing spend → APPLY memory-structure lens: does this refresh brand-situation links at reach, or entertain the already-loyal?
