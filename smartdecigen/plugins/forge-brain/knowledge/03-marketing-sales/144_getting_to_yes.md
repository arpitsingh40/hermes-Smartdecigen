# V144 · GETTING TO YES — Roger Fisher & William Ury
Tier 3 · Sales · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The single most influential negotiation book ever written. Fisher & Ury's Harvard Negotiation Project framework: (1) Separate the people from the problem. (2) Focus on interests, not positions. (3) Invent options for mutual gain. (4) Insist on objective criteria. The cornerstone concept: BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement) — your power in any negotiation is your ability to walk away. A weak BATNA means you'll accept a bad deal. A strong BATNA means you can demand a good one. Never enter a negotiation without knowing your BATNA — and improving it before you sit down.

## TRUNK
Positional bargaining (I want X, you want Y, we meet in the middle) is the default — and it's terrible. It produces unwise agreements (splitting the difference on a bad deal is still a bad deal), is inefficient (extreme opening positions → slow concessions), and damages relationships. Principled negotiation: (1) People: be soft on the person, hard on the problem. Negotiate side-by-side against the problem, not face-to-face. (2) Interests: ask WHY they want what they want. Behind every position is an interest. (3) Options: separate inventing from deciding. Brainstorm without criticism first. Then evaluate. (4) Criteria: objective standards (market value, precedent, expert opinion, law). Frame as "what's fair" not "what I want." BATNA: know your walk-away and improve it. The better your BATNA, the stronger your position.

## FRUIT
- WHEN negotiating anything → APPLY BATNA first: what's your alternative if this deal fails? What's theirs? Whose is stronger?
- WHEN stuck on positions → APPLY interest exploration: "help me understand why that's important to you."
- WHEN the other side won't budge → APPLY objective criteria: "what standard should we use to decide what's fair?"

## SEEDS
- "Your power in any negotiation is your ability to walk away. Know your BATNA."
- "Negotiate on interests, not positions. Why do you want what you want?"
- "Separate the people from the problem. Negotiate side-by-side against the problem."
- "Insist on objective criteria. 'Fair' beats 'I want.'"
