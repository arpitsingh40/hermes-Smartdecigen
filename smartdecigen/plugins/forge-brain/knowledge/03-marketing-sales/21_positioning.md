# V21 · POSITIONING: THE BATTLE FOR YOUR MIND — Al Ries & Jack Trout
Tier 1 · Brand Strategy · Engine: Brand Positioning · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Positioning is not something you do to a product — it is what you do to the MIND of the prospect: in an overcommunicated society the only reliable strategy is to own a simple word or slot in memory, because minds accept only what matches their existing state and reject everything else.

## TRUNK — core thesis
The consumer mind is the battlefield and it is drastically limited: it hates confusion, holds tiny ladders of ~7 brands per category, and once made up, almost never changes. Therefore being FIRST in the mind beats being better in the product — people remember the first, not the best. If you cannot be first in a category, create a new category you CAN be first in, or position yourself AGAINST the leader by occupying the empty slot they cannot take. Marketing failures are almost always positioning failures: trying to be everything to everybody, extending a name across categories until it stands for nothing, or attacking a leader head-on at their strongest point.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The overcommunicated mind
├─ Defense mechanism: minds filter out everything that doesn't match prior beliefs; messages don't enter, they're admitted
├─ The only entry: oversimplified, sharpened messages that attach to something already in the mind
└─ LEAF: a founder's pitch that needs a paragraph has already lost; find the one sentence that hooks an existing belief

### Branch 2 — First beats better
├─ The first brand in the mind gets ~2x the long-run share of #2; people learn the first and use it as the category's name
├─ Being first in the MARKET matters only because it usually makes you first in the MIND — the mind is the prize
└─ LEAF: ask not "is my product better?" but "what am I FIRST at, in whose mind?"

### Branch 3 — Cherchez le creneau (find the hole)
├─ The leader owns the main slot; challengers must find the empty position: size (VW Beetle: 'Think small'), price (premium or budget), sex/age, timing, distribution
├─ Against-positioning: Avis 'We're No. 2, we try harder' — uses the leader's position as the hook for your own
├─ Repositioning the competition: plant doubt about the incumbent's slot (Tylenol vs aspirin: 'for the millions who should not take aspirin')
└─ LEAF: map the mental slots in the founder's category; the strategy IS the unoccupied slot, not the product spec sheet

### Branch 4 — The line-extension trap
├─ Putting a proven name on a new category product borrows short-term recognition and destroys long-term meaning (the name comes to stand for nothing)
├─ A name is a rubber band: stretch it and it weakens; the mind keeps one meaning per name
└─ LEAF: when a founder wants to launch product #2 under brand #1, ask what word brand #1 owns and whether #2 dilutes it — usually a new name wins

### Branch 5 — You cannot change minds
├─ Repositioning YOURSELF in a made-up mind is nearly impossible; work with existing perceptions, redirect them
├─ The mind has no room for the new and different unless it's related to the old
└─ LEAF: position new things as 'the X of Y' (link to existing schema) rather than educating from zero

### Branch 6 — Sacrifice and focus
├─ Owning a position requires giving up everything that contradicts it — the anti-instinct move founders resist
└─ LEAF: the positioning test: what will this brand deliberately NOT be, NOT serve, NOT do?

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder describes their product with a list of features/audiences → APPLY slot search: what one word or slot can they own first in a definable mind, and what must they sacrifice to own it?
- WHEN they face a dominant leader → APPLY against-positioning: find the leader's inherent weakness (the strength's flip side) and occupy the opposite slot; never attack the strength head-on.
- WHEN they want to stretch one brand across products → APPLY line-extension audit: what word does the name own now, and does the extension dilute it?
- WHEN prospects 'don't get it' → APPLY schema hook: reframe as 'the X of Y' connecting to what's already in the mind.
- WHEN choosing a market entry → APPLY category-creation check: can they define a narrower category where they are honestly FIRST?

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "What one word do you own in the customer's mind? If it's a sentence, you own nothing."
- "First in the mind beats better on the spec sheet."
- "Can't be first? Create the category where you are."
- "What will you deliberately NOT be? No sacrifice, no position."
- "Don't educate an empty mind; hook a full one."
