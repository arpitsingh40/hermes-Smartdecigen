# V32 · NEVER SPLIT THE DIFFERENCE — Chris Voss
Tier 3 · Negotiation · Engine: Deal-Making · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Negotiation is not a rational exchange of offers — it is an emotional, psychological confrontation driven by the other person's need to feel HEARD, SAFE, and in CONTROL; the most powerful tool is not logic but EMPATHY strategically deployed (tactical empathy), and the fastest way to lose is to concede or split the difference.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Voss, former FBI hostage negotiator, argues that the bargaining-table rules in business are wrong: reason doesn't work on an irrational opponent (and all opponents are irrational in negotiation). The solution is tactical empathy — active listening techniques that make the other side feel understood, which lowers their amygdala-driven resistance and opens the door to real problem-solving. Core tools: LABELING (name the emotion — 'it sounds like you're frustrated with pricing' — to defuse it); MIRRORING (repeat the last 1-3 words — pauses, silence does the work); ACCUSATION AUDIT (list every worst thing they could say about you before they say it, exhausting the list defangs the negativity); CALIBRATED QUESTIONS (how/what questions that force the other side to solve your problem — 'how am I supposed to do that?'); the RULE OF THREE (get the same agreement three different ways to test commitment); the PINK GLOVE (the one unreasonable demand you won't bend on). Never split the difference — the split is the lose-lose outcome that both sides accept because they don't know how to do better.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The voice: late-night FM DJ
├─ A calm, slow, downward-inflected voice triggers safety; the assertive/authoritative voice triggers amygdala (fight/flight)
├─ The listener's brain syncs to the talker's voice; if you're tense, they resist
└─ LEAF: before any difficult negotiation call, consciously set to late-night FM DJ — record 30 seconds and check if you'd trust that voice

### Branch 2 — Mirroring and labeling
├─ Mirror: repeat the last 1-3 words (or key word) with an upward inflection — the silence after the mirror forces them to elaborate
├─ Label: 'it sounds like ___' / 'it seems like ___' — naming the emotion drains its power and builds trust
└─ LEAF: in the first 2 minutes of any high-stakes conversation, mirror the first statement and label the first emotion — you'll watch the shoulders drop

### Branch 3 — The accusation audit
├─ List every negative thing the other side could say about you, your product, or your offer
├─ Deliver them at the start — 'you're probably thinking our pricing is insane, our support is mediocre, and switching costs will kill you'
├─ Exhausting the negativity makes the rest of the conversation constructive; the listener corrects you instead of attacking
└─ LEAF: in the first pitch to a skeptical prospect, spend 60 seconds on the accusation audit — the trust gain is worth more than the 60 seconds of positive talk it replaces

### Branch 4 — Calibrated questions and the rule of three
├─ 'How' and 'what' questions force the other side to engage their problem-solving brain: 'how am I supposed to do that?' (forces them to find a solution), 'what's the biggest challenge you see?'
├─ 'Why' triggers defensiveness — replace every 'why' with a 'how' or 'what'
├─ The Rule of Three: get agreement to terms, then ask two more calibrating questions; if the answer stays consistent, the agreement is real
└─ LEAF: ban 'why' from negotiation vocabulary; replace with 'what' that invites collaboration — 'what makes you say that?' instead of 'why do you think that?'

### Branch 5 — The Ackerman model (Bargaining)
├─ Set a target price (your goal). Set a first offer at 65% of target. Then three raises: 85%, 95%, 100%.
├─ Each raise is smaller and delivered with increasing reluctance — the final 'can't go any higher' with a pause signals the limit
├─ Precise numbers ($67,423) beat round numbers ($67,000) — precision signals calculation, not approximation
└─ LEAF: the founder's biggest negotiation mistake is showing eagerness; the Ackerman sequence turns eagerness into reluctant concession

### Branch 6 — Black swans
├─ In every negotiation there is something the other side values that they haven't revealed — the Black Swan that changes the deal
├─ Find it by asking what's behind the 'no': the reason they can't is often the key to unlocking a different yes
└─ LEAF: when a deal is stuck, the missing piece is almost always an unstated constraint or a hidden stakeholder — ask 'what's the real obstacle?'

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder faces price pushback → APPLY calibrated questions: 'how am I supposed to hit our numbers at that price?' — forces the buyer to solve for your constraints
- WHEN a negotiation stalls → APPLY accusation audit: list everything the other side is thinking but not saying; the relief of naming it unsticks the conversation
- WHEN an investor is skeptical → APPLY labeling: 'it sounds like you're worried about market timing' — the label makes the concern feel heard, not challenged
- WHEN a founder seems to concede too easily → APPLY Ackerman model: pre-calc the 65/85/95/100 sequence; precision builds value
- WHEN a team conversation turns defensive → APPLY mirroring: repeat the last 2 words, wait — the person rewrites their own argument in better terms
