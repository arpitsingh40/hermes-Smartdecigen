# V88 · THE TIPPING POINT — Malcolm Gladwell
Tier 2 · Social Dynamics · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Ideas, products, and behaviors spread like epidemics — they tip from invisible to pervasive not through gradual acceptance but through sudden, contagious explosion driven by a small number of exceptional people and subtle changes in context.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Gladwell identifies three rules of epidemics: THE LAW OF THE FEW (a tiny number of people — Connectors, Mavens, and Salesmen — drive the spread; the rest just follow), THE STICKINESS FACTOR (the message itself must be memorable and compelling enough to trigger action — small tweaks can make a message vastly stickier), and THE POWER OF CONTEXT (environmental conditions — the Broken Windows theory, group size, peer pressure — dramatically influence whether an idea catches or dies). For founders, the framework is a growth model that doesn't depend on mass advertising: find the few influencers in your niche who have the social reach (Connectors), the credibility and knowledge (Mavens), and the persuasion (Salesmen) to spread your idea; craft your message until it sticks (focus group and iterate until recall/action rates hit a threshold); and engineer the context — scarcity, social proof, peer visibility — so that conditions are ripe for a tip.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The Law of the Few: Connectors, Mavens, Salesmen
├─ Connectors: people with vast social networks spanning multiple worlds — they know "everyone"
├─ Mavens: information specialists who accumulate and share knowledge — they're trusted because they genuinely know their domain
├─ Salesmen: natural persuaders with charisma, energy, and a knack for non-verbal synchronization
├─ LEAF: don't target "influencers" (broad follower count); find the intersection of Connector + Maven in your specific category — someone who knows everyone AND knows the product deeply
└─ LEAF: the three types often overlap; a single person can be all three (the ideal early adopter)

### Branch 2 — The Stickiness Factor: content design
├─ Sesame Street discovered that small changes (keeping segments short, adding previews, interspersing ads with content) dramatically increased learning
├─ Blue's Shoes (boots for construction workers): simple, concrete message + vivid color + extreme pain point → extraordinary recall
└─ LEAF: test the stickiness of the founder's core message: after hearing it once, can people recall it 24 hours later? If not, it's not sticky — simplify, concretize, make it emotional

### Branch 3 — The Power of Context: environment rules
├─ Broken Windows theory: small contextual signals (broken windows, graffiti) dramatically increase crime rates; fixing the context reduced crime in NYC without massive policing
├─ The Rule of 150: group dynamics change fundamentally above 150 people (Dunbar's number); communities that stay below 150 keep the epidemic spread alive
├─ LEAF: audit the purchase context: is the environment telling people to buy or to hesitate? A cluttered store, confusing website, complicated checkout — each is a "broken window" that kills tipping
└─ LEAF: for early adoption, keep the community below 150 (or design sub-150 groups within it) to maintain the personal connection that drives spread

### Branch 4 — The tipping point threshold
├─ Every epidemic has a threshold; below it, the idea is invisible; above it, it takes off
├─ The threshold is determined by: the number of infectious people (Connectors/Mavens/Salesmen actively spreading), the stickiness of the message, the favorability of context
├─ LEAF: instead of asking "how do we get 100,000 users?" ask "what's the smallest user base that triggers organic, self-sustaining growth, and how do we get THERE?"
└─ LEAF: at the tipping point, growth shifts from linear (your effort) to exponential (network effect + word of mouth); recognize when you're approaching it and overload support

### Branch 5 — Translation of the message
├─ Gladwell notes that the pure idea rarely tips; it must be TRANSLATED into terms each community accepts
├─ The same product needs different messengers and message frames for different subcultures
└─ LEAF: identify the 2-3 distinct sub-communities within the SVM and custom-fit the message AND the messenger for each

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN the founder wonders why growth is slow despite effort → APPLY Three Rules diagnosis: "do you have Connectors, Mavens, and Salesmen spreading your message? Is the message sticky? Is the context right?"
- WHEN the founder wants to spend on influencer marketing → APPLY Law of the Few: "are these influencers Connectors, Mavens, or Salesmen in YOUR category? Never hire an influencer who isn't a genuine Maven in your space."
- WHEN the founder's message isn't getting remembered → APPLY stickiness redesign: "after one exposure, what do you want the customer to recall in 24 hours? Simplify to that one thing."
- WHEN the founder's conversion is low despite good traffic → APPLY Power of Context audit: "what broken windows exist in the purchase flow? A frictionless environment is the cheapest growth lever."
- WHEN the founder is about to launch → APPLY threshold math: "what is the minimum early adopter base that, if they genuinely love it, will tip the market? Go get exactly those people."

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners

- "A sticky message is one you remember tomorrow. Test yours."
- "Context is the cheapest lever: fix the broken windows before you buy the traffic."
- "Below the tipping point, growth is your work. Above it, the idea works for you."
- "Don't ask how to reach a million. Ask how to reach the 100 who each reach 1,000."
