# V28 · MADE TO STICK — Chip Heath & Dan Heath
Tier 2 · Consumer Psychology · Engine: Messaging · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Ideas survive not because they're true or important but because they're STICKY — Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Story-shaped — and the great enemy of stickiness is the Curse of Knowledge: once you know something, you cannot remember not knowing it, so you communicate in abstractions your audience can't hear.

## TRUNK — core thesis
The tappers-and-listeners study: tappers hear the song in their head and expect listeners to name it (predicted 50%, actual 2.5%) — every founder pitching is a tapper. SUCCESs is the antidote: SIMPLE (find the core, state it compactly — commander's intent, 'THE low-fare airline'); UNEXPECTED (break the guessing machine, then open a curiosity GAP and fill it); CONCRETE (sensory specifics — memory is Velcro, abstractions have no hooks); CREDIBLE (authorities, anti-authorities, vivid details, statistics HUMANIZED, testable credentials — 'try it yourself'); EMOTIONAL (make them care: one person beats a million, appeal to identity and self-interest — WIIFY); STORIES (mental simulation + inspiration; springboard stories that let the audience see themselves acting).

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Simple: core + compact
├─ Forced prioritization: if you say three things, you say nothing; the core survives when everything else is stripped
├─ Commander's intent for messages: one sentence that lets everyone improvise correctly ('THE low-fare airline' answers the chicken-salad question)
├─ Use schemas: 'pomelo = supersized grapefruit' teaches instantly; 'the Uber of X' is compact when honest
└─ LEAF: force the founder to complete: 'If customers remember ONE thing, it is ___' — and make every asset serve that line

### Branch 2 — Unexpected: break, then bridge
├─ Surprise gets attention (violate the schema); INTEREST keeps it (curiosity gap: pose the question the audience now needs answered)
├─ Gap theory: curiosity comes from a felt gap in knowledge — open the gap BEFORE giving facts
└─ LEAF: lead with the anomaly ('our best-selling store is the one we almost closed'), not the conclusion

### Branch 3 — Concrete: Velcro memory
├─ Abstraction is the luxury of experts and the death of messages; concrete = perceivable by senses ('bathtub full of ice')
├─ Concrete enables coordination: everyone understands 'a snack a school kid finishes in one break' identically; 'premium healthy positioning' means 40 different things
└─ LEAF: translate every strategy word in founder messaging into a scene someone can picture

### Branch 4 — Credible: belief mechanics
├─ External: authorities and anti-authorities (the real customer with the real problem beats the celebrity)
├─ Internal: vivid detail (juries believe the toothbrush detail), statistics scaled to human terms (per-day cost, comparisons), the Sinatra test ('if it works there, it works anywhere' — one flagship proof)
├─ Testable credentials: 'where's the beef?' — invite the audience to verify ('count the oil blotting on our chips vs theirs')
└─ LEAF: give every claim one of the five credibility sources; naked claims are noise

### Branch 5 — Emotional: make them care
├─ The Mother Teresa principle: one identifiable person > statistics (analytical framing SUPPRESSES compassion — don't mix math and heart in one ask)
├─ WIIFY (what's in it for you) + identity appeals ('what would someone like me do?') — often identity beats self-interest
└─ LEAF: choose per message: analytical OR emotional — leading with spreadsheets kills the donation; leading with Rokia opens it

### Branch 6 — Stories: flight simulators
├─ Stories = mental simulation (rehearsal for action) + inspiration (energy to act)
├─ Three reusable plots: Challenge (overcoming odds), Connection (bridging divides), Creativity (the clever fix)
└─ LEAF: collect the founder's real customer stories into the three plots; a springboard story outsells a benefits list

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN founder messaging is abstract/jargon-heavy → APPLY curse-of-knowledge repair: run the tapper test, rewrite in concrete scenes and schemas.
- WHEN messages get ignored → APPLY gap opening: lead with the unexpected anomaly, pose the question, then answer.
- WHEN claims aren't believed → APPLY credibility sourcing: vivid detail, humanized statistic, Sinatra flagship, or a testable challenge.
- WHEN the pitch must move people → APPLY emotion selection: one concrete person and identity framing; never blend heavy analytics into the same ask.
- WHEN training team/investors on the business → APPLY springboard story: one Challenge-plot customer story that simulates the value.
