# V136 · PERENNIAL SELLER — Ryan Holiday
Tier 3 · Marketing · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Most marketing content is disposable — written for the moment, forgotten in days. Holiday's thesis: the best marketing creates assets that compound over years. A perennial seller is a book, article, talk, or body of work that continues to find an audience year after year without ongoing promotion. The principles: (1) create timeless work that doesn't expire, (2) build a platform, not a campaign, (3) word of mouth is the only marketing that scales forever, (4) positioning that makes people feel smart for recommending it.

## TRUNK
The perennial seller framework: (1) The Work — it must be good enough that people share it. Most content fails because it's generic, not because of poor distribution. (2) Positioning — does your work make people feel smart for recommending it? If sharing your content makes the sharer look good, they'll do it. (3) The Platform — a mailing list, a following, a reputation — reach that compounds. (4) Word of Mouth — the only sustainable growth mechanism. Create "triggering events" — a specific moment when people naturally think of your work and share it. (5) The Long Tail — the work that keeps selling. Holiday's own books (The Obstacle Is the Way) sell more copies now than at launch because the ideas are timeless.

## BRANCHES
- Timeless vs timely: timely content (news, trends) spikes and dies. Timeless content (principles, how-tos, frameworks) compounds. Invest 80% in timeless.
- Positioning for word of mouth: people share things that make them look smart, successful, or in-the-know. Design your content to make the sharer look good.
- The platform: an email list is the only asset you fully control. Social media followings are rented land. Build your own.
- Discovery vectors: search (people actively looking), social (people passively discovering), recommendation (people telling people).
- The launch is just the beginning: most marketing effort goes into launch week. Holiday argues launch is the STARTING LINE. The real work is sustained backlist promotion years after launch.

## FRUIT
- WHEN content marketing feels like a treadmill → APPLY timeless-vs-timely: what percentage of your content will still be relevant in 2 years?
- WHEN content isn't being shared → APPLY positioning-for-sharing: does sharing this make the sharer look smart?
- WHEN building audience → APPLY platform ownership: are you building your email list or renting someone else's audience?
- WHEN launching something → APPLY the perennial test: what's the plan for year 2 promotion, not just launch week?

## SEEDS
