# V25 · OGILVY ON ADVERTISING — David Ogilvy
Tier 1 · Brand Strategy · Engine: Messaging · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Advertising has one job — to SELL — and what sells is discovered by research and measured results, not by taste: "the consumer isn't a moron, she is your wife", so respect her intelligence, give her facts, and never let cleverness bury the offer.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Ogilvy fused direct-response discipline (where every ad's sales are counted) with brand building. His rules survive because they were tested against cash registers: the HEADLINE is 80% of the money (5x more people read it than the body); long, factual copy outsells short and vague when interest exists; every ad must flow from a BIG IDEA (one that makes you gasp, fits the brand, and could run for 30 years); every ad is a long-term investment in BRAND IMAGE — the personality that accumulates ad by ad; specifics beat superlatives ("at 60 mph the loudest noise is the electric clock"); and positioning decides more than execution: what the product is FOR and WHO it is for.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Sell, don't entertain
├─ "If it doesn't sell, it isn't creative" — awards and virality that don't move product are self-indulgence
├─ The temptation to entertain is the occupational disease of marketing; the discipline is relevance to purchase
└─ LEAF: for every founder campaign, define the ACTION it should produce and how it will be counted, before admiring the creative

### Branch 2 — The headline economy
├─ 5x more people read headlines than body copy: the headline must telegraph the promise, name the audience, include the brand where possible
├─ Headlines with specific benefit + news outperform cleverness; curiosity without relevance attracts the wrong readers
└─ LEAF: founders should write 20 headlines per piece and test two; the ad IS the headline for most viewers

### Branch 3 — The Big Idea and brand image
├─ Big Idea test: Did it make me gasp? Do I wish I'd thought of it? Is it unique? Does it fit the strategy? Could it run 30 years?
├─ Brand image: every communication deposits into (or withdraws from) one personality account; scattered tones bankrupt it
├─ The Hathaway eyepatch, the Rolls clock: one concrete device carrying the whole brand
└─ LEAF: hunt the founder's one repeatable device (visual, phrase, ritual) that can carry the brand for years

### Branch 4 — Facts, specifics, long copy
├─ The more facts you tell, the more you sell — when the reader is in-market, information is welcome
├─ Specifics are believed ("46,000 km tested on Indian roads"); superlatives are wallpaper ("best quality")
├─ Long copy works when it's INTERESTING copy: structure, subheads, captions (captions get read — put selling copy under every image)
└─ LEAF: replace every adjective in founder copy with a number, a name, or a demonstration

### Branch 5 — Research before creativity
├─ Ogilvy's edge was Gallup training: know what promise matters, what words the customer uses, what has already worked
├─ Test promise before executing; reuse proven ads until fatigue is proven (advertisers tire long before audiences)
└─ LEAF: mine reviews/support chats for the customer's own phrases — the best copy is assembled, not invented

### Branch 6 — Direct response discipline for brand builders
├─ Direct marketers KNOW what works because they count; general advertisers guess — borrow the counting
└─ LEAF: give every founder ad a response mechanism (code, link, offer) so learning compounds even in brand campaigns

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN reviewing founder marketing → APPLY the sell test: what action, counted how? Kill decoration that doesn't sell.
- WHEN copy underperforms → APPLY headline surgery: 80% of the leverage; benefit + specificity + audience in the first line.
- WHEN the brand feels scattered → APPLY Big Idea search: one gasp-worthy device, repeatable for years; audit all touchpoints for one personality.
- WHEN claims are vague → APPLY specificity conversion: numbers, names, demonstrations instead of adjectives.
- WHEN writing anything → APPLY customer-language mining: harvest phrases from reviews and conversations first.
