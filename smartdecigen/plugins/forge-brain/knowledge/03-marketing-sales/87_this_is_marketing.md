# V87 · THIS IS MARKETING — Seth Godin
Tier 1 · Marketing Philosophy · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Marketing is not about convincing people to want what you have — it is about finding people who already want what you have, creating trust and belonging for them, and making it easy for them to act on their existing desire; the industrial-age model of "interrupt and persuade" is dead, replaced by "seek and serve."

## TRUNK — core thesis
Godin reframes marketing from manipulation to service. The five steps: (1) Choose a SMALLEST VIABLE MARKET — the smallest group that can sustain your business, people whose problem you perfectly solve; (2) Understand their STATUS and their WORLDVIEW — marketing changes people's status; you must honor where they are and offer a path to where they want to be; (3) Build TRUST through consistency, generosity, and empathy — marketing is the generous act of helping people solve problems; (4) Tell a STORY that fits their narrative — a story about who they will become by engaging with you; (5) Make it EASY to buy and share — remove friction from the path. The core shift: from "how many people can I reach?" to "how deeply can I serve the people I choose?" For founders, this is the opposite of growth-hacking — it's the patient, generous building of a tribe around a shared identity and purpose.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Smallest viable market (SVM)
├─ The biggest marketing mistake: trying to reach everyone. Pick the smallest group that can sustain the business — if 1,000 true fans at ₹1,000/year = ₹1Cr, that's enough
├─ The SVM is the group whose problem you solve SO well that they will tell others (the sneezers)
├─ LEAF: define the founder's SVM: name the specific 100-1000 people who would be CRUSHED if your product disappeared; design everything for them
└─ LEAF: revenue math: SVM size × price × frequency = viability; optimize the numerator by understanding the denominator better, not making it bigger

### Branch 2 — Status, affiliation, and worldview
├─ People buy based on who they want to BECOME, not what they need; marketing is status-market matching
├─ Every product/service changes the buyer's status in their social world; understand the status change you're selling
├─ LEAF: for each product, answer: "what status does this give the buyer? (e.g., savvy mom, smart investor, local patriot, insider)"
└─ LEAF: founder worldview mapping: what does your ideal customer believe about the world? Market to that belief, not to their demographic

### Branch 3 — Trust is the only scalable asset
├─ Permission marketing (Godin's earlier concept): ask BEFORE you send; earn attention, don't buy it
├─ Trust is built through consistency (show up, deliver, be honest) and generosity (give more than you take, even before they buy)
├─ LEAF: audit the founder's customer touchpoints for trust-building: is each interaction generous or extractive? Does the first interaction give value or demand it?
└─ LEAF: the most expensive marketing mistake: spending money to acquire a customer you immediately treat badly

### Branch 4 — People don't want what you make; they want what it DOES for them
├─ "People don't want a quarter-inch drill bit; they want a quarter-inch hole" — benefit selling vs feature selling
├─ The deeper truth: they want the SINKING FEELING of a hanging picture being DONE; they don't want the hole, they want the relief
├─ LEAF: for every feature, ask "what does this FEEL like for the customer?" — the feeling, not the benefit, is the thing to market
└─ LEAF: the job-to-be-done (Christensen) frames this: what job is the customer HIRING your product to do, and what job are they firing?

### Branch 5 — The marketing maturity model
├─ Stage 1: Interruption (ads, cold calls, spam) — works but increasingly resisted
├─ Stage 2: Permission (email lists, subscriptions, communities) — earned attention, better
├─ Stage 3: Belonging (tribe, identity, shared mission) — the customer IS the marketer
└─ LEAF: assess the founder's marketing stage: if still at interruption, the plan has a short shelf-life; build the path to belonging

### Branch 6 — Generosity as strategy
├─ Godin's core: marketing is generous when you create something useful for free, teach instead of sell, help someone without expectation
├─ Generous marketing compounds — the recipient remembers, reciprocates, tells others
└─ LEAF: what free value can the founder create that perfectly serves their SVM? A guide, a tool, a community, a checklist — this IS the marketing

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN the founder's marketing plan targets "everyone" → APPLY SVM discipline: "who is the smallest group that can sustain this business? Serve them perfectly; everyone else is bonus."
- WHEN the founder describes features → APPLY status/feeling reframe: "what does this feel like for the customer? What status does it give them?"
- WHEN the founder's first touchpoint asks for money → APPLY generosity test: "what value do you give BEFORE asking for anything?"
- WHEN the founder is frustrated with ad performance → APPLY permission audit: "are you interrupting strangers or speaking to people who gave you permission?"
- WHEN the founder has a loyal customer base → APPLY belonging: "how do you make them feel like they're part of a movement, not just buyers?"
