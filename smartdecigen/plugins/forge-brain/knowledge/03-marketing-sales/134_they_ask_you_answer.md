# V134 · THEY ASK, YOU ANSWER — Marcus Sheridan
Tier 3 · Marketing · Tree Memory

## ROOT
Sheridan saved his swimming pool company during the 2008 financial crisis by answering every customer question honestly and publicly — including the ones competitors hide (pricing, problems, comparisons). The result: a content machine that generated $4M+ annually from organic search alone. The thesis: your customers are asking the same questions every day. If you answer them publicly and honestly, you become the trusted authority. Most companies hide pricing, compare only to themselves, and never address weaknesses — which is exactly why buyers don't trust them.

## TRUNK
The Big 5 topics every company must address in content: (1) Pricing and cost — exactly what it costs, what factors influence cost, why it's worth it. (2) Problems and weaknesses — what you're bad at, who shouldn't buy from you, what people complain about. (3) Comparisons — how you compare to competitors, honestly, naming them. (4) Best-in-class — who's the best for specific use cases (even if it's not you). (5) Reviews and opinions — customer reviews, your opinion on industry trends. Content becomes a 24/7 salesperson. The ROI: organic traffic compounds, sales calls become shorter (buyers self-educate), trust is built before the first conversation.

## BRANCHES
- Content types: written (blog posts, guides), video (explainers, walkthroughs), podcasts, webinars. Video is the most trust-building format — seeing a human face builds rapport faster than text.
- The cost article: the single most-visited page on Sheridan's site was "How much does a fiberglass pool cost?" — the question every customer asks and every competitor avoids answering.
- The problems article: "5 things people dislike about our product and how we're fixing them." Radical honesty about weaknesses builds more trust than pretending you're perfect.
- Hub-and-spoke model: one pillar page per major topic (the hub), with multiple supporting articles (spokes) linking back. This is the SEO architecture.
- Sales alignment: content doesn't replace sales — it educates buyers so the sales conversation starts at step 7, not step 1.

## FRUIT
- WHEN content isn't driving leads → APPLY Big 5 check: are you answering pricing, problems, comparisons, best-in-class, and reviews? If not, you're hiding what buyers want to know.
- WHEN the sales cycle is too long → APPLY self-education check: does your content answer the first 10 questions every prospect asks? If it did, calls would be 50% shorter.
- WHEN trust is low → APPLY the problems article: publish what people DON'T like about your product. The honesty paradox: admitting weaknesses increases overall credibility.
