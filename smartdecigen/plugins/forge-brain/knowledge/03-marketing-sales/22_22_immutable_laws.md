# V22 · THE 22 IMMUTABLE LAWS OF MARKETING — Al Ries & Jack Trout
Tier 1 · Brand Strategy · Engine: Brand Positioning · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Marketing is not a battle of products but a battle of PERCEPTIONS, governed by laws as unforgiving as physics — and the most violated law is Line Extension: success creates the ego and the pressure to stretch the brand until it stands for nothing.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Twenty-two laws, one spine: own a category or a word in the mind, first; focus wins; broadening loses. The laws founders break at their peril: Leadership (better to be first than better), Category (if not first, invent a category), Perception (there is no objective 'best product' — only perceptions), Focus (own one word), Sacrifice (you must give up product lines, target markets, or constant change), Duality (every mature market becomes a two-horse race), Opposite (the #2 must be the ALTERNATIVE, not a weaker copy), Candor (admitting a negative is the fastest way to be believed), Resources (a right idea without money stays in the mind of its owner).

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Laws of primacy (1-4)
├─ Leadership: the first brand becomes the generic (people say 'Xerox it'); imitators fight uphill forever
├─ Category: can't be first? Create a category — not 'better beer' but 'first light beer'; minds are curious about categories, defensive about brands
├─ Mind: being first in stores means nothing if you're second in memory
├─ Perception: marketing battles happen in minds; 'truth' is whatever perception the prospect holds
└─ LEAF: reframe every 'we're better' claim into a 'we're first/only' claim — or admit there's no position yet

### Branch 2 — Laws of focus (5-7, 13-15)
├─ Focus: the most powerful concept is owning ONE word (Volvo=safety, FedEx=overnight)
├─ Exclusivity: a word owned by a rival cannot be taken — don't spend money attacking an owned word
├─ Ladder: strategy depends on your rung — #1 plays defense, #2 attacks, #3 guerrillas a niche
├─ Sacrifice: give up product line, target market, or constant change; the generalist dies slowly
├─ Attributes: don't emulate the leader's attribute — own the OPPOSITE one (Crest=cavities → rival takes taste/whitening)
├─ Candor: 'Avis is only No.2' / 'the ugly car' — an admitted negative disarms the mind, then the positive lands hard
└─ LEAF: candor is the cheapest trust-builder a small brand has; scripted honesty beats polished puffery

### Branch 3 — Laws of competition (8-11)
├─ Duality: markets mature into two horses (Coke/Pepsi); plan to be one of the two or a profitable niche
├─ Opposite: #2 must position AGAINST: study the leader's essence and present its alternative (old → young, big → personal)
├─ Division: categories split over time (beer → light/craft/imported); each split births a new leadership chance
├─ Perspective: marketing effects play out long-term; short-term boosts (discounts) often cause long-term erosion
└─ LEAF: discounting trains customers to wait for discounts — the short-term/long-term inversion founders keep missing

### Branch 4 — Laws of ego and reality (12, 16-22)
├─ Line Extension: the most violated — today's success (one name, everything) is tomorrow's mush
├─ Singularity: only ONE bold move produces substantial results — not a portfolio of small efforts
├─ Success: ego from success → objectivity lost → line extension → decline; the CEO starts believing the brand causes the buying
├─ Failure: expect it, cut losses fast; personal-career safety makes managers ride dead positions
├─ Hype: real revolutions arrive without press conferences; heavy hype usually marks a faltering position
├─ Acceleration: build on TRENDS (slow, durable) not FADS (fast, fatal); feeding a fad kills it faster
├─ Resources: an idea without money to spread it stays an idea — budget for the mind-entry, not just the product
└─ LEAF: when a founder's category is 'hot', ask whether they're building on a trend or feeding a fad — and whether they'd survive the fad's death

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder plans a second product under the same brand → APPLY Line Extension law: what word does the brand own, does this dilute it, does a new name serve better?
- WHEN they're #2+ in a category → APPLY Opposite law: define the leader's essence, position as its alternative — never a cheaper copy.
- WHEN they claim 'better quality' → APPLY Perception law: better is invisible; first/only/opposite are visible — find their 'only'.
- WHEN spreading budget across many initiatives → APPLY Singularity: identify the one bold stroke, concentrate.
- WHEN riding a hot wave → APPLY Acceleration: trend or fad? Build durable, or take fast profit and exit — but choose knowingly.
