# V86 · PURPLE COW — Seth Godin
Tier 2 · Marketing Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
In a world where consumers are bombarded with 5,000 marketing messages a day, the ONLY safe strategy is to be remarkable — something worth noticing, talking about, and sharing — because safe and invisible is a faster path to irrelevance than being polarizing.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Godin's central metaphor: drive past a field of cows and you don't look twice; see a PURPLE cow and you tell everyone. Marketing has moved from the TV-industrial era (mass production, mass advertising, mass consumption) to the post-advertising era (splintered media, infinite choice, consumer indifference). The safe brown-cow approach — better features, faster service, lower price within the same category frame — is invisible because it's what everyone says. The only cure: do something genuinely remarkable that appeals to a small group of early adopters (sneezers) who will spread the story. Remakable means WORTHY of remark — not "better" or "good" but "worth talking about." The corollary: remarkable products are almost always polarizing — they offend some people and delight others. Safe products delight nobody and offend nobody — and are invisible.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The death of the TV-industrial complex
├─ Pre-1990: mass manufacturing → mass advertising (3 TV channels) → mass consumption — a linear funnel that rewarded the biggest spender
├─ Post-2000: infinite media (web, social, streaming) → consumer in control → attention is the scarce resource → only the remarkable gets attention
└─ LEAF: any marketing plan that starts with "we'll run ads" in a competitive space without a remarkable product is allocating budget to invisibility

### Branch 2 — The Purple Cow test
├─ If your product/service/offer is "just as good as X but cheaper/better/locally-made" — you have a brown cow
├─ A Purple Cow is: "I saw this thing, you HAVE to check it out" — organic, unprompted word of mouth
├─ LEAF: apply the Purple Cow test to every product/feature: "would a customer spontaneously tell a friend about this at dinner?" If the answer is not an immediate yes, it's invisible
└─ LEAF: "better" is invisible; "different" is visible; remarkable = different + worth remarking on

### Branch 3 — Sneezers, not everyone
├─ "Find the sneezers" — the small group of early adopters/enthusiasts who will spread the idea for its own sake
├─ Don't try to reach everyone; design the product FOR the sneezers, who will then reach the mainstream
└─ LEAF: instead of "what's our total addressable market?" ask "who are the 100 people who would LOVE this and tell 10 friends each?" — that's your launch segment

### Branch 4 — Polarization is a feature, not a bug
├─ Remarkable products offend some people; if nobody hates it, nobody loves it enough to talk about it
├─ Avis: "We're #2, we try harder" — polarized (some found it arrogant, others loved the honesty); that polarization drove conversation
└─ LEAF: a founder who wants everyone to like their product has designed for invisibility; ask "who will HATE this product?" — if the answer is "nobody," redesign

### Branch 5 — The innovation curve for products
├─ Products go through a lifecycle: early adopters (sneezers) → mainstream → late adopters → commodity
├─ The Purple Cow phase is the early adopter phase; the goal is to create something that delights them so much they MARKET it for you
└─ LEAF: map the founder's product against the adoption curve; if it's in the commodity phase, a marginal improvement won't save it — only a new Purple Cow will

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder describes their product's "better" features → APPLY Purple Cow test: "would a stranger tell a friend about this unprompted? If not, redesign the message or the product."
- WHEN a founder targets "everyone" → APPLY sneezer search: "which 100 people, if they loved it, would spread it organically? Design for them first."
- WHEN the founder is afraid of criticism → APPLY polarization reframe: "some people SHOULD hate this — that means the people who love it will LOVE it."
- WHEN a marketing plan relies on ad spend for distribution → APPLY TV-industrial complex diagnosis: "what happens if you can't outspend the competition? If the answer is 'nothing,' the product isn't remarkable."
- WHEN a founder says "we need to educate the market" → APPLY Godin's rejoinder: "education is the tax you pay for being a brown cow — make it so they GET it instantly."

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Brown cows are invisible. Purple cows get talked about."
- "If nobody hates it, nobody loves it."
- "Better is a threat. Different is an opportunity."
- "Don't target everyone; target the sneezers."
- "You can't out-advertise a remarkable product — and you can't advertise an unremarkable one into success."
