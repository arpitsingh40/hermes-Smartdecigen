# V156 · THE JOLT EFFECT — Matthew Dixon & Ted McKenna
Tier 3 · Sales · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The biggest competitor in B2B sales is not another company — it's "no decision." 40-60% of qualified B2B deals are lost not to a competitor, but to the status quo. Dixon & McKenna's research on 2.5M+ sales conversations reveals why: high-performing reps (top 20%) lose fewer deals to indecision, not because they're better closers, but because they address the customer's fear of failure (FOF). Customers don't buy because they fear making the wrong decision, and standard sales techniques (creating urgency, discounting) actually INCREASE indecision.

## TRUNK
The JOLT framework: (1) Judge the level of indecision — is the customer stuck because of the decision's complexity, the number of stakeholders, or the fear of being blamed? (2) Offer the right recommendation — don't ask "what do you want to do?"; say "here's what I would do if I were in your position." (3) Limit the exploration — too many options = decision paralysis. Present fewer options, not more. (4) Take risk off the table — offer a pilot, a phased rollout, a money-back guarantee. The key insight: validation-seeking behaviors (asking for more demos, more references, more ROI analysis) are signs of indecision, not interest. Standard reps comply; JOLT reps diagnose the fear and address it directly.

## FRUIT
- WHEN deals are lost to "no decision" → APPLY FOF diagnosis: is the customer afraid of making the wrong call? Name the fear, offer a pilot, de-risk.
- WHEN customers keep asking for more information → APPLY indecision check: validation-seeking behavior is not interest — it's indecision. Address the fear, not the request.
