# V124 · THE CHALLENGER SALE — Matthew Dixon & Brent Adamson
Tier 3 · Sales · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The old sales wisdom that "relationship builders" win is wrong — at least in complex B2B sales. Dixon & Adamson's CEB research on 6,000+ salespeople across 100+ companies found that sales reps fall into 5 profiles: Hard Workers, Relationship Builders, Lone Wolves, Problem Solvers, and Challengers. Challengers — who teach customers something new, tailor the message to their specific context, and take control of the sale — dramatically outperform all others, especially in complex sales. Relationship Builders are the LEAST effective.

## TRUNK
The Challenger model: (1) TEACH — bring a unique insight that reframes the customer's problem (not "here's our product," but "here's a problem you didn't know you had and here's how much it's costing you"). (2) TAILOR — adapt the insight to the specific customer's industry, role, and pain points. (3) TAKE CONTROL — confidently guide the buying process, challenge the customer's assumptions, and don't be afraid to push back on price or scope. The commercial teaching pitch has 6 components: Warmer (credibility), Reframe (show them what they're missing), Rational Drowning (quantify the pain), Emotional Impact (make them feel it), A New Way (your solution as the logical bridge), Your Solution (features only now, after they're convinced of the problem).

## BRANCHES
- Organizational model: Challengers dominate in complex sales because complex sales require consensus-building across a buying committee. Teaching unique insights gives the champion ammunition to sell internally.
- Marketing's role: arm Challenger reps with the insights. The commercial teaching pitch comes FROM marketing, delivered BY sales. Without marketing-generated insights, Challenger reps burn out trying to self-generate them.
- Tension management: Challengers must learn to create constructive tension — pushing back on the customer's thinking — without becoming obnoxious. Train this deliberately.
- Hiring: screen for challenger traits — ability to form a unique point of view, comfort with debate, resilience under pressure. Traditional "people person" screening misses challenger talent.

## FRUIT
- WHEN demos aren't converting → APPLY the pitch diagnosis: are you leading with product features or with a unique insight? Reps who pitch features lose to status quo.
- WHEN hiring salespeople → APPLY the challenger profile: screen for the ability to teach and debate, not relationship-building.
- WHEN competing against "no decision" → APPLY the commercial teaching framework: the customer hasn't been taught why doing nothing is more expensive than buying.
- WHEN marketing and sales are misaligned → APPLY the insight pipeline: is marketing generating TEACHING INSIGHTS or product collateral?

## SEEDS
- "The best salespeople teach, they don't pitch."
- "If the customer doesn't learn something new in the conversation, you're just another vendor."
- "Relationship builders are the least effective in complex B2B sales. Challenge, don't comfort."
- "Teach them why doing nothing costs more than buying."
- "Marketing's job is to arm reps with insights, not collateral."
