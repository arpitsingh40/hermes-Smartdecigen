# V34 · TRACTION — Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares
Tier 3 · Growth Marketing · Engine: Channel Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Most startups fail not because they built the wrong product but because they didn't find a channel that worked at scale — and most founders spread their marketing budget across many channels rather than doubling down on one until it's exhausted, which is the single biggest growth mistake in early-stage companies.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Nineteen traction channels exist (virality, paid ads, SEO, content marketing, email, sales, engineering as marketing, PR, trade shows, existing platforms, business development, affiliate, community, targeting blogs, offline ads, speaking engagements, direct sales, SEM, and unorthodox channels). The 'Bullseye Framework' is the systematic process to find the ONE channel that works: BRAINSTORM all 19 possibilities → RANK the top 3 by potential/lowest cost of test → RUN cheap experiments (not full campaigns) on the top 3 in parallel → IDENTIFY the winner → DOUBLE DOWN until marginal returns decay → REPEAT. Most startups waste 6+ months and 80%+ of their budget on the wrong channels. The secret is to test channels — not 'do marketing' — and to commit to one channel with the intensity of a person who has no other option.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The Bullseye Framework
├─ Brainstorm: list all 19 channels, cross-reference against product type, stage, and founder strengths
├─ Rank: pick the 3 most promising based on cost of test, expected reach, and fit with product
├─ Run cheap experiments: spend 1-3% of monthly budget per experiment — the goal is evidence, not revenue
├─ Identify winner: which channel shows unit economics that work (CAC < LTV, with room)?
├─ Double down: single-channel focus until marginal returns drop below the next untested channel
└─ LEAF: run three channel experiments per month, max; more spreads too thin — the Bullseye is a filter, not a buffet

### Branch 2 — The channels (selected critical ones)
├─ CONTENT MARKETING: blog/SEO that compounds — slowest to start, longest payoff; best for products with high search volume
├─ SALES: direct outbound — highest cost per lead, highest conversion; best for high-ticket B2B
├─ VIRALITY: product-embedded sharing (Dropbox referral, Hotmail signature) — near-zero CAC when it works, needs product-native sharing mechanic
├─ ENGINEERING AS MARKETING: tools, calculators, free utilities that do real work (HubSpot's Website Grader)
├─ TARGETING BLOGS: getting featured on a niche publication that the target segment reads daily
├─ COMMUNITY: building a Slack/Discord/forum where the target audience congregates — slow, high retention, low volume initially
└─ LEAF: the channel that works for your space is often the one everyone in your space ignores — if every SaaS does content, try events or direct sales

### Branch 3 — The biggest trap: channel hopping
├─ A/B testing across 5 channels means 5x the budget spent learning, 1/5th the focus per channel; partial commitment kills the test's validity
├─ The 'Zig-Zag' pattern: month 1 social media (no traction) → month 2 ads (some, high CAC) → month 3 content (too slow) → nothing compounds
└─ LEAF: commit to ONE channel for 90 days with at least 70% of the growth budget — if it fails, you learned it efficiently; if it works, you're months ahead of the hopper

### Branch 4 — Channel-product fit: which channel for which product
├─ High-price B2B → direct sales, PR, speaking
├─ Consumer apps → virality, existing platforms, paid ads
├─ Low-price SaaS → content marketing, SEM, email
├─ Marketplace → both sides: PR + community for supply, paid for demand
└─ LEAF: if the channel doesn't fit the price point (spending ₹50 CAC to acquire a ₹200 LTV), it's not the channel — it's the fit

### Branch 5 — The 'unfair advantage' test
├─ Before choosing a channel, ask: 'can our competitors copy this channel play within 30 days?'
├─ If yes, the channel is a commodity — temporary lead at best
├─ The best channel is the one only YOU can execute because of your skills, network, product, or position
└─ LEAF: double down on the channel that leverages a founder's unique asset (network, expertise, product quirk); commoditized channels belong to companies with the budget to own them

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder is 'doing a bit of everything' in marketing → APPLY Bullseye: stop the scatter, pick 3 channels to test this month with explicit pass/fail criteria
- WHEN a founder says 'we tried content marketing and it didn't work' → APPLY commitment failure: did they give it 90 days with 5+ pieces per week? Or one blog post?
- WHEN CAC is too high → APPLY channel refit: match the channel to the price/LTV ratio; high CAC for low LTV products should not exist
- WHEN a competitor is outsized on one channel → APPLY unfair advantage: which channel can the founder own that the competitor can't replicate?
- WHEN growth has plateaued → APPLY channel rotation: the dominant channel's marginal returns have decayed; time to run the next Bullseye iteration

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
