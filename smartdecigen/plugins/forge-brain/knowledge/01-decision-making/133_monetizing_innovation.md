# V133 · MONETIZING INNOVATION — Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke
Tier 3 · Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The #1 cause of new product failure is not bad engineering or poor marketing — it's pricing. Most companies build the product first, then figure out pricing right before launch. Ramanujam (Simon-Kucher partner, advised on 10,000+ pricing projects) argues pricing must be designed INTO the product from the start — what customers are willing to pay determines what to build and for whom. Products designed without pricing constraints almost always overbuild (include features nobody will pay for) or underbuild (miss the features that would command premium pricing).

## TRUNK
The 4-step pricing process: (1) Willingness-to-pay research: before building anything, test what features customers will pay for and at what price. Use conjoint analysis, Van Westendorp, or Gabor-Granger methods. (2) Feature pricing: the answer to "should we add this feature?" is always "will they pay more for it?" — if no, don't build it. (3) Packaging: group features into packages that match buying segments. Good-Better-Best packaging covers 90% of cases. The "good" tier defines the floor; the "best" tier captures willingness-to-pay from price-insensitive buyers. (4) Price realization: the gap between list price and actual transaction price (after discounts). Track realization rate (actual/list). Below 70%, pricing strategy is broken.

## BRANCHES
- The pricing disaster zone: 80%+ of new products either overprice (no adoption) or underprice (leave money, signal poor quality). The remaining 20% nail it.
- Value-based vs cost-plus: value-based pricing (charge what the customer perceives the value to be) beats cost-plus (charge cost + margin) in every market except commodities.
- Psychological pricing: charm pricing ($9.99), anchoring (show expensive option first), decoy (a deliberately bad option that makes the target look better), price ending effects.
- Subscription pricing: per-user, per-usage, tiered, hybrid. The best model aligns price to the customer's value metric (what they actually get more of as they use more).
- Price increases: annual 5-10% increases are expected in SaaS. Grandfather existing customers at old rates for 12 months, then migrate them.

## FRUIT
- WHEN setting pricing → APPLY value-based: what does the customer save/gain by using this product? Price at 10-20% of that value.
- WHEN adding a feature → APPLY the pricing test: will customers pay more for this? If no, don't build it.
- WHEN designing packages → APPLY Good-Better-Best: 3 tiers where the middle tier is the anchor (most people pick middle).
- WHEN price objections arise → APPLY realization check: is the list price right but discounts too deep?

## SEEDS
