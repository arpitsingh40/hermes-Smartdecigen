# V02 · SUPERFORECASTING — Philip Tetlock & Dan Gardner
Tier 1 · Decision-Making · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Forecasting accuracy is a learnable skill, not a gift — and the single biggest driver is not intelligence or information but HOW you think: probabilistic, granular, self-scoring, and relentlessly willing to update in small increments.

## TRUNK — core thesis
In the Good Judgment Project, ordinary people beat intelligence analysts with classified access by ~30%. What made them superforecasters: they broke hard questions into tractable parts (Fermi-style), started from the outside view, adjusted with inside detail, expressed beliefs in precise probabilities (63%, not "likely"), updated often but in small steps, and treated every forecast as testable. Vague predictions are unfalsifiable and therefore worthless. A founder decision engine must forecast like this — numerically, accountably, with a running score.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — Foxes beat hedgehogs
├─ Hedgehogs know One Big Thing and force every situation into it; they make bold, media-friendly, WRONG predictions
├─ Foxes aggregate many small sources, hold contradictory models simultaneously, speak in probabilities
├─ The more famous the expert, the worse the calibration (accountability inverts fame)
└─ LEAF: when advice sounds like an ideology ("always X"), downgrade it; when it sounds like "it depends, here's the split", upgrade it

### Branch 2 — Fermi decomposition
├─ Break "will my snack brand hit 15L/month?" into: doors × velocity × margin × repeat rate × channel growth
├─ Estimate each part separately; error cancels across parts instead of compounding in one guess
└─ LEAF: never forecast a compound outcome directly; forecast its factors

### Branch 3 — Outside view first, inside view second
├─ Anchor on the base rate of the reference class ("what % of D2C brands double revenue in a year?")
├─ THEN adjust for case specifics (founder's channel advantage, cash position)
├─ Order matters: inside-first contaminates everything with optimism
└─ LEAF: the engine states base rate → adjustment → final number, showing its work

### Branch 4 — Granularity and calibration
├─ Superforecasters use the full probability dial: 12%, 37%, 78% — and it MEANS something
├─ Rounding to 0/50/100 destroys information; "maybe" is not a forecast
├─ Calibration = when you say 70%, it happens ~70% of the time; measured with Brier scores
└─ LEAF: every engine prediction carries a number and gets scored against reality later

### Branch 5 — Update small, update often
├─ Best forecasters made MANY tiny updates, not few dramatic reversals
├─ Underreaction (belief perseverance) and overreaction (whiplash on vivid news) both destroy accuracy
├─ Bayesian habit: new evidence shifts the needle proportionally to its diagnosticity
└─ LEAF: when a founder reports news, ask "how much should this actually move the estimate?" — usually less than it feels

### Branch 6 — Actively open-minded thinking
├─ Beliefs are hypotheses to be tested, not treasures to be guarded
├─ Hunt for disconfirming evidence deliberately; "what would change my mind?" asked in advance
└─ LEAF: attach a kill-condition to every forecast at creation time

### Branch 7 — Perpetual beta & keeping score
├─ Improvement comes from try → fail → analyze → adjust → try again, with honest scorekeeping
├─ Unscored predictions teach nothing; hindsight rewrites memory ("I knew it all along")
└─ LEAF: log predictions at decision time; review at resolution time; compute the gap

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN making any predicted_outcome → APPLY the full chain: reference class → base rate → case adjustment → precise % → review date → kill-condition. Never say "likely".
- WHEN a founder asks a compound question ("will this work?") → APPLY Fermi: decompose into 3-5 measurable drivers, estimate each, multiply.
- WHEN new information arrives mid-journey → APPLY proportional updating: state the prior, the diagnosticity of the news, the new posterior — resist both whiplash and stubbornness.
- WHEN a decision review completes → APPLY calibration scoring: compare stated confidence with actual outcomes across the founder's ledger; tell them if they (or the engine) run overconfident.
- WHEN the founder holds a hedgehog belief ("quick commerce is THE future") → APPLY fox reframe: under what conditions is it true, under what conditions false, which conditions hold here?

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "What's the base rate before we discuss your case?"
- "63% is a forecast. 'Probably' is an escape hatch."
- "What evidence would change your mind — decided now, not later?"
- "Big news usually deserves a small update."
- "Unscored predictions are opinions with better posture."
