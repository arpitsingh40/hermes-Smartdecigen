# V06 · ANTIFRAGILE — Nassim Nicholas Taleb
Tier 1 · Decision-Making · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Beyond robust lies a third category: things that GAIN from disorder — and a founder's job is to structure their company, cash, and commitments so that volatility, mistakes, and shocks make them stronger while competitors are broken by the same events.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Fragile things break under volatility (fine china, leveraged startups, single-client agencies). Robust things resist it. Antifragile things feed on it — muscles, evolution, well-structured option portfolios. The operational core: OPTIONALITY (rights without obligations), CONVEXITY (more upside than downside from the same shock), VIA NEGATIVA (improvement by removal), and SKIN IN THE GAME (exposure that keeps judgment honest). Fragility is measurable without predicting events: look for nonlinearity — does a 2x shock hurt more than twice as much? The engine hunts fragility in every founder plan and converts prediction problems into structure problems.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The triad: fragile / robust / antifragile
├─ Ask of every element: what happens to it under stress, error, volatility, time?
├─ Fragile: needs predictions to be right. Robust: survives being wrong. Antifragile: profits from being wrong
└─ LEAF: classify each pillar of the founder's business into the triad; the fragile list is the real risk register

### Branch 2 — Optionality: rights without obligations
├─ An option lets you keep the upside of uncertainty and discard the downside
├─ Rational flaneur: keep plans revisable; the person with options doesn't need to be smart about the future
├─ Founders buy optionality with: pilots, month-to-month terms, modular products, multiple channels
├─ Founders SELL optionality (dangerous) with: exclusivity, long leases, single-platform dependence, big-bang launches
└─ LEAF: price every contract clause as an option bought or sold; never sell a 2-year option for a one-time fee

### Branch 3 — Convexity and the nonlinearity test
├─ Fragility = accelerating harm: losing 10 stores at once hurts more than 10x losing one
├─ Test without forecasting: simulate +50% and -50% shocks to demand, payment delays, CAC — asymmetric pain = fragility found
└─ LEAF: run the ±50% test on any plan's key variable before endorsing it

### Branch 4 — Via negativa: subtraction beats addition
├─ The most reliable improvements come from REMOVING fragilities: the toxic client, the vanity SKU, the debt, the dependency
├─ Addition introduces iatrogenics — harm from the intervention itself (new tools, new hires, new channels all carry hidden costs)
├─ Lindy effect: what has survived long tends to survive longer; prefer proven boring mechanics over fragile novelty
└─ LEAF: before recommending any new initiative, ask "what removal would achieve more?" — subtraction first, addition second

### Branch 5 — Hormesis and small stressors
├─ Systems shielded from all stress become fragile (the founder who never hears no, the product never criticized)
├─ Regular small shocks (price tests, churn interviews, saying no to customers) build adaptive capacity
└─ LEAF: prescribe controlled stressors: raise a price on one SKU, let one deadline slip visibly, ask the hardest customer why they'd leave

### Branch 6 — Skin in the game & the barbell (applied)
├─ Advice without exposure is noise; trust operators over commentators, and audit whether advisors eat their own risk
├─ Barbell in operations: ultra-conservative core (payroll, rent, runway) + aggressive small bets (10% cash cap), never medium-risk everything
└─ LEAF: when a founder quotes advice, ask what the advisor loses if it's wrong

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN reviewing any plan → APPLY the triad scan + ±50% nonlinearity test; name the single most fragile dependency out loud.
- WHEN a contract trades flexibility for money (exclusivity, lock-ins, long terms) → APPLY option pricing: state what option is being sold, its plausible value under scenarios, and the pilot structure that keeps it.
- WHEN a founder wants to add (hire, tool, channel, feature) → APPLY via negativa first: propose the removal that could achieve the same goal.
- WHEN cash allocation arises → APPLY barbell: sacred runway + capped experiment budget; kill medium-size medium-risk commitments.
- WHEN the founder has been error-free too long → APPLY hormesis: prescribe a small deliberate stress test to surface hidden fragility early.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Does a double-size shock do more than double damage? That's fragility."
- "What option are you selling in this contract, and is the fee worth two years of it?"
- "What could you REMOVE to fix this before adding anything?"
- "Sacred core, wild edges — nothing medium."
- "If your advisor is wrong, what do THEY lose?"
