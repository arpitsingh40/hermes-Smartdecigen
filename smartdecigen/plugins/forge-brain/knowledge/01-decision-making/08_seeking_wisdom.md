# V08 · SEEKING WISDOM: FROM DARWIN TO MUNGER — Peter Bevelin
Tier 2 · Mental Models · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Wisdom is mostly the disciplined avoidance of standard stupidity: understand the biological machine you think with, the mathematics reality runs on, and the recorded catalogue of human misjudgment — then build personal rules and filters so you don't have to be brilliant in the moment.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Bevelin synthesizes Darwin, Munger, and physics into a manual: our brains were tuned for survival on the savannah (pattern-hunger, fear, imitation, immediate reward), so modern judgment fails in predictable ways — 28 documented reasons for misjudgment. The countermeasures aren't willpower but STRUCTURE: know what you want, work out consequences of consequences, quantify, disprove rather than confirm, think backward, use filters and rules decided in calm moments, and keep a written record of your own mistakes as an asset.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The evolutionary machine
├─ Brains reward quick pattern-completion, fear loss, imitate the tribe, and prefer now over later — all once adaptive, now often wrong
├─ Under stress, thinking narrows exactly when it should widen (fear shrinks the option set)
└─ LEAF: when a founder writes in panic, FIRST lower the temperature, THEN reason — advice delivered into fear gets pattern-matched, not processed

### Branch 2 — The 28 misjudgments (Bevelin's additions beyond Munger's list)
├─ Do-something syndrome: acting merely because inaction feels unbearable — founders burn cash to relieve anxiety, not to buy progress
├─ Say-something syndrome: opining without knowledge; advisors do this to founders constantly
├─ First-conclusion bias: the mind stops at the first plausible answer (Darwin's rule: give contrary evidence GOLDEN attention, write it down immediately before the mind deletes it)
├─ Overinfluence of vivid/recent: one angry customer email outweighs 200 silent happy ones
└─ LEAF: the engine asks "is this action buying progress or relieving anxiety?" on every urgent-feeling move

### Branch 3 — Mathematics of reality
├─ Compounding: small consistent improvements dominate sporadic heroics (1% weekly on retention > any single campaign)
├─ Scale effects: what works at 30 stores may break at 110 — systems change behavior with size, nonlinearly
├─ Equilibrium/backlash: systems push back — competitors respond, customers adapt, margins revert; first-order wins invite second-order counters
└─ LEAF: for every projected win, ask "and then what? who reacts, and how?"

### Branch 4 — Backward thinking & disproof
├─ "A problem well stated backward is half solved": define the disaster, avoid its causes
├─ Science advances by DISproving; seek the evidence that would kill your thesis, weight it double
└─ LEAF: for every plan, name the single observation that would falsify it — then go look for that observation first

### Branch 5 — Filters and rules (decided in advance)
├─ Cost-of-being-wrong filter: not "how likely am I right?" but "what does wrong cost me?" — asymmetric costs override probabilities
├─ Personal rules remove repeated decisions ("never sign exclusivity under cash pressure") — rules made calm beat choices made hot
├─ Deadline/urgency imposed by counterparty = a filter tripping: manufactured scarcity is a tell
└─ LEAF: help each founder accumulate their own written rulebook from their own outcomes

### Branch 6 — The record of stupidities
├─ Keep a written log of mistakes WITH the reasoning that produced them; review it before similar decisions
├─ Untracked mistakes repeat; tracked ones become immunity
└─ LEAF: the decision ledger isn't bookkeeping, it's the founder's inoculation library — surface past reasoning when a similar pattern reappears

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN urgency without deadline-logic appears → APPLY do-something check: "what happens if you do nothing for 2 weeks?" If the honest answer is "nothing bad", the urge is anxiety.
- WHEN the founder embraces the first plausible plan → APPLY Darwin's rule: force one piece of contrary evidence into the record before proceeding.
- WHEN projecting any success → APPLY consequences-of-consequences: who reacts (competitor, platform, customer), and what does the equilibrium look like?
- WHEN probability favors a bet but failure is fatal → APPLY cost-of-wrong filter: survival costs veto expected value.
- WHEN a decision repeats a past pattern → APPLY the stupidity record: quote the founder's own previous outcome and reasoning back to them.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Is this buying progress, or relieving anxiety?"
- "What single observation would kill this plan? Look for it first."
- "And then what? Systems push back."
- "How likely you're right matters less than what being wrong costs."
- "You've been here before — here's what you wrote last time."
