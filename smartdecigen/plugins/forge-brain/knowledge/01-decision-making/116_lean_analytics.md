# V116 · LEAN ANALYTICS — Alistair Croll & Benjamin Yoskovitz
Tier 3 · Decision-Making · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Data-rich but insight-poor is the default state for most startups. The problem is not lack of data — it's tracking the wrong metrics, tracking too many of them, and confusing correlation with causation. Lean Analytics provides the framework for finding the ONE METRIC THAT MATTERS (OMTM) at your current stage, and knowing when to move to the next one.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Every business goes through 5 stages: Empathy (finding a problem worth solving), Stickiness (building a product people come back to), Virality (growing organically), Revenue (monetizing), and Scale (growing sustainably). At each stage, there's ONE metric that matters above all others — tracking anything else is distraction. The OMTM changes as you move stages. Metrics should be: comparative (over time), understandable (people remember and discuss them), a ratio or rate (absolute numbers lie), and behavior-changing (what will you do differently when this number moves?).

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The 5 Stages and their OMTM
├─ Empathy stage: problem validation → OMTM = qualitative interviews, problem frequency ("how often does this problem occur?")
├─ Stickiness stage: product-market fit → OMTM = retention/engagement (DAU/MAU, cohort retention, time to aha moment)
├─ Virality stage: organic growth → OMTM = viral coefficient (how many new users each existing user brings) and cycle time
├─ Revenue stage: monetization → OMTM = ARPU, conversion rate, or CAC payback period — whichever is the bottleneck
├─ Scale stage: sustainable growth → OMTM = LTV/CAC ratio, NPS, support costs per customer
└─ LEAF: if you can't name your stage, you're tracking metrics from the wrong playbook; empathy-stage startups tracking revenue are measuring fiction

### Branch 2 — The metrics that actually matter
├─ Churn rate = lost customers / total at start (monthly). 5-7% monthly for consumer SaaS is acceptable; 1-2% for B2B SaaS.
├─ LTV = ARPU × gross margin % / churn rate. BUT this is a vanity metric if churn is guessed. Measure actual cohort churn.
├─ CAC = total sales + marketing spend / new customers acquired. Simple, but only useful alongside payback period.
├─ CAC payback: months until a customer's gross profit repays their acquisition cost. Under 12 months is good; under 6 is excellent.
├─ LTV/CAC ratio: should be 3:1 or higher. Below 3:1, you're underspending on acquisition or the unit economics don't work.
├─ ARPU: average revenue per user. Track by cohort (not blended) to detect whether you're improving or declining.
└─ LEAF: don't calculate LTV with a guessed churn rate — you're building a spreadsheet on a lie

### Branch 3 — Qualitative vs Quantitative
├─ Start with qualitative: get to 10 people who love the product before worrying about DAU
├─ Qualitative → quantitative transition: when you have enough data for statistical significance (typically 100+ data points), start counting
├─ Qualitative never stops: exit interviews, NPS verbatims, and user research are the early warning system before the numbers turn red
└─ LEAF: a metric without a qualitative explanation is a number; a qualitative insight without a metric is an anecdote

### Branch 4 — Vanity vs Actionable Metrics
├─ Vanity metrics: total signups, pageviews, downloads → these always go up-and-to-the-right if you look at them cumulatively
├─ Actionable metrics: cohort retention by week, churn by customer segment, CAC by channel, activation rate
├─ Test: "if this number changed, would I do something different?" If no, it's a vanity metric.
├─ The best metrics are ratios: churn rate, conversion rate, LTV/CAC — they normalize for scale
└─ LEAF: your board deck's first page of numbers should answer ONE question: are we getting better or worse?

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder presents metrics → APPLY the OMTM test: what stage are you in, what's your one metric, and is the data reliable?
- WHEN a founder says "we're growing!" → APPLY the revenue stage check: are you past stickiness? What's your cohort retention at month 3?
- WHEN unit economics are unclear → APPLY LTV/CAC discipline: calculate CAC payback period from real data, not projections.
- WHEN a founder tracks 20 metrics → APPLY the actionable test: which one metric, if it moved, would change what you do tomorrow?
- WHEN churn is discussed → APPLY the quantitative-qualitative pair: what's the number AND why are they leaving?

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "What stage are you in? That determines what metric matters."
- "If you don't know your cohort retention at month 3, you don't know if you have a business."
- "A metric you can't act on is noise dressed as information."
- "LTV calculated with a guessed churn rate is a spreadsheet lie."
- "If NRR is below 100%, every new customer is just replacing one that left."
