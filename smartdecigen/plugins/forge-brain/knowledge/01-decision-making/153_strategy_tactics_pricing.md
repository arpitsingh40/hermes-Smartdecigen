# V153 · THE STRATEGY AND TACTICS OF PRICING — Thomas Nagle & Georg Muller
Tier 3 · Strategy · Tree Memory

## ROOT
The definitive pricing textbook — used in MBA programs worldwide. Nagle's framework: pricing strategy must be integrated with business strategy, not an afterthought. The core framework: (1) Value Creation — what is the product's differentiated value? (2) Price Structure — how do you charge (per unit, subscription, usage-based, bundled)? (3) Price Level — at what price point? (4) Price Communication — how do you present the price to maximize perceived value? The most important concept: Economic Value Estimation (EVE) — calculate the total economic value your product creates for the customer vs the next-best alternative. Price between the reference price (competitor) and the total economic value.

## TRUNK
Economic Value Estimation: (1) Identify the reference product (what the customer would use instead). (2) Identify differentiation value (the additional economic benefits your product provides — cost savings, revenue gains, risk reduction). (3) Calculate total economic value = reference price + differentiation value. Your price should be between the reference price and the total economic value — capturing some of the value you create while leaving enough surplus for the customer. Key insight: price segmentation — charge different prices to different segments based on willingness-to-pay, using versioning, bundling, or channel-based segmentation.

## FRUIT
- WHEN setting price → APPLY EVE: calculate total economic value = reference price + differentiation value. Price between them.
- WHEN serving multiple segments → APPLY price segmentation: versioning, bundling, or channel-based pricing.

## SEEDS
- "Price between the reference price and the total economic value you create."
- "The customer must capture some of the value. Your price leaves them surplus."
- "Versioning captures willingness-to-pay across segments without alienating anyone."
