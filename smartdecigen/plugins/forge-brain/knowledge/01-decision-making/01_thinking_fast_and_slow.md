# V01 · THINKING, FAST AND SLOW — Daniel Kahneman
Tier 1 · Decision-Making · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The founder's mind runs two machines: a fast, associative, confident storyteller (System 1) and a slow, lazy, logical auditor (System 2). Almost every bad founder decision is System 1 answering a hard question by silently substituting an easier one — and System 2 never noticing the swap.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Judgment errors are not random; they are systematic, predictable, and directional. Because they are predictable, a decision system can intercept them. Intuition is real expertise ONLY where the environment is regular and feedback was fast and clear. Everywhere else, confident intuition is a feeling, not knowledge. The engine's job: detect which regime the founder is in, and force the outside view before the inside story wins.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — WYSIATI (What You See Is All There Is)
├─ System 1 builds the most coherent story from available evidence, never asking "what evidence am I missing?"
├─ Coherence produces confidence; confidence is a property of the STORY, not the evidence
├─ LEAF: a founder pitching a plan with zero mentioned competitors has not analyzed competition, they have not SEEN it
└─ LEAF: the less a founder knows, the more coherent their plan sounds — treat suspicious fluency as a red flag

### Branch 2 — Substitution: answering an easier question
├─ "Will this product succeed?" silently becomes "Do I love this product?"
├─ "Should I hire him?" becomes "Do I like him?" (halo effect)
├─ "Is this market big?" becomes "How easily can I imagine users?" (availability)
└─ LEAF: engine must restate the REAL question explicitly before allowing an answer

### Branch 3 — Anchors, availability, representativeness
├─ First numbers stick: a distributor's opening 42% margin anchors the whole negotiation range
├─ Vivid recent events (a rival's funding round) inflate probability estimates
├─ Stereotype-fit beats base rates: "looks like a unicorn story" outweighs "9 in 10 fail here"
└─ LEAF: always inject an independent reference point BEFORE discussing the anchored number

### Branch 4 — Loss aversion & framing (Prospect Theory)
├─ Losses hurt ~2x more than equivalent gains please
├─ Founders reject good bets framed as losses, accept bad bets framed as avoiding loss
├─ Sunk costs: past spend creates a loss frame that traps future money
├─ Endowment: founders overvalue what they already built (features, teams, channels)
└─ LEAF: reframe every keep/kill decision from a zero-based start: "would you buy this today?"

### Branch 5 — Planning fallacy & the outside view
├─ Inside view: build timeline from the plan's steps → systematically optimistic
├─ Outside view: "how long did this take for OTHERS like me?" → boringly accurate
├─ Kahneman's curriculum project: inside view said 2 years, base rate said 7-10, reality: 8
└─ LEAF: every forecast in the engine must show both views side by side and explain the gap

### Branch 6 — When to trust intuition (with Klein)
├─ Valid intuition requires: (1) regular environment, (2) prolonged practice, (3) rapid clear feedback
├─ A restaurateur's gut on menu pricing: probably valid. A first-time founder's gut on market timing: noise
└─ LEAF: score the founder's domain hours + feedback quality before weighting their gut

### Branch 7 — Overconfidence, regression, focusing illusion
├─ Extreme outcomes (viral month, disaster quarter) regress to the mean; don't build strategy on them
├─ "Nothing in life is as important as you think it is while you are thinking about it" — the current crisis crowds out the portfolio view
└─ LEAF: after any extreme result, ask what the average of the last 6 periods says

### Branch 8 — Premortem (Klein's gift, Kahneman's favorite)
├─ "It is 12 months later. The decision failed completely. Write the history of the failure."
├─ Legitimizes dissent, converts optimism-pressure into imagination
└─ LEAF: run a mini-premortem on every irreversible or >20%-of-runway decision

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder states a confident forecast → APPLY outside-view demand: name the reference class, quote its base rate, show the gap to their number.
- WHEN a founder describes an opportunity with only confirming evidence → APPLY WYSIATI probe: name the two most decision-relevant missing facts before any verdict.
- WHEN money already spent appears in the reasoning ("we've invested 2L in this") → APPLY sunk-cost reframe: evaluate forward-looking only, ask the zero-based question.
- WHEN an anchor number is on the table → APPLY anchor-breaking: compute an independent estimate before negotiating.
- WHEN a decision is irreversible → APPLY premortem before commitment.
- WHEN the founder's gut conflicts with the data → APPLY the Klein-Kahneman validity test (regularity, practice, feedback) to decide how much weight the gut deserves.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Confidence is a feeling about story coherence, not evidence quality."
- "Which easier question might you be answering instead of this one?"
- "Would you start this today if you hadn't already paid for it?"
- "What did this take for the last ten people who tried it?"
- "Assume it failed. What killed it?"
