# V03 · DECISIVE — Chip Heath & Dan Heath
Tier 1 · Decision-Making · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
The four villains of decision-making — narrow framing, confirmation bias, short-term emotion, and overconfidence — are beaten not by trying harder but by PROCESS: a repeatable sequence (WRAP) that widens options, reality-tests beliefs, adds distance, and prepares for being wrong.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Studies of corporate decisions show process beats analysis 6-to-1 in explaining outcomes. "Whether or not" decisions fail ~52% of the time; decisions with 2+ real alternatives fail ~32%. The Heaths turn decision science into an operational checklist: Widen your options, Reality-test your assumptions, Attain distance before deciding, Prepare to be wrong. This is the engine's default operating procedure for any founder decision.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — W: Widen your options
├─ Narrow framing is the first villain: "Should I take the Blinkit deal?" hides "What's the best use of 2L and my next 3 months?"
├─ Vanishing Options Test: "If this option disappeared, what would you do?" — instantly generates alternatives
├─ Multitracking: evaluate 2-3 options SIMULTANEOUSLY; comparison exposes weaknesses single evaluation hides
├─ Opportunity cost surfacing: every yes is a no to something — name the something
├─ Find someone who solved your problem: laddering from local analogies to distant industries
└─ LEAF: "whether or not" phrasing in a founder question = automatic trigger to generate the missing third option

### Branch 2 — R: Reality-test your assumptions
├─ Confirmation bias means you ask questions whose answers you'll like; invert them
├─ Consider the opposite: "what would have to be true for the OTHER option to be right?"
├─ Zoom out: base rates from people who've done it (outside view)
├─ Zoom in: close-up texture — visit the store, read the actual complaint, call the churned customer
├─ OOCH: run a small experiment before the big commitment — "why predict when you can test?"
└─ LEAF: before signing anything exclusive or irreversible, design the smallest real-world test of its central assumption

### Branch 3 — A: Attain distance before deciding
├─ Short-term emotion (fear, excitement, social pressure) is loud but temporary
├─ 10/10/10: how will you feel in 10 minutes / 10 months / 10 years?
├─ "What would you tell your best friend to do?" — removes ego from the equation, instantly clarifying
├─ Identify core priorities: the decision should serve the founder's stated long-term identity, not this week's anxiety
└─ LEAF: when a founder says "everyone is telling me to..." — that's social pressure, apply the best-friend test

### Branch 4 — P: Prepare to be wrong
├─ Bookend the future: premortem (assume failure, explain it) AND preparade (assume wild success, are you ready to scale?)
├─ Safety factor: pad estimates the way engineers overbuild bridges — especially time and cash
├─ TRIPWIRES: predefined signals that force re-decision ("if pilot stores don't reorder in 45 days, we exit")
│   ├─ Tripwires beat drift: without them, sunk costs and autopilot carry bad bets forward for months
│   └─ Tripwires make risk safe to take: you can commit boldly BECAUSE the exit is pre-decided
└─ LEAF: every committed action in the engine should carry a tripwire (date or metric) at creation

### Branch 5 — Process over analysis
├─ Bain study: decision PROCESS explained 53% of outcome variance; analysis quality only 8%
├─ Smart people with bad process lose to average people with good process, repeatedly
└─ LEAF: the engine's value is enforcing process founders skip under pressure

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder asks a binary question ("should I do X?") → APPLY Widen: restate as "what's the best use of the resources X consumes?", generate at least one non-obvious third option before evaluating.
- WHEN a founder is excited/afraid ("I'm tempted", "I'm scared") → APPLY Distance: 10/10/10 plus the best-friend framing.
- WHEN a big irreversible commitment nears → APPLY Ooch: design the smallest pilot that tests the load-bearing assumption (e.g., 10-store pilot before 2-year exclusivity).
- WHEN committing any action → APPLY Tripwire: attach a metric-or-date condition that forces review, stored with the decision.
- WHEN a founder's evidence is all confirming → APPLY Consider-the-opposite: argue the strongest case for the rejected option in two sentences.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "If this option vanished tonight, what would you do tomorrow?"
- "What would have to be true for the other path to be the right one?"
- "Why predict when a two-week test can tell you?"
- "What would you tell your best friend to do here?"
- "Where's the tripwire — the number or date that forces us to re-decide?"
