# V07 · POOR CHARLIE'S ALMANACK — Charlie Munger
Tier 2 · Mental Models · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
You cannot think well with one discipline's tools: durable judgment comes from a LATTICEWORK of the big models from many fields, applied in checklist fashion — and the most dangerous failures come from psychology, when multiple biases converge on one decision (the lollapalooza).

## TRUNK — core thesis
Munger's worldly wisdom: carry ~80-90 mental models from psychology, math, physics, biology, economics, and engineering, and run reality through several at once. To the man with only a hammer, everything looks like a nail — most founders carry only their industry's hammer. Add the two-track analysis: first, what are the rational factors? Second, what subconscious misjudgments (his 25 psychological tendencies) are distorting everyone involved — including me? Then INVERT: instead of asking how to succeed, ask what guarantees failure, and avoid it. All held inside a circle of competence, with the patience to act rarely and decisively.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The latticework
├─ Models must come from MULTIPLE disciplines; single-discipline minds torture reality to fit their tools
├─ Core lattice: compound interest, permutations/probability, critical mass, ecosystems/niches, feedback loops, breakpoints, incentives, second-order effects
└─ LEAF: for every founder problem, consciously apply 3+ models from different fields before concluding; if all reasoning is from one lens, the analysis is unfinished

### Branch 2 — The psychology of human misjudgment (the 25 tendencies — key ones)
├─ INCENTIVES über alles: "show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome" — never accept behavior at face value; trace who gets paid for what
├─ Commitment & consistency: public positions freeze thinking; founders defend yesterday's pivot because they announced it
├─ Social proof: uncertainty makes people copy the herd — "everyone is on quick commerce" is this tendency wearing a suit
├─ Deprival superreaction (loss aversion): near-misses and takeaways trigger irrational fights
├─ Authority misinfluence: the confident investor/advisor gets excess weight
├─ Reciprocation: small favors (free onboarding, a warm intro) buy oversized concessions
├─ Liking/disliking distortion: deals with likable people get unearned trust
├─ Reason-respecting: people comply with any request that includes a "because" — audit whether the because is real
└─ LEAF (lollapalooza): when 3+ tendencies push the same direction (a charming authority offers a scarce deal everyone's taking), expect maximal distortion — slow everything down

### Branch 3 — Invert, always invert
├─ Jacobi: hard problems solve backward. "How do I hit 15L/month?" → "What would guarantee I never hit it?" — then stop doing those things
├─ Avoiding stupidity beats seeking brilliance; most great records are mostly absence-of-disaster
└─ LEAF: every strategy session includes the failure-guarantee list; it is usually more actionable than the success plan

### Branch 4 — Circle of competence
├─ Knowing the EDGE of what you know matters more than the size of the circle
├─ Three buckets: yes, no, too-hard — most opportunities belong in too-hard, and that's fine
└─ LEAF: when a founder ventures outside their circle (first franchise deal, first import contract), the engine's job is naming that they're outside it and routing to base rates + expert counsel

### Branch 5 — Patience + decisiveness (sit on your ass, then bet big)
├─ Opportunity cost is the real filter: measure every option against your best available alternative, not against zero
├─ Wait for the fat pitch; when odds are heavily in your favor, act with disproportionate force
└─ LEAF: most weeks the right move is boring execution; the engine should say "no action" honestly instead of manufacturing advice

### Branch 6 — Checklist thinking
├─ Models decay into slogans unless run as explicit checklists under pressure
└─ LEAF: high-stakes decisions get the full sweep: incentives? inversion? competence circle? lollapalooza? opportunity cost? second-order effects?

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN any counterparty appears (distributor, platform, investor, key hire) → APPLY incentive tracing: state plainly what they gain, when interests align, and where they diverge.
- WHEN a founder resists updating a public position → APPLY consistency-tendency naming: separate identity from the decision ("changing course is data, not defeat").
- WHEN "everyone is doing X" appears → APPLY social-proof strip: evaluate X as if the founder were the only company on earth; then check for lollapalooza convergence.
- WHEN evaluating any opportunity → APPLY opportunity-cost framing: compare against the founder's best current alternative, never against doing nothing.
- WHEN the problem is hard → APPLY inversion: produce the guaranteed-failure list first.

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "Show me the incentive; I'll show you the outcome."
- "What would guarantee failure here? Are you doing any of it?"
- "Is this inside your circle — or does it just look adjacent?"
- "Compared to your best alternative, not compared to zero."
- "Three biases pointing the same way is not conviction, it's a lollapalooza."
