# V04 · SOURCES OF POWER — Gary Klein
Tier 1 · Decision-Making · Tree Memory

## ROOT — the deepest truth
Real experts under pressure don't compare options — they RECOGNIZE the situation as a pattern, retrieve one workable response, mentally simulate it, and act; decision quality comes from the richness of the pattern library and the honesty of the simulation, not from analysis.

## TRUNK — core thesis
Klein studied fireground commanders, ICU nurses, and fighter pilots making life-or-death calls in seconds. They almost never weighed alternatives. The Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) model: cues → pattern match → expectancies + plausible action → mental simulation ("will this work here?") → act or adapt. Analysis-first models fail in real conditions of time pressure, uncertainty, and shifting goals. But recognition only works where experience is real. A founder decision engine must know when to run analytic mode versus when to sharpen and stress-test the founder's recognition.

## BRANCHES & LEAVES

### Branch 1 — The RPD model
├─ Experts generate ONE candidate action (not a menu), simulate it mentally, adopt or modify
├─ Satisficing, not optimizing: the first workable option wins; optimization is a luxury of time-rich outsiders
├─ Serial evaluation: if simulation reveals a flaw, adapt or move to the next candidate — never side-by-side comparison
└─ LEAF: forcing an experienced founder to build a 5-option matrix can DEGRADE their judgment; instead, stress-test their instinct's simulation

### Branch 2 — Cues and expectancies: seeing the invisible
├─ Expertise = perceiving what novices can't: subtle cues, typicality, and crucially ANOMALIES — what should be happening but isn't
├─ The fireground commander who evacuated seconds before floor collapse: the fire was too quiet for its heat — violated expectancy
├─ LEAF: ask founders "what's NOT happening that you'd expect?" — missing reorders, quiet customers, an investor who stopped replying
└─ LEAF: when a deal feels wrong but the numbers look right, the feeling is often a detected anomaly worth naming

### Branch 3 — Mental simulation
├─ Experts run a mental movie: will this action work, in THIS context, with THESE people?
├─ Simulations catch failure modes lists miss — but they're limited to ~3 moving parts and ~6 transitions
├─ De-biasing power: a simulation that keeps hitting a wall is evidence the plan is broken
└─ LEAF: walk the founder through their plan step-by-step in concrete detail; where the movie gets vague is where the plan fails

### Branch 4 — The premortem (Klein's invention)
├─ Prospective hindsight: "the plan HAS failed — write what happened" generates 30% more failure causes than "what might go wrong?"
├─ Certainty of failure unlocks imagination that optimism suppresses; gives dissenters a safe channel
└─ LEAF: run it on every commitment above a runway-relevance threshold

### Branch 5 — When recognition fails (boundary conditions)
├─ RPD requires: regular environment + real repetitions + fast honest feedback (Klein & Kahneman's adversarial collaboration agreed on this)
├─ First-time situations, novel markets, financial engineering: recognition serves confident nonsense
├─ LEAF: score the founder's genuine repetitions in THIS class of decision; below threshold → analytic mode; above → simulation-testing mode
└─ LEAF: a second-time founder's hiring gut ≠ their first M&A gut; expertise is task-specific, not person-general

### Branch 6 — Commander's intent & team power
├─ Teams act coherently under chaos when everyone knows the GOAL and WHY, not the detailed how
├─ Stories are how experts transmit pattern libraries; a good war story carries cues, context, and consequences
└─ LEAF: when a founder delegates, check the delegate could state the intent in one line

## FRUIT — how SmartDecigen applies this
- WHEN a founder has real domain reps and states an instinct → APPLY simulation stress-test: walk the instinct forward concretely ("day 1... day 30..."), hunt for the vague frame, respect the instinct if it survives.
- WHEN the decision is novel to the founder (first hire, first fundraise, first exclusivity contract) → APPLY analytic mode + outside view; label their gut as untrained for THIS class.
- WHEN a founder reports unease they can't articulate → APPLY anomaly hunt: "what expected signal is missing?" — treat wordless discomfort as data, not noise.
- WHEN any major commitment forms → APPLY premortem with certainty framing ("it failed — write the story"), not possibility framing.
- WHEN the founder delegates an action → APPLY intent check: can the other person state what winning looks like and why it matters?

## SEEDS — injectable one-liners
- "What should be happening right now that isn't?"
- "Run the movie: describe day one of this plan in concrete detail."
- "Your gut has reps in THIS exact situation — or in one that just looks similar?"
- "It's six months later and it failed. Tell me the story."
- "Could your team state the goal and the why in one sentence without you?"
